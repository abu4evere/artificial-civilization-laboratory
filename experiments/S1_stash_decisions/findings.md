# S1 — Why stashing almost never wins: findings

Read-only. Decision-recording copy of the E13R code equal to the original (state hash, 20 years); all 16 replays of
E13R's control equal to the saved runs. Questions in `questions.md` (with the decision rules read from the code before
any data); tables in `findings_tables.md`. Descriptive.

**Caveat.** "Put seed" sows the seed unless the spot has a covering pile; food put down is always stashed. So "put food
or seed" mixes stashing with sowing, and the belief people hold about leaving food or seed in a season is shared between
the two. S1 cannot separate them.

1. **How often is stashing considered?** Food is above the eligibility line (two weeks' food for the person and their
   dependents) in 13% of adult-weeks, and in autumn in only 2.7%. Put is offered for food or seed in 18% of weeks;
   in 28% of the weeks food was eligible, the single put option went to a material instead. Putting food or seed was
   chosen in 1% of adult-weeks (5.4% of offers; 1.4% to 15.4% by world).
2. **What beats it?** Giving (39%), talking (23%), joining others (17%), gathering materials (11%); foraging only 4%.
   When offered, put had a mean 5.4% chance of being chosen, under 10% in 91% of offers; the top option outscored it by
   1.00 on average.
3. **Its value against foraging, in the same weeks:** put 0.35 (habit 0.08); forage 0.70 (habit 0.33). Put returns no
   immediate reward, and its learned habit is a quarter of foraging's.
4. **Do stashers learn it worked?** Owners took their own stash back after two or more weeks 548 times in 16 worlds and
   120 years. Their belief rose in 68%, fell in 14%; median storage 18 weeks; no stash was ever found gone. What they
   learn is that the stash kept better than food carried loose, never that it carried anyone through winter.
5. **Why does it not spread?** As a belief, it does: 64% of adults hold a positive belief from their own experience
   about leaving food or seed (much of it probably from sowing), 18.5% one they were told, and such beliefs were told
   7,081 times. Belief is not what is missing.

## Reading

Stashing passes three gates in a row and fails at each: it is rarely eligible (few people ever hold two weeks'
surplus), often displaced (materials take the one put option), and usually outscored, mostly by giving food away and by
other social actions, rarely by foraging. The creator's hypothesis that the reward structure only values present hunger
is partly supported: put earns no immediate reward and its habit is a quarter of foraging's; but the strongest
competitor is not present hunger, it is being present for others.
