# N6 — How do real forager couples live together? (evidence review; questions written before any search)

**Chosen by the creator (October 2026), after C1.** No code, no experiment, no choice. Rules 12, 15, 16, 17.

## Why

C1 (confirmed on a holdout): ACL's couples are apart (beyond the 2 tiles conception needs) about half their weeks; two in
five apart weeks sit in a gap between two separately written thresholds (conception within 2 tiles; the pull back toward a
partner only beyond 3); partners forage independently, and food outbids the pull back. Before any change to nature, the
evidence must say what "living together" means for real foragers, so that any change is anchored, not tuned (rule 17).

## A constraint carried over from N5

ACL has no physical scale, and its step is a **week**: a person's weekly location stands for where they are that week.
Real couples may forage apart by day and sleep in the same camp at night. The evidence is therefore read for what holds
at the scale of a week: whether spouses share a camp, how often and for how long they are separated, and how far apart
they range in a day relative to how far a camp moves.

## Questions

1. **Co-residence.** How often do forager spouses live in the same camp or residential group? How often are they in
   different camps, and for how long?
2. **Daily foraging.** Do spouses forage together or apart by day? How does this vary (by society, season, task)?
3. **The camp as a unit.** Do people return to a shared camp each night (central-place foraging)? How often do camps move,
   and how far, compared with daily foraging ranges?
4. **Longer separations.** Hunting trips, visits to other camps, travel: how common, how long, and by whom?
5. **Consequences for births.** Is there evidence that spouses' separation lowers fertility in foragers or similar
   populations?
6. **Nature or culture?** Which of these patterns are plausibly biological (pair bonding, attachment) and which cultural
   (residence rules, division of labour)? Rule 1 forbids writing culture into ACL.
