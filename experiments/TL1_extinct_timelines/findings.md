## Detailed Findings

### 1. Extinction is overwhelmingly ageing out (Q1)
In extinct worlds, births stop almost half a century before the final person dies:
- **Median gap:** 49.0 years in discovery, 46.0 years in the holdout.
- **$\Delta T \ge 20$ years:** 96.3% in discovery, 98.8% in the holdout. Only 3 of 187 extinct worlds in discovery and
  0 of 80 in the holdout collapsed within 10 years of their last birth.
- **Verdict:** Extinction in ACL's baseline is almost never an acute demographic catastrophe (e.g. mass starvation wiping
  out a reproducing population); it is the slow attrition of non-reproducing elderly cohorts.

### 2. The mating pool was not empty when births ceased (Q2)
At the snapshot of the final birth:
- In 82.9% (discovery) and 87.5% (holdout) of extinct worlds, at least one single woman of childbearing age was alive.
- Among those worlds, single fertile women had eligible (living, unrelated) men in the world 67.6% of the time in
  discovery (median 100.0%) and 60.6% in the holdout (median 95.0%).
- In half or more of qualifying worlds (56.1% discovery, 50.0% holdout), **100% of single fertile women had an eligible
  man alive**. The mate pool was completely exhausted (`eligible_share == 0`) in only 24.5% (discovery) and 31.4% (holdout).
- **Verdict:** When births stop forever, eligible pairs usually still exist in the world. The bottleneck is the failure
  to encounter, court, and co-reside (the behavioral pipeline from PF1/PF2), not biological absence of mates.

### 3. A 10-year birth pause is not an absorbing point of no return (Q3)
- Worlds frequently experience prolonged multi-year pauses in births:
  - 5-year hiatus: experienced by 61.9% (discovery) and 57.4% (holdout). 65.0% and 71.4% record subsequent births;
    41.3% and 45.6% survive to year 120.
  - 10-year hiatus: experienced by 47.5% and 40.6%. 27.2% and 32.7% record subsequent births; **23.5% and 23.1%
    survive to year 120**.
  - 15-year hiatus: experienced by 43.8% and 37.1%. 8.9% and 15.8% record subsequent births; 19.2% and 15.8% survive
    to year 120.
- **The prior was wrong:** The assistant predicted that a 10-year hiatus would have $< 5\%$ survival (an empirical
  point of no return). In reality, nearly a quarter of worlds that endure a decade without a single birth recover to
  survive to year 120 (often as adolescent girls mature into childbearing age).

### 4. Causes of final deaths (Q4)
In the final 10 years of extinct worlds:
- **Old age is the plurality cause** in both sets (37.3% in discovery, 41.9% in holdout).
- Starvation is second (29.3% discovery, 27.1% holdout), followed by illness (21.2% discovery, 25.2% holdout) and
  thirst (10.7% discovery, 5.7% holdout).
- Violence, childbirth, neglect, and cold are practically zero in the final decade.
- While discovery fell narrowly short of the pre-registered 40% threshold (37.3%), the holdout crossed it (41.9%),
  confirming that natural geriatric attrition combined with standard baseline starvation/illness closes these worlds.

## What It Means for ACL

TL1 settles the demographic autopsy of extinct worlds:
1. Worlds do not die because everyone suddenly starves.
2. They do not die because every eligible man is dead when the last child is born.
3. They die because eligible pairs that still share the world fail to court and co-reside, leading to a decades-long
   reproductive freeze where the remaining population simply grows old and dies.
4. Furthermore, a pause in reproduction—even one lasting 10 years—does not seal a world's fate; recovery remains
   possible until the reproductive window completely closes.
