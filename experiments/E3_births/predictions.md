# E3 — Why births fell in E2 · Predictions (FROZEN)

**Question:** E2's population at year 100 was about 40% below E1's. Deaths kept the same mix of causes;
births fell by a third (0.87 against 1.31 per world-year). Why?

**Method (read-only):** re-run the same seeds under E1's code and under E2's code with a forensic recorder
attached. Nothing in the world changes. Each re-run must match its saved year-100 state exactly before its data is
used. Features and laws stay frozen; nothing is "fixed".

**Written:** 2026-10-02, before any forensic data was collected.

## The creator's predictions (as given)

- 60%: **B, island separation.** Isolated islands mean smaller mating pools, fewer partner encounters, fewer
  births.
- 25%: **A / C, time and attention.** People spend weeks checking claims, following reports, crossing by raft,
  exploring, reading marks; relationship-building loses out.
- 10%: **reproduction thresholds.** Some interaction with the conditions for conceiving (health, hunger, nursing,
  living close).
- 5%: something completely unexpected.

The deciding chart: birth rate against partner encounters, for E1 and E2. If encounters collapsed, that is
likely the cause. If they did not, the answer is deeper.

## Claude's predictions

- 40% B: couples and possible mates split by water that can no longer be walked across.
- 35% A / C: new pulls (water and game reports, far shores, puzzles from claims taken on) take people away from
  partners, so couples spend fewer weeks within reach of each other.
- 15% thresholds: more women below the health needed to conceive, or more starving weeks.
- 10% something else.

## What would answer each hypothesis

- **B:** a smaller pool of possible mates on one's own island; fewer weeks with a possible mate within reach;
  fewer pairs formed; partners stranded on different islands.
- **A / C:** fewer weeks with partners together (within 2 tiles); more weeks spent on travel actions; attention on
  puzzles, reports or shores instead of mates.
- **D (food timing):** more fertile women hungry or below health 50 even though harvests rose.
- **Thresholds:** the share of together-and-fertile weeks that are blocked (health, starving, nursing) rose.

## Interim (after the first forensic pass, before the feeding probe)

The first pass answered the question differently from all the hypotheses above: **births per fertile woman did
not fall** (0.146 vs 0.144 per year). E2's "births fell by a third" compared totals, and a smaller population has
fewer births. Per person, births held (0.0333 → 0.0342 per person-year) and **deaths rose 18%** (0.0262 →
0.0309). The rise is concentrated in **infants: 57.5 → 95.6 deaths per 1000 infant-years, almost all
starvation**, mostly infants cared for by a living mother.

### Predictions for the feeding probe (frozen before it runs)

Why do mothers in E2 have too little for their infants, when fertile women overall eat as well as in E1?

- 40%: **fewer gifts reach mothers.** Giving fell from 11.4% to 10.0% of choices, and gifts are how food
  reaches the needy.
- 30%: **mothers spend weeks on other things.** Checking reports, trying claims, puzzles, travel.
- 20%: **foraging yields less.** Water now blocks walking, so fewer places can be foraged.
- 10%: something else.
