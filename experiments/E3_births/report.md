## What the investigation found

1. **Births per fertile woman did not fall** (0.146 against 0.144 per year, pooled). E2's "births fell by a
   third" compared totals across smaller populations. Partner encounters and pair formation fell a little
   (encounters 28.5% → 25.6% of unpaired weeks; pairs formed −8%), but partners were together slightly more often
   and conceived slightly more per free week, which cancelled it out.
2. **Per person, deaths rose 18%** (26.2 → 30.9 per 1000 person-years); births per person held (33.3 → 34.2).
   The biggest rise is among infants: starvation deaths rose from 49 to 82 per 1000 infant-years.
3. **Week by week, infants were not hungrier.** They went hungry in 6.2% of infant-weeks, against 5.9% in E1.
   Their carers (97% their mothers) had the same food, the same shortfalls, foraged as much, received gifts as
   often, and did the same things. None of the predicted mechanisms (fewer gifts, time spent elsewhere, less
   foraging) shows up in the averages.
4. **The excess is concentrated in a few worlds.** Seed 107 alone had 20 of E2's 53 starved infants (16 mothers),
   and seed 106 had 11. Three worlds had fewer infant deaths in E2 than in E1.
5. **One world shows Hypothesis B exactly.** In seed 105 under E2, almost nobody had a possible mate on their own
   island (0.03 per unpaired adult-week, against 0.37 in E1). Partnered women fell from 44% to 21%, births from
   80 to 22 per century, and the world dwindled away.
6. **The overall gap may be partly chance.** Population at year 100: E1 46.6 ± 27.7, E2 28.1 ± 17.7 over 8
   worlds; t = 1.6. Seven of eight worlds ended smaller under E2, which is suggestive but not conclusive.

### Predictions, scored

- The creator's 60% on B as **the** main driver: **partly.** B decided one world (105) completely, but pooled
  across worlds the birth rate per woman did not fall.
- The creator's 25% and Claude's 35% on A/C (time and attention): **not supported.** Fertile adults' choices
  barely changed (forage 44.4% → 44.1%, talk 12.0% → 11.0%, court 0.2% → 0.3%), and carers of hungry infants
  behaved the same in both.
- Thresholds (10–15%): **not supported.** The share of together-and-fertile weeks blocked by health, hunger or
  nursing did not change.
- Feeding probe: fewer gifts (40%), time elsewhere (30%), less foraging (20%): **none supported** by the averages.
- "Something else" (5–10%): **closest.** The extra deaths are infants in a few worlds, not lost births, and the
  weekly averages do not show why.

### What would settle it

Whether the four changes cost lives on average, or E2 drew unlucky histories, cannot be told from 8 worlds. More
worlds under both codes (both are kept and verified) would separate an effect from chance, and would show whether
infant starvation and isolation recur or were particular to seeds 105–107.
