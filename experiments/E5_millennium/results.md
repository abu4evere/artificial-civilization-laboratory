## Checks (read-only, after the run)

**Validity.** No run crashed; all 8 worlds completed (5 reached year 1000, 3 died out). No code file changed after
the run began (newest code file 2026-10-02 18:21; first E5 snapshot 23:32). The kill criterion is not met: the
experiment is valid.

**Seed 301's two peoples are not a small-sample artefact.** Two islands of 15–53 people each, side by side for
990 years. In 1000 years not one person on either island formed a relationship with anyone on the other
(cross-island ties = 0 in every sample after year 10): they never met. Ancestry (`ancestry.py`): one island
descends entirely from one founding band of 5; the other from two bands that merged before year 100
(84% / 16%). The founders' trait gap was 0.115; it grew to about 0.16 by year 60 and stayed there
(0.163 at year 60, 0.172 at year 510, 0.159 at year 1000), while *which* traits differ changed (diligence gap
0.22 → 0.07, anxiety 0.05 → 0.24, care 0.09 → 0.23). Within-island spread stayed at about 0.10.

What differs in culture at year 1000:
- One island holds practice beliefs: leaving fibre, stone and wood at places in particular seasons (the building
  practice first seen in E1). The other holds none, in every sample for 1000 years.
- The other binds with hide and works bone; the first does not.
- Their marks share nothing: each island made its own shapes for rabbits, graves and water. Independently made
  marks will almost never coincide, so symbol distance 1.0 is expected for any two peoples who never meet.
- Techniques overlap by about half (distance 0.3–0.75).

**Rival identity was not tested, not refuted.** Prediction 7 scores "confirmed", but no two peoples ever had
contact (seed 301: zero ties), and every other world had a single people. Rival identity needs contact.

**"One mixed population or die" (the creator's prediction).** 7 of 8 worlds ended as one people or none. But
only one of those became a *mixed* people (seed 306: everyone descends from two founding bands, 86% / 14%).
In 302, 303, 304 and 308, everyone alive at year 100 descends from a single founding band of 5: the other three
bands left no descendants. The usual path is not merging but one band outliving the rest (as in E4).

**Knowledge peaks early, then thins.** Every world had made most of the techniques it would ever make by year 100 (e.g. 301:
441 techniques ever made by year 100, 510 by year 1000). Techniques known at once peaked around year 50–100
(95–156) and fell to 11–19 by year 1000. Most of that loss is junk from play being forgotten. But every kind of
tool any world ever had (carrier, boat, club, blade, spear, warm wrapping) first appeared within the first 11
years, and nothing new appeared in the 990 years after. Spears were lost in every long-lived world (between
years 141 and 487) and never came back; warm wrappings were lost in all of them (between years 16 and 600).
Carrier, boat and club lasted the whole thousand years everywhere; blades too, except in 302, which lost them
and made them again more than once.

**Populations held flat, at 30–90.** Births matched deaths for 900 years. In the long-lived worlds, deaths were
about 40% starvation, 35% illness, 20% old age. The three that died out: 305 (year 54) and 307 (year 53) from
starvation, thirst and neglect of children; 308 slowly, after 200 years at 5–25 people, mostly from illness.

**The 1000-year carrier is passed on, not just reinvented.** `provenance.py` replayed years 900–1000 of each
surviving world from its snapshot with a probe that only reads events. Every replay reproduced the original
population at year 1000 exactly (86, 68, 65, 36, 61). Each time someone came to hold the carrier
(`(fiber+fiber)#fiber`) in that century, it was counted by how they got it:

| seed | taught | invented it in play | taught share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 301 | 108 | 18 | 86% |
| 302 | 52 | 21 | 71% |
| 303 | 74 | 32 | 70% |
| 304 | 69 | 20 | 78% |
| 306 | 44 | 28 | 61% |
| all | 347 | 119 | 74% |

About three in four learned it from someone. The rest found it again themselves, since it is the easiest thing to
make. So the tradition is real transmission, helped along by easy rediscovery. The building practices (leaving
fibre or wood at places in autumn and winter) were told hundreds of times in each world in that century, and are held
by 20–53 people in the worlds that keep them.
