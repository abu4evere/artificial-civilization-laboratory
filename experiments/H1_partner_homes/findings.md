## Reading

1. **Partners' homes coincide only about half the time.** Each person's home is their own best-remembered place, and in
   half of couple-weeks the two homes lie further apart than conception allows. Aligning the thresholds (L1) would not
   bring together a couple whose homes are 4 or more tiles apart (about 30% of couple-weeks).
2. **About two in five apart weeks are "apart at home"**: both partners near their own homes, which are themselves apart.
   The rest are weeks when at least one partner is away from home.
3. **Homes are not stable places.** A person's home changes in about 18% of adult-weeks, on average every five or six
   weeks. A "home" in ACL is the best-feeling recent place, not a base that a couple keeps. (Coincidentally, Hadza camps
   are reported to move about every six weeks: record only, N6; this is noted, not used as an anchor.)
4. Read against N6: real couples share one central place; ACL's partners each have their own, which agree half the time
   and shift every few weeks. **L1 alone would leave much of the separation; L2 (a shared home base) is the change the
   evidence and this check point to**, but by the frozen rule the result is mixed and the choice is the creator's.

## For the creator (no choice made)

- **L1** (align the thresholds): smallest change; would affect the 3-tile gap (C1: two in five apart weeks) but not homes
  4+ tiles apart.
- **L2** (a shared home base): anchored by N6; would need its own design: what makes a home shared, how it moves, and
  how strongly it pulls, each fixed from evidence before any run (rule 17).
- **L1 then L2**, as two separate experiments, so that each change's effect is measured on its own.
