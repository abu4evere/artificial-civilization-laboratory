# N7 — Keeping couples together without a fixed home: candidate designs

**Asked by the creator (October 2026), after HF4 closed the HB1 question.** Candidates only: **no code, no experiment, no
choice.** The next step, for whichever candidates the creator picks, is an evidence review (rule 17), then a feasibility
check (rule 12), then a design for sign-off.

## What constrains the candidates (the record so far)

| study | what it established |
|---|---|
| C1 | couples are apart about half their weeks; each partner picks a foraging tile on their own; the pull back is outbid by food; 2 in 5 apart weeks sit in the 2/3-tile gap |
| N6 | real couples often forage apart by day but **share a camp at night**; camps **move** every few weeks; at ACL's weekly step a real couple would be together almost every week |
| H1 | partners' personal homes coincide only half the time and change every 5–6 weeks |
| T1 | a pull toward the partner at 2 tiles exists and is outbid by food; a slight change flips the fate of 19 of 64 worlds: **noise is large** |
| HB1 | a fixed shared home kept couples together and **raised extinction**; people were hungrier through a broad, modest fall in food held (HF1–HF4), about half explained |

**Lessons the design must respect:**
1. Do not tie each week's foraging to a fixed place (HB1).
2. A pull that competes with food loses (C1, T1); strengthening it until couples stay together would be tuning (rule 17).
3. Any change reshuffles every world's history: a **sham arm** and a **paired test** are required (T1, HB1).
4. Measure food held and starvation from the start (HF4's ledger), not only extinction.
5. **Nature, not culture:** no division of labour, no residence rule, no rule about who joins whom at marriage.

## How the candidates differ

- **K1 changes no behaviour, only what counts as living together.** It is the smallest change. Its case rests entirely on
  scale: ACL's weekly position stands for a week of foraging, and real couples a day's walk apart by day sleep in the
  same camp. Against it: it raises births directly, without anyone doing anything differently, which is exactly what
  T1 set aside as close to tuning. It needs a strong anchor before it can be considered.
- **K2 changes behaviour, minimally, and follows the evidence most closely:** couples come together each week, but where
  they come together moves with where they forage. It differs from HB1 in the one respect HB1's failure points to: no
  fixed place.
- **K3** has weak and contrary evidence; **K4** is tuning.

## Assistant's view (the creator decides)

Take **K2** and **K1** to evidence, as two separate possible experiments (as T1 and HB1 were):
- **K2** is the candidate the evidence already points to (central place that moves); the evidence review must read
  Kelly (2013) on residential mobility and look for how far apart spouses range in a day relative to camp moves.
- **K1** is worth examining because it changes the least; the evidence review must decide whether the week-scale
  argument is strong enough that counting couples within a day's trip as co-resident is anchored, not tuned. If it is
  not, K1 is dropped.

K3 and K4 are recorded and not taken further.
