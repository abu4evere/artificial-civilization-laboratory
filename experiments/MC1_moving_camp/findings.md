# MC1 — A camp that goes where the couple goes: findings

Frozen as `MC1-frozen` (signed off by the creator; candidate K2 of N7). Arms on F1's 64 seeds, 120 years, one code copy:
C control (settings off; re-run so it carries the hunger ledger, and reproduces F1's populations on every seed), M moving
camp (partners within reach end each week together on the better of their two tiles; no fixed home), S sham. Tables:
`results.md`. **Valid:** zero checks, feasibility pilot (couples apart 10.2%), control reproduces F1, code unchanged.

## The frozen reading

| | C | M | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| couples apart, per partnered non-pregnant woman-week (manipulation: ≤ half of C) | 57.8% | **17.0%** | 56.9% |
| worlds died out (of 64) | 22 | **26** | 24 |
| paired against C: saved / lost | – | 8 / 12 | 8 / 10 |
| exact two-sided sign test | – | **p = 0.503** | p = 0.815 |

**Frozen rule: no detectable effect on extinction at this sample.** The manipulation worked; the sham behaved as noise.

## What MC1 did (descriptive)

| | C | M | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| births per woman-year | 0.133 | **0.149** | 0.134 |
| ledger: median food held ÷ need at eating | 2.682 | **2.683** | 2.683 |
| ledger: starving share of adult-weeks | 0.038 | 0.040 | 0.039 |
| women 16–45 without a partner (share of woman-weeks) | 0.550 | 0.588 | 0.546 |
| child deaths per birth | 0.196 | 0.234 | 0.195 |
| starvation's share of deaths | 0.332 | 0.395 | 0.335 |
| deaths per person-year | 0.030 | 0.033 | 0.030 |
| median population, year 119 | 28 | 25 | 22 |

1. **Couples together without HB1's hunger.** Partners were apart in 17% of couple-weeks instead of 58%, and the food
   people held when they ate was unchanged (2.683 against 2.682); the starving share barely moved. A camp that moves with
   the couple did not do what the fixed home did (HB1: food held fell 6–7%).
2. **More births** (about 12% more per woman-year), as the mechanism predicts.
3. **No rescue.** Extinction did not change detectably. More of the extra children died (child deaths per birth 0.234
   against 0.196), starvation took a larger share of deaths, and women spent somewhat more weeks without a partner.

## Reading (caps)

- **Keeping couples together is not what saves fragile worlds in ACL.** F2 showed that worlds die mainly because people
  do not *find* partners (perfect finding cut extinction from 22 to 7). MC1 fixed the next link, couples staying
  together, cleanly and without harm to food, and births rose; but the extra births did not carry worlds through. The
  thread F1 → F2 → C1 → MC1 ends here: **pair stability was a real limit on births, and not the limit on survival.**
- Why the extra children did not tip survival (more of them died; more widowhood) is not measured here; recorded, not
  explained.
- MC1 is the first change in this series that is anchored, achieves its aim, and does not harm the world.
