## Described (both sets alike)

- **Where habits act.** The learned term outweighs instinct for giving (about 8 ×) and talking (about 2 ×), is about half
  of instinct for foraging and joining, and is near zero for hunting, taking, stealing, attacking, fleeing and courting.
  Habits steer the social life and foraging, not conflict or courtship.
- **By age.** Children's habits are their carer's (C about 80% under 12). From 16, the copied share grows with age (about
  45% → 63% at 45+); own experience stays near 35–44%. Nothing inherited at birth survives the carer's influence.
- **Persistence in a life.** Habits unused for a year or more keep their value (0.32–0.37) and still move choices when
  their situation returns (choice effect about 0.065).
- **Across generations.** A child's habits correlate with their parents' at birth 0.96 (age 12) to 0.91 (age 40), and
  with an **unrelated adult's** 0.92 to 0.87: parents are closer by only **0.04** at every age. Of an adult's habit term,
  **8%** traces to experience two or more generations up.
- **Reach.** Twelve of 32 situations recur 3–5 times a person-year; most of the rest almost never. The used habit value
  last changed a median of 3 weeks before.

## Reading

**ACL's brain does meaningfully steer behaviour, but what steers is mostly absorbed from others, not learned.** About one
adult choice in seven would go differently without habits. More than half of what those habits hold was copied in
conversation, whole tables at a time, so people end up with habits nearly as close to a stranger's as to their parents'.
Own learning is a real but smaller part (37%), and very little of anyone's experience reaches past a generation.

This puts a number on the mechanism the creator questioned on 13 October ("it sounds like a programmed machine"): **talk
copying (NL1's N7, listed as a known compromise) is the largest single source of what people habitually do**, and it
homogenises them.

## What it decides

The frozen "what it would decide" names three cases; the result is a fourth, between them: **habits steer, and own
experience is a mixed minority (37%) beside a copied majority (56–57%).** By the rules' own logic:

- It is not "barely steer", so a question of *why habits do not matter* does not arise.
- It is not "mostly learned", so a new learning mechanism (forgetting, need-gating, generalisation) is **not** next.
- It leans to "a channel of transmission", and the frozen consequence of that case applies in spirit: the next question is
  **how habits are transmitted**, and whether that channel is honest. That is the open decision on talk copying, which
  the council (14 October) proposes to test as today's rule against situation-specific copying and no copying in talk.

The creator decides. Nothing in nature changes.
