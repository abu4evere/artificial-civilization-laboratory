# BL0 — does ACL's brain meaningfully learn? Discovery reading (written before the holdout is run)

Frozen as `BL0-frozen` with Amendment 1 (`BL0-amend1`, part T); scripts and notes of 14 October appended. Tables:
`results_discovery.md`, from `analyse_bl0.py`. **Valid:** zero check PASS (logging copy = control world, with and without
the log); one fingerprint; I + C + E + T = habit and the origin split = habit for every person every week, largest error
4.9 × 10⁻¹⁵; nobody seen with habits the observer had not followed. Worlds 501–516, 601–616; 24 of 32 survived 120 years.

## By the decision rules (provisional; a label stands only if the holdout agrees)

- **Q1: habits steer.** Mean choice effect over adult decisions **0.140 [0.128, 0.159]** (rule: ≥ 0.10). About one adult
  decision in seven would go differently without the learned term; the top-scoring option changes in 14%. Not close to
  a line (the interval's lower end is 0.128).
- **Q2: mixed.** Own experience's share of the adult habit term, weighted by the choice effect: **0.372 [0.359, 0.386]**
  (rule: 25–50% = mixed). **The largest part is copied in talk (T): 0.569 [0.540, 0.603].** The caregiver part is 0.056;
  the inherited part is zero by adulthood.

## Beside it (described)

- **Where habits act:** the learned term outweighs instinct for **giving (8.2 ×)** and **talking (1.8 ×)**, is about half
  of instinct for foraging (0.62) and joining (0.50), and is near nothing for hunting, taking, stealing, attacking,
  fleeing and courting (≤ 0.06).
- **By age:** children's habits are mostly the carer's (C 80% under 12); from 16 on, copied-in-talk grows with age (48%,
  56%, 65% at 45+) and own experience stays near 35–42%.
- **Persistence:** habits unused for a year or more still hold value (0.37 on average) and still move choices when their
  situation returns (choice effect 0.068).
- **Descendants:** a child's habits correlate with their parents' at birth 0.97 (age 12), 0.96 (20), 0.92 (40), but with an
  **unrelated adult's 0.93, 0.92, 0.88**: parents are closer by only about **0.045** at every age. Everyone's habits are
  alike.
- **Lineage:** 8.8% of an adult's habit term traces to the experience of people two or more generations above them.
- **Reach:** of 32 situations, 12 recur 3–5 times per person-year; most others almost never. A used habit value was last
  changed a median of 3 weeks before.

## Discovery reading (provisional)

ACL's habits **do steer behaviour**, about one adult choice in seven, but what steers is **mostly not the person's own
learning**: more than half of the habit term is absorbed from others in talk, and people end up with habits nearly as
similar to a stranger's as to their parents'. By the frozen "what it would decide": *habits steer, but are not mostly
learned* (mixed, leaning to absorbed); the habit system acts largely as a channel of transmission, and the lineage
measure (8.8%) says how little of it reaches back more than a generation.

**Caveat written before the holdout.** Talk copying (T) is the mechanism already listed as a known compromise
(knowledge and habits pass mind to mind in talk, `docs/OPEN_DECISIONS.md`). BL0 measures its size; it does not judge it.
