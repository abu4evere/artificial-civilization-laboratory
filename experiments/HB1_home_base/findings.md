# HB1 — A shared home base: findings

Frozen as `HB1-frozen` (signed off by the creator). Arms on F1's 64 seeds, 120 years: C control (F1's replays), H shared
home base (partners within reach return each week to the place both remember best), S sham (one extra random draw in
week one). Tables: `results.md`; follow-up: `explore.md` (`explore_hb1.py`). **Valid:** zero checks, feasibility pilot
(couples apart 0.0% of 13,899 couple-weeks: structural, since partners start each week at home and travel at most
`reach`), code unchanged.

## The frozen reading

| | C | H | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| couples apart, per partnered non-pregnant woman-week (manipulation check: ≤ half of C) | 57.8% | **0.1%** | 56.9% |
| worlds died out (of 64) | 22 | **33** | 24 |
| paired against C: saved / lost | – | 7 / 18 | 8 / 10 |
| exact two-sided sign test | – | **p = 0.043** | p = 0.815 |

**Frozen rule: HB1 increases extinction.** The sham did not differ from the control (p = 0.815), as noise should not.

## Why (exploratory, read-only; `explore.md`)

1. **The mechanism worked.** Partnered women conceived more: births per partnered woman-year 0.382 (C 0.338) in years
   0–9, 0.383 (0.306) in years 10–39, 0.378 (0.281) after; the share of partnered, non-pregnant weeks eligible to conceive
   rose (e.g. 0.249 against 0.187 in years 10–39), and nursing rose with the extra births.
2. **Food near home ran short.** Starving share of person-weeks: 0.070 against 0.041 in years 0–9, 0.048 against 0.029 in
   years 10–39. In years 0–39, adult starvation deaths were 714 against 412, infant starvation 232 against 154, and teen
   deaths 166 against 60 (teens, 12 to 15, no longer follow a carer and feed themselves). Illness, the expected cost of
   crowding, fell as a share of deaths (35.7% → 32.4%): **crowding was not the driver; hunger was.**
3. **Widowhood followed.** With adults starving, women spent more weeks without a partner (0.647 against 0.564 in years
   10–39; 0.770 against 0.575 after year 40), pairing slowed (0.005 against 0.009 per person-year after year 40), and
   single women more often had no eligible man within sight (0.637 against 0.477 after year 40). Births per woman-year
   overall fell after year 40 (0.087 against 0.119), and populations shrank (median 10 against 23 at year 80).

**Reading (exploratory):** tying couples, and with them their children, to one place each week concentrated foraging
within reach of a fixed home. A home in ACL is the place that has *felt* best, which moves only slowly as memories update;
it does not move when the food around it runs out. Families grew, the land around their homes was eaten down, and
starvation, not crowding, undid the gain. A food-per-tile measure near homes was not recorded, so local depletion is
inferred from starvation and deaths, not measured directly.

**Correction (HF1, October 2026):** the reading above, that food near fixed homes ran short, was tested directly by HF1 (read-only, holdout on new seeds) and **not confirmed**: the land within 3 tiles of HB1's homes was about 3% less full than the control's (0.970 / 0.976). HB1's extra starvation is real and repeats, but its cause is open. The text above stands as written, with this correction beside it.

## Read against N6

N6's evidence had two parts, and HB1 implemented one. Foragers return to a central place each night, **and the place
itself moves when local returns fall**: Garg et al. (2021) model foragers who "either foraged close to their home within
a given radius or moved the location of their home to new areas"; Kelly (2013, record only) describes camp moves against
foraging radius; Hadza camps are reported to move every few weeks (record only). **HB1 gave ACL's couples a central place
without the residential mobility that real central places have.** The failure is informative: a shared home is not
enough; a home that does not follow the food is worse than none.
