# GPT's view on LS1's results and the children-and-tools review (26 October 2026)

**GPT's opinion, not sign-off.** Asked in the main chat in sleep mode, after the findings were final. Claude sent the
LS1 results and the draft `docs/evidence_review_children_tools_2026-10-26.md`. GPT's answer, condensed by Claude
(its wording kept where quoted):

## 1. The headline
Mostly right. GPT would phrase it: "Knowledge disappears with its holders because useful making and transmission fail
to reproduce themselves. The decisive behavioral collapse occurs at action selection, not at feasibility or initial
evaluation."
- Death's 0.98 is "an accounting result, not proof that mortality is the root causal problem". Death destroys
  knowledge because few others hold it.
- The −3.03 at the choice, with feasibility and weighing rising, "strongly suggests competition with other actions"
  (forage wins 0.68).
- Tools disappear through failed replacement. The replacement figure (0.59 to 0.07) may be the clearest evidence.
- The 1.57 is a decomposition contribution (other terms offset it), not a share of causal responsibility.
- The habit for making *rises* while practice collapses: "a positive disposition toward making is not sufficient to
  make the behavior competitive."

## 2. The low-skill trap
Plausible, but a hypothesis. Skilled agents might also rationally avoid making if foraging pays more.
- **First, a read-only choice audit:** compare low- and high-skill agents in matched states (hunger, materials, tools,
  food, opportunities). Record the full action scores, actual returns, and whether making would give a lasting benefit.
- **Then, if justified, an isolated 2×2 diagnostic:** making skill (existing vs set higher) × foraging return (existing
  vs set lower). If skill alone makes making competitive, that supports the trap; if skilled agents still reject it,
  opportunity cost dominates; if both matter, the interaction is the result.
- Setting skill in a diagnostic world is a controlled intervention, "never as an ordinary Nature rule". Don't change
  reward weights first: "that risks making the desired behavior win by definition."

## 3. The evidence
Broadly accurate, with one qualification: children do not always learn "mostly by watching". The evidence is a mixture
of observation, play, participation, access to objects, imitation and teaching, changing with age and society.
Sources GPT pointed to (to check):
- Lew-Levy et al. 2017: 58 publications reviewed. Early exposure, mixed-age playgroups, and growing involvement in
  complex skills in adolescence.
- **Hewlett & Roulette 2016:** Aka caregivers give tools, demonstrate, correct and arrange learning, even for infants.
- **Lew-Levy et al. 2020:** "opportunity scaffolding", e.g. Gidra children given well-made bows before they can make
  them.
- **Lew-Levy et al. 2022:** 434 observations from 54 forager societies of children using miniature and adult-like tools.
- Gurven, Kaplan & Gutierrez 2006: some hunting skills take 10–20 more years after physical maturity.
- Kaplan et al. 2000: the embodied-capital argument (a theory, not proof that every child is subsidised).

The strongest point: "Human children often acquire access to useful technology before they can manufacture it
competently. That is almost the reverse of ACL."

## 4. Order
"Study the making-choice collapse first. Do not merge fly-brain step 3."
1. Action selection and the low-skill hypothesis.
2. The tool lifecycle and access between generations: why are working tools almost never given? Are there no chances,
   or do the rules reject transfers?
3. Transmission before death.
4. Brain-inspired learning, only if the first three show a missing general mechanism.

Keep tool-giving as "a serious future Nature candidate, not an automatic fix". **Warning:** LS1 measured the world
after erosion. Compare matched early and late states before blaming individual learning alone.

GPT's closing: "The next breakthrough may be discovering how an artificial individual learns to invest effort in a
capability whose benefits arrive much later." Its vote: "freeze nothing tonight; prepare the making-choice diagnostic
and tool-lifecycle audit for the creator's review."
