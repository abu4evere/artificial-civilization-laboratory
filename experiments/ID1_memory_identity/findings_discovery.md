# ID1 — what makes an ACL person who they are? Discovery reading (written before the holdout is run)

Frozen as `ID1-frozen`; correction of 12 October; notes of 14 October (deep-copy snapshots); **Amendment 1** approved
14 October (`ID1-amend1`). Tables: `results_discovery.md`, from `analyse_id1.py`. **Valid** under Amendment 1: one
fingerprint; the original copy reproduces the uncopied world in all 31 worlds with focal people (seed 513 had none);
the sham and every swap differ, except two swaps of one person who died in week 3 (seed 511), exempt by the amendment.
57 focal people (median age 40, 24 partnered); 1 dropped from weeks 1–26 and 2 from weeks 27–52 (died).

**Disclosure (as recorded with the amendment):** these tables were displayed once before the amendment was approved.
The amendment changed no number in them.

## By the frozen tests (provisional; a reading stands only if the holdout agrees in direction and significance)

- **Q1, which parts move a person (drift beyond the sham, weeks 1–26):** only **everything but temperament** (median
  extra drift +0.085, Holm p 1.5 × 10⁻⁷). No single part does. Two single parts, **memories** and **know-how**, show
  significantly *less* drift than the sham (median −0.018 and −0.034; Holm p 0.034 and 0.013); by the frozen rule
  (positive difference required) they do not "move the person".
- **Q2, pulled toward the donor:** **beliefs** (14 people pulled where the sham was not, against 1; Holm p 0.007) and
  **everything but temperament** (25 against 0; Holm p 4.8 × 10⁻⁷). Temperament, habits, memories, relationships,
  know-how and places alone: no.
- **Q3, is temperament enough:** **"the person follows their parts, not their temperament."** Given another person's
  parts and keeping their own temperament, people became more like the donor.
- **Q4, does restoring bring them back:** for everything but temperament, **no detectable lasting change** after their
  own parts are restored at week 26 (Holm p 0.23). Not proof of a full return.

## Reading (provisional)

In ACL, no one part makes a person who they are; together, their acquired parts do, and temperament does not hold them
against those parts. Of single parts, only **beliefs** (what they believe about practices and seasons, what they were
told, whom they trust) pulls a person toward someone else. Restoring their own parts brings them back with no lasting
trace that 64 tests of this size can detect.

**Two things to read with care (written before the holdout):**
1. **The sham is a large disturbance.** One extra random draw changes everyone's future, so a person's behaviour drifts
   in the sham too. A swap of a part that rarely matters disturbs the world *less* than the sham, which is why memories
   and know-how show negative differences. The yardstick is "beyond what chaos alone does", and it is strict.
2. **Habits alone did not move people**, though BL0 finds habits steer one adult choice in seven. Swapping habits
   replaces a table that, by BL0, is mostly shared with everyone (a stranger's habits correlate 0.9 with one's own), so
   a donor's habits are not very different from one's own.

**The assistant's prior, against it (provisional):** "habits and temperament will matter most" — wrong for both alone;
"memories the least" — memories moved people less than chaos does, consistent; "restoring will mostly bring people back,
not completely" — no lasting change detected.
