3. **confirmed** — 1 of 113 planned again the next autumn (prediction: fewer than a third).

## What this says

- **Nothing in their nature made them planners.** Their temperaments and skill match everyone else's. What set them
  apart was a harder lesson, an expectation of going short 2.6 times as strong.
- **Their stores were shared and used up.** They gave away nearly a third, ate the rest, left almost nothing in heaps,
  and ended winter with about five food. No child in their care starved, though with about 0.4 deaths expected at the
  base rate, that is encouraging rather than conclusive.
- **The lesson undoes itself (interpretation).** Almost no planner planned again. Storing food means not going short;
  not going short lets the expectation fade (it is learned from short weeks, and moves toward zero in weeks without
  them); so the next autumn there is nothing to anticipate, and the shortage returns. A lesson learned only from
  suffering is erased by its own success. In human societies, what carries such a lesson past the years that no longer
  teach it is something that outlasts the experience: a story, a custom, a store that is kept, a rule. ACL's people
  have none of these for food.
