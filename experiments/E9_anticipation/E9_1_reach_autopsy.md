# E9.1 — Whom does anticipation reach, and does storing save them? · Predictions (FROZEN)

**Written:** 2026-10-05, after E9's results and before any E9.1 run, by the assistant while the creator was away.
Not to be edited after the results.
**Type:** read-only replays of the E9 A2-full worlds (A2 code, weight 1.0, seeds 301–304, 120 years), checked
against E9's saved populations. No code changes; no new weight.

**Why:** E9's manipulation check failed, yet the 112 adult-autumns that reached 80 food in hand were all people
who went short the winter before; they foraged in 73% of their autumn weeks and entered winter with a median 74
food against 12 for everyone else. The mechanism seems to act strongly on a few people and not at all on most. The
frozen decision table directs the next question to why going short is so rare an experience for adults.

## Measures (adults 16+, A2-full)

- each adult-autumn: their expectation of going short in winter (`short@3`) at the start of autumn; the share of
  their autumn weeks in which next season's expected shortage outweighed their present hunger (the mechanism acting);
  food at the first week of winter;
- whether each adult alive at the first week of winter is still alive at the first week of spring, and whether
  they starved that winter;
- the same survival figures for adults who went short the winter before but did not reach 80 food in autumn.

## Predictions

1. **It reaches few.** Fewer than 20% of adult-autumns have the mechanism acting in at least half of their autumn
   weeks.
2. **The lesson must be strong.** Among adults who went short the winter before, those who reached 80 food had a
   winter expectation at least twice that of those who did not (medians).
3. **Storing saves lives.** Adults who reached 80 food in autumn starve in the following winter less often than
   adults who went short the winter before but did not, by at least 5 percentage points.
