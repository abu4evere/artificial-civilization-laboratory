# E9.4 — Does the lesson undo itself? · Predictions (FROZEN)

**Written:** 2026-10-06, after E9.3, before any E9.4 run. Requested by the creator, to turn the interpretation in
E9.3 ("successful preparation removes the suffering that created the lesson") into a measurement or reject it.
**Type:** read-only replays of E9 A2-full (A2 code, weight 1.0, seeds 301–304, 120 years), checked against E9.

## What is guaranteed by the code, and so not tested here

The winter expectation moves a tenth of the way toward "short" or "not short" each winter week. A winter with no
short weeks therefore multiplies it by about 0.9^13 ≈ 0.25. That the expectation falls after a winter without hunger
is a rule, not a finding.

## What is empirical, and tested here

Whether preparing actually prevents going short, and whether the fall in expectation is what stops people preparing
again.

## Measures (every adult present through an autumn, the winter after, and the next autumn)

Winter expectation at the start of the autumn; held 80+ in that autumn (prepared); short weeks in the winter that
followed; winter expectation at the start of the next autumn; prepared again.

Groups: **prepared + no short weeks**; **prepared + some short weeks**; **did not prepare + short weeks** (among those
who went short the winter before); **prepared again** (two autumns in a row).

## Predictions

1. **Preparing prevents going short.** Planners have on average at most half as many short weeks in the winter they
   prepared for as adults who went short the winter before and did not prepare.
2. **The pattern the creator described.** Median ratio of next-autumn expectation to this-autumn expectation:
   prepared + no short weeks ≤ 0.35; did not prepare + short weeks ≥ 0.8; prepared + some short weeks in between.
3. **The fall explains the stop.** Among planners, those whose expectation at the next autumn is still at least 0.3
   prepare again more often than those whose expectation fell below 0.3.
