# E9.2 — Does a carer's store protect children through winter? · Predictions (FROZEN)

**Written:** 2026-10-06, after E9.1 and before any E9.2 run, by the assistant while the creator was away.
Not to be edited after the results.
**Type:** read-only replays of E9's Arm 0 (E7 code) and A2-full (A2 code, weight 1.0), seeds 301–304, 120 years,
checked against E9's saved populations.

**Why:** E8.3 found infants starving at seven times their share. E9.1 found that any one adult rarely starves in any
one winter, and that the few adults who stored food did not starve less. Children depend on their carers, so a store
may matter most for them. If it does, the value of laying food by falls on the next generation, which matters for how
such a practice could ever be learned.

## Measures

For every infant and child (under 12) alive at the first week of winter: their carer (or, failing that, mother),
the carer's food and seeds at that week, and whether the child starved before spring. Grouped by the carer's food:
under 20, 20–49, 50 or more.

## Predictions

1. **Stores protect children.** Pooled over both arms, children whose carer entered winter with 50 or more food starve
   that winter at less than half the rate of children whose carer entered with under 20.
2. **Such carers are rare without anticipation.** In Arm 0, fewer than 5% of child-winters have a carer with 50 or
   more food.
3. **Anticipation makes them more common.** A2-full has a higher share of child-winters with such a carer than Arm 0,
   pooled.
