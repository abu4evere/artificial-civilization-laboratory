## Frozen predictions, scored

1. **wrong** — the mechanism acts in at least half the autumn weeks of 40% of adult-autumns (prediction: under 20%).
2. **confirmed** — those who reached 80 had 3.2× the winter expectation of those who went short but did not.
3. **wrong** — they starved the following winter at 1.8% against 0.8% (prediction: at least 5 points less). With 2
   deaths among 112, this cannot be read in either direction.

## What this says

- **The problem is strength, not reach.** Nearly every adult learns to expect going short in winter, and the mechanism
  touches nearly every autumn. But the expectation is the share of winter weeks spent starving, which for most people
  is small, so what it adds to the pull to forage is small: about a tenth of a unit of hunger. Only people with a
  strong lesson (around half their winter weeks spent short) are moved to store, and they do store.
- **For an adult, a single winter rarely kills.** Across all adults, 0.6% of adult-winters end in starvation. Winter
  is the main season of starvation (E8.3), but for any one adult in any one winter the risk is small; much of the toll
  falls on infants and children, through their carers. A store may matter less for an adult's own survival than for
  their dependents', which this autopsy did not measure.
- **What this means for design (for the creator to decide, not done here).** The faithful copy of the thirst
  mechanism learns an average. People seem to remember the worst of a hard season, not its average; a lesson learned
  from how bad the worst week was, rather than how many weeks were bad, would be stronger without being any less
  grounded in experience. That would be a new mechanism with its own pre-registration, not a change to E9.
