# V1 — What do ACL's existing bad years do? Findings

Read-only replays of E13R's control (Arm A), seeds 501–516; probe read-only (state hash, 20 years); all 16 replays equal
to the saved runs. Questions in `questions.md`; tables in `findings_tables.md`. Run overnight while the creator slept, as
the baseline for H-L5. Descriptive; associations, not causes.

1. **ACL's years are far more predictable than a real grassland's.** Food per land tile varies from year to year with a
   coefficient of variation of about 0.07 in mid-autumn and 0.08 in mid-winter (median over worlds), against about 0.37
   for real grassland production (Shi et al. 2014, N4 evidence). The year's growing weather explains part of it
   (correlation 0.35 with winter food per tile).
2. **Bad winters do kill.** Each world's poorest quarter of winters by food per tile had 9.39 winter starvation deaths per
   1,000 people, against 1.37 in its richest quarter (about seven times), and more adults going short (8.1% of
   winter-weeks against 5.8%). Deaths are rare enough that all of a world's winter starvation falls within a quarter of
   its winters.
3. **Year to year, population barely moves.** The median population a year and three years after a bad winter equals the
   population before it, as after a mild one: small populations change by a person or two, and this measure is too
   coarse to show density absorbing single bad years.
4. **People respond a little, and not by storing.** In the autumn after a food-poor winter, against after a food-rich one:
   adults holding 80+ in autumn (planners) 0.9% against 0.2%; seed sown per person 3.5 against 1.4; but median household
   stock ÷ winter need the same (0.115 against 0.111), carers with food stashed **fewer** (3.4% against 10.3%), and the
   learned expectation of going short in winter barely higher (0.078 against 0.069). The pattern by deaths is the same.
5. **Poor years cluster.** Food per tile correlates 0.73 with the next year's, although the weather draws are
   independent: the land, and how densely it is used, carries each year into the next. So "the year after a bad winter"
   is often another poor year, and food per tile partly reflects population density.

## Reading

ACL already has bad winters, and they are deadly: about seven times the starvation of good ones. But they are mild in
the sense that matters for H-L5: the world's food varies from year to year about a fifth as much as a real grassland's.
After a bad winter people sow more and a few more hold large stores in autumn, but households do not hold more against
the next winter, and fewer stash. Within ACL's current, highly predictable years, bad winters do not teach storage.

This is the baseline the U design needs: storage does not appear at ACL's present level of uncertainty, and the land
itself makes poor years persistent (which the design must account for, since U3's "runs of bad years" are partly present
already).
