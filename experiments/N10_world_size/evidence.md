# N10 — Evidence: how big should their world be?

**Written:** October 2026. Questions and anchor criteria: `questions.md` (tag `N10-questions`, committed before any
search). **No code in ACL, no experiment, no choice.** "Full text read" = text extracted and the relevant passages or
tables read; "abstract read" = abstract only; "record only" = bibliographic record or a report in another source.

## Sources

- **Hamilton, M. J., Milne, B. T., Walker, R. S., Burger, O. and Brown, J. H. (2007).** The complex structure of
  hunter–gatherer social networks. *Proc. R. Soc. B* (PMID 17609186; [doi](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2007.0564);
  **full text read**, Table 1, via PubMed Central). 1,189 social groups in **339 forager societies** (Binford's
  ethnographic compilation). Geometric mean sizes: **family 4.5; dispersed group 15.6; aggregated group 53.7; periodic
  aggregation 165; whole population 839** (95% CL 736–954). Each level is about **3.8** times the one below (3.77,
  3.68–3.87), "within and across cultures and continents".
- **White, A. (2017).** A model-based analysis of the minimum size of demographically-viable hunter-gatherer
  populations. *JASSS* 20(4): 9 ([doi](https://doi.org/10.18564/jasss.3393); **abstract read**; summary carried from
  N5). A non-spatial model: populations of **about 150** are demographically viable; **as few as 75** have a good chance;
  the threshold ranges from 40 to 140 with marriage rules. Space (Wobst's spatial model) raises it.
- **Wobst, H. M. (1974)** (carried from N5; **record only**, as reported by White 2017 and Pearce 2014): minimum mating
  networks of 79–332 persons, 175–475 with spatial assumptions.
- **Tallavaara, M., Eronen, J. T. and Luoto, M. (2018).** Productivity, biodiversity, and pathogens influence the global
  hunter-gatherer population density. *PNAS* (PMC5819417; [doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1715638115); **full text
  read** for method and findings; the density values themselves are in figures and supplementary data, **not
  extracted**). Global ethnographic data: forager density is set mainly by the land's productivity (with biodiversity and
  pathogens); subtropical and temperate forests carry the most people.
- **Hamilton, M. J., Lobo, J., Rupley, E., Youn, H. and West, G. B. (2016).** The ecological and evolutionary energetics of hunter-gatherer residential mobility.
  *Evolutionary Anthropology* (PMC5094510; [doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/evan.21485); **full text read**, definitions).
  A territory is the sum of individual home ranges, each the area that meets a person's needs at the local resource
  supply: the land a population needs scales with its numbers and the land's productivity.
- **Blinkhorn, J., Timbrell, L., Grove, M. et al. (2026).** Life on the edge: Mesolithic population size and viability on Malta. *PNAS Nexus*
  ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgag234); **abstract read**). Land area times productivity-based density
  gives population; once cut off, Malta could not hold a viable forager population; only repeated voyages explain
  people there. An example of land too small for a viable population.
- **Pearce (2014), Hill et al. (2014)** carried from N5/N8/N9.

## Answers

1. **Population.** A viable forager population needs **at least ~75–150 people** (White 2017; Wobst higher with space);
   real forager populations average **~840** (Hamilton 2007). **ACL founds 20 people, and its worlds hold about 20–40.**
   That is below the minimum viable range, and about **one-thirtieth** of a typical forager population; the whole ACL
   world is the size of **one aggregated band** (~54).
2. **Density.** Set by the land's productivity (Tallavaara 2018); the values were not extracted here. **Not anchored**
   by the criterion (two quantitative sources read).
3. **Bands.** A dispersed band ~16, an aggregated band ~54, a seasonal gathering ~165 (Hamilton 2007). ACL's groups
   (a few to about 17 adults) are at the scale of **dispersed bands**; ACL has no higher levels because there are not
   enough people for them.
4. **Space per band.** Scales with numbers and the land's productivity (Hamilton et al. 2016); no figure that maps to tiles.
5. **Scale in ACL's terms.** **Not anchored.** No conversion from tiles to kilometres follows without a chosen number.
   As the criteria require, any study must be framed in **people** and **people per tile**, not kilometres.

## What the evidence says about the creator's idea

The idea "the world is too small for them to develop" is **supported, measured in people**: ACL's population is below
the minimum viable size and far below a real forager population, and its world holds only enough people for one
aggregated band, so there are no other bands to marry into or move between. That fits the record exactly: F1 (partners
not found), P2 (singles rarely search), G1 (groups hardly mix).

What the evidence does **not** support is more space **alone**: land without more people would spread the same 20
thinner (the record suggests that makes meeting harder), and ACL's density has no external anchor to scale it by.

**A form that needs no chosen density (for the creator; nothing chosen):** scale people and land together, keeping
ACL's own density (20 founders per 40 × 40 tiles). For example, 4× land and 4× founders (80 × 80, 80 founders: inside
White's viable range, about one-tenth of Hamilton's typical population), or 9× (120 × 120, 180 founders: above White's
range). Runs would be several times slower (people and tiles both grow).

- **(b)** Also test more space alone (same 20 founders), to show the prediction that it hurts.

## Note added after review (rule 19), October 2026

The creator pointed out a caveat that the text above understates: Hamilton et al. (2007) report the **typical sizes** of
forager groups and populations, and White (2017) a **minimum viable population** in a non-spatial model. They describe
different concepts, and **neither establishes the correct founder count or map size for ACL.** Phrases above such as
"inside White's viable range" describe where a number falls, not a target. Any founder count or map size used in S1 is
an operational choice, labelled as such.
