# D1 — Is delayed return adaptive in ACL's world? Findings

Read-only replays of E13R's control (Arm A), seeds 501–516; probe read-only (state hash, 20 years); all 16 replays
equal to the saved runs. Questions in `questions.md`; tables in `findings_tables.md`. Descriptive.

## Part A — the world

1. **Not dense.** The richest 10% of land holds about 24% of all plant food in every season (10% would be perfectly
   even): mildly patchy, with no rich concentrations worth defending or harvesting in bulk.
2. **Predictable.** A tile's mid-autumn food correlates 0.90 with the same tile's a year later (median; 0.66–1.00), and
   a world's total autumn food varies about 7% from year to year.
3. **Winter is barely lean on the land.** Plant food on land per person: autumn 582, winter 504 (13% less). U1 found a
   week of foraging yields 17.5 in winter against 19.8 in autumn.
4. **Storable, as seed.** Seed keeps (half gone after 138 weeks); loose food does not (17 weeks). 69% of what people
   hold at the start of winter is already seed (W1).

**Reading A.** ACL's world is predictable and its food can be stored as seed, but food is spread thin and **winter
leaves the land almost as full as autumn**. The lean season that makes storage necessary in the human literature
(Binford's collectors in strongly seasonal environments; Marean's dense, predictable resources) barely exists. In this
world, gathering each week through the year is a workable way to live: **the environment itself favours
immediate-return living.**

## Part B — sowing

5. **It is practised.** 115,396 units of seed sown across 16 worlds and 120 years, by a median of 40 different people
   per world; 66% of it in autumn, 22% in summer.
6. **It pays, later.** Seed sown grew 2.36 units of crop per unit (from the world's own rule). Summer sowing yields the
   most; autumn-sown seed waits a median 20 weeks for its first crop. 50,000 units of crop rotted in winter.
7. **Most of it goes to others.** Of crop harvested from recently sown tiles, the sower took 24%, the sower's close kin
   24%, and other people 52%. Anyone may harvest a sown tile.
8. **Sowers mostly learn that it did not pay, and sow again anyway.** 54% came back to the spot within a year. Their
   belief about sowing a year later had risen in 38% of cases and **fallen in 58%**, in every season alike (an
   exploratory split by season sown: falling in 54–60%). Yet 77% sowed again within 13–65 weeks (86% when their belief
   rose, 74% when it fell).
9. **It is small.** Crop grown from sowing came to 1.6% of all food eaten (against W1's ledger of the same worlds).

**Why do beliefs fall?** Not measured. The learning rule compares how much a spot stands out when a person returns with
how it stood out when they sowed; candidate reasons, unverified: others harvest the crop before the sower returns, the
crop is gone or not yet grown when they look, or winter dieback lowers the spot. A question for later.

**Reading B.** Sowing already is a delayed-return behaviour in ACL: an investment that returns more than twice its
seed, weeks or months later, practised by dozens of people per world and repeated year after year. But it has no
owner (half its crop feeds others), its learning signal more often says it failed, and it stays tiny.

## Answer to the creator's question

The creator set the rule: "If either answer is no, then changing agent psychology may be premature."

- **Part A: largely no.** Predictable and storable, but neither dense nor lean in winter; the world gives little reason
  to store.
- **Part B: yes, but.** A delayed-return behaviour exists and persists, without property and with a learning signal
  that mostly says it failed.

By the creator's own rule, **changing people's psychology is premature.** The world as built favours the
immediate-return life ACL's people lead.

## Correction (added after D2)

Finding 8 above read the fall in sowers' belief over a year ("fell in 58%") as learning that sowing did not pay. D2
showed that measure was confounded with fading (every belief fades about 8% a year) and with the balance of many small
negative looks against fewer, larger positive ones. Separating the sources, experience raised sowers' belief in 55% of
sowings and lowered it in 30%: on balance sowers learn that sowing works. The reading "sowers mostly learn that it did
not pay" is withdrawn. See `../D2_sowing_learning/findings.md`.
