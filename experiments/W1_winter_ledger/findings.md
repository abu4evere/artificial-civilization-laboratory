# W1 — The winter ledger: findings

Read-only. Instrumented copy of the E13R code (counting only) equal to the original after 20 years in both arms; all 32
replays equal to the saved E13R runs. Questions in `questions.md`, tables in `findings_tables.md`. Descriptive.

## What the ledger shows (control, Arm A; B alike unless stated)

1. **Winter is lived hand to mouth.** Food gathered during winter covers 102% of what is eaten in winter; food held at
   the start of winter (people and stashes) covers 14%. Over all winters people ate 96% of their need: winter is a small,
   steady shortfall, not a famine in the aggregate.
2. **There is almost nothing to keep.** The median household (carer, dependents, own stashes) enters winter with 21.5
   food against a winter need of 195 (11%); 96% hold under a quarter of their winter; none holds all of it. Rot is
   small (food rotting on people is 7% of what is eaten), and 69% of what people hold at the start of winter is already
   seed, which barely rots.
3. **Stashes are almost unused.** By the rules alone, a carer with children cannot carry a winter (97% of households
   need more than their carer can carry), so a family's winter could only be held in stashes. Across 16 worlds and 120
   years, 6,060 food went into stashes in autumn; 7% of carers had any food stashed at the start of winter.
4. **People gather about what they eat, all year.** Gathered ÷ eaten: spring 1.16, summer 1.09, autumn 1.08, winter
   1.02. The autumn surplus E8.4 measured on the land is not gathered.
5. **Going short is common, and stock only delays it.** About half of adults go short at some point every winter. Of
   those starting winter with 50+, 41–45% still do, around week 9–10 instead of week 4–5.
6. **Copying's difference is sharing.** In B, 40% more food was given away in winter (413,669 against 294,935), as the
   E13R autopsy found; gathering and eating are otherwise alike.

## Reading

The question was why food does not last through winter. The answer is that almost no food is held to last: people
forage to meet present hunger, a surplus never accumulates, and the one means by which a family could hold a winter,
the stash, is almost never used. Rot, theft and the cold are not the bottleneck.

This connects to what E9 found about learning: the forage reward in the code scales with present hunger, so a well-fed
person is rewarded little for gathering more (a reading of the code, not a W1 measurement).

**Next question it raises (the creator decides):** why do people not stash? The mechanism exists: stashes under cover
rot slowly, seeds barely at all, and people learn from what their stashes kept.
