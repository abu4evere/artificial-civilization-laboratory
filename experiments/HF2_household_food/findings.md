# HF2 — Where does HB1's hunger come from? The household food balance: findings

Questions and decision rule frozen as `HF2-questions` (signed off by the creator). Read-only, first 40 years, arms H (HB1)
and C (control code). Discovery 501–516 (all replays reproduce HB1's and F1's saved runs); holdout on new seeds
1401–1416. Observer read-only in both arms; code unchanged. Tables: `results_both.md`.

| → **meat** | no | yes | both sets |

**Neither lead is supported in both sets.** HB1's extra hunger falls on every kind of adult (carers about half; partnered
adults without dependants 15–21%; unpartnered adults about 30%). Meat is a negligible share of food in ACL (about 0.02%),
so whatever hunting does, it does not feed or starve anyone measurably. HB1's couples did have somewhat more dependants
(8–12% more per carer), but not enough, and not concentrated enough, to account for the hunger.

## The gifts lead (written down before the holdout; repeated in it)

| | discovery | holdout |
|---|---|---|
| food received as gifts per adult-week, H ÷ C | 0.591 | 0.586 |
| food given away per adult-week, H ÷ C | 0.627 | 0.625 |
| drop in total food in per adult-week (C − H) | 0.404 | 0.383 |
| of which, gifts | 0.310 | 0.310 |
| of which, foraging | 0.101 | 0.078 |
| share of gifts going to people who are neither partner nor kin, H / C | 0.18 / 0.22 | 0.17 / 0.26 |

Under HB1, food passing between people fell by about two fifths, and that fall is about three quarters to four fifths of
the drop in what adults took in; foraging fell only about 1%. The lead was recorded in `findings_discovery.md` before
the holdout was run, and the holdout repeated it closely. It had no threshold set in advance, so under rule 15 it is
**consistent, not formally confirmed**.

## Reading (caps)

- HB1's hunger is not a household problem of too many children, and not a problem of meat.
- The strongest lead, now seen twice: **under HB1, people gave each other less food**, and the gifts that still flowed
  went more to kin. ACL's earlier studies (G1, P1) found that food passing between people is what protects the hungry.
  A shared home that ends every couple's week at home may cut couples off from the people they would otherwise feed and be
  fed by. Why gifts fell (fewer meetings? givers with less to spare? recipients out of reach?) is not yet measured.
- The assistant's prior (mouths supported) was wrong; recorded as such. It is the second wrong prior in a row (HF1).
