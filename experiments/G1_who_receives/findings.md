# G1 — Who receives the food: findings

Read-only replays of E13R's control (Arm A), seeds 501–516; probe read-only (state hash, 20 years); all 16 replays equal
to the saved runs. Every gift (the world's own `give` events) classified at the moment it happened. Questions in
`questions.md`; tables in `findings_tables.md`. Descriptive.

**A fix during the run.** The first run crashed in 15 of 16 worlds: when a recipient died later in the same week, the
probe could not find them among the living to rank their standing. The probe was fixed to compute standing directly
(committed with its reason) and all 16 worlds were rerun. Nothing about the world or the questions changed.

1. **To whom? Family, above all siblings.** 72% of all food given went to family: siblings 33% (at least a fifth of all
   giving in 16 of 16 worlds), other kin 18%, parents 7.5%, the giver's own children 11% (young children in another's
   care, usually the other parent's, 6%; teens 2%; grown children 3%), partners 3.5%. Friends received 9%,
   acquaintances 17%, strangers 1.4%.
2. **To which ages?** Adults 64%; children in someone else's care 20% (in 9 of 16 worlds at least a fifth); teens 8%;
   elders 7%; infants 1%. (Tie and age were not cross-tabulated; part of the sibling share is likely older siblings
   feeding younger ones.)
3. **To the hungry.** 63% of food went to people holding under half a week of their own need, 70% in winter; 2% to
   people holding four weeks or more.
4. **Slightly to the respected.** Adults in the top quarter of standing received 30%, the bottom quarter 20%.
5. **Reciprocal, and spread.** 53% of food given was paying back an earlier gift. The top 10% of recipients received a
   median 28% of a world's gifts (23–57%); a giver fed a median of 4 different people a year, the main one getting 39%.

## Reading

Food given away is not lost to strangers or gathered by the powerful. It moves inside families, mostly between
siblings, to whoever is hungry right now, and half of it is repayment. It behaves like a family's shared store held in
other people's stomachs: a surplus is turned into someone's meal this week and a debt owed back later, rather than into
food kept for winter.

That is why storage and sharing compete in ACL (S1): the same surplus can become a stash or a gift, and the gift pays
the giver at once and is often repaid. Whether this sharing helps or harms winter survival overall is not answered here.
