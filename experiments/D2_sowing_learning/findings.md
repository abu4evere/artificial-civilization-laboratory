# D2 — Why do sowers learn failure? Findings

Read-only. Logging-only copy of the E13R code equal to the original (state hash, 20 years); all 16 replays of E13R's
control equal to the saved runs. Questions in `questions.md` (with two findings made before any data); tables in
`findings_tables.md`. Descriptive.

## The answer in one line

**Sowers do not learn failure; they learn that sowing works. But they do not get the return: the sower's family gets
back about what it put in, and most of the crop feeds others.**

## Learning

1. **On balance, experience teaches that sowing pays.** Over the year after a sowing, the sower's belief about sowing
   was moved by: looks at the spot they sowed (+614 summed over all sowings), looks at other spots they sowed or saw sown
   (+2,036), other surprises (+208), and fading (−787). Setting fading aside, experience raised the sower's belief in
   55% of sowings and lowered it in 30% (exploratory), and the rises were larger (median +0.36 against −0.23).
2. **The lessons are mostly right.** At looks at one's own sown spot, the credit was positive 55%, negative 32%. When
   crop had grown there and nobody else had harvested it: positive 73%, negative 17%. When others had harvested some:
   54% and 33%. When nothing had grown yet: 42% and 44%.
3. **Negative lessons come from the spot falling,** not the neighbours rising (the spot's own food had fallen in 97% of
   negative looks): crop eaten or not yet grown, plants foraged or dying back.
4. **Most spots are sown where food is thin** (the spot stood out less than its surroundings in 78% of sowings). The
   minority sown on rich spots taught a negative lesson 66% of the time: a regression to the mean, but a small share.

**Correction to D1.** D1 reported that sowers' belief "fell in 58%" a year after sowing and read it as learning failure.
That measure was confounded: every belief fades about 8% a year, and a belief lower a year later can come from fading,
or from many small negative looks against fewer, larger positive ones. D2 separates the sources: the net effect of
experience is positive. D1's reading "sowers mostly learn that it did not pay" is withdrawn; its other findings stand.

## Capture

5. **The sower's family gets back about what it sowed.** Per unit of seed, the sower and close kin harvested 1.08 from
   the spot within a year (pooled); the median sowing returned 0.25; only 35% of sowings returned more than their seed
   to the sower's family. Others harvested 1.43 per unit of seed: more than the family did.
6. **Autumn sowing, two thirds of all sowing, is a loss to the family:** 0.84 per unit of seed (summer 1.74, spring
   1.62, winter 1.19).

(Harvests are counted from the world's harvest events, which record only harvests of one unit or more; crop on a sown
spot includes crop from others' sowing there.)

## What it means for the creator's question

The creator's rule: "If sowing fails because returns aren't captured, that's a world-level explanation already present
in ACL. If sowing is not the explanation, then N3 becomes the leading path."

- **The learning signal is not the explanation.** Sowers' experience on balance tells them sowing works, and most keep
  sowing (77%, D1).
- **Capture is a real, world-level part of the explanation.** With no possession of what one sows, the investment pays
  the community (2.36 food per seed, D1) but not the investor's family, which breaks even, and loses on the autumn
  sowing it mostly does.
- **But capture does not explain why sowing stays so small** (1.6% of food, D1). People believe in it and keep doing it.
  Sowing is a "put seed" choice, so it likely faces the same gates S1 found for stashing (seed above two weeks' need, the
  one put option shared with materials, the pull scaled down by hunger). That is a hypothesis, not measured here.
- **And even fully captured, sowing would matter little for winter** in a world whose winter is barely lean (D1).

So sowing explains part of the absence of delayed return (why it is not an investment for a family), not all of it.
N3 (a harder lean season) remains a leading path, now beside property and capture.
