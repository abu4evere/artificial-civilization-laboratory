# CP1 — Courtship-to-pairing pipeline audit: findings

Frozen as `CP1-frozen` before any analysis (sections 1–6 of `questions.md`; correction appended before the first
read). Read-only on saved v2 controls: RT1b (`output_rt1b`, seeds 5701–5956) and FX2 (`output`, seeds 6001–6256) as
discovery (512 worlds, 187 extinct, 325 surviving); FX3 (`output`, seeds 7301–7556) as holdout (256 worlds, 80 extinct,

## Validity

All worlds loaded without error (512 / 512; 256 / 256). Every fertile woman-week accounted for: G1+G2+G3+G4+G5+
G6-eligible+G6-pregnant+residual = 100.0% in every world (gate shares sum to one). No world was excluded for low

## What it shows

1. **Extinct worlds fail at encounter, not courtship.** G2 is the dominant leak in both sets: in extinct worlds, 43%
   of fertile woman-weeks are spent single with an eligible man alive **somewhere** but **not within sight**. In
   surviving worlds that figure is 23% — half as much. G1 (no eligible man alive at all) adds another 20% vs 8%:
   together, extinct worlds spend 63% of fertile woman-weeks with no partner-candidate within sight, against
   32% in surviving worlds.

2. **G3 (courting-not-tried) is *smaller* in extinct worlds, not larger.** PF1 and PF2 found G3 was the biggest leak
   in MB1b's 80-founder worlds. In the 20-founder cohort TL1 read, G3 is 2–3% of fertile woman-weeks in extinct
   worlds and 21% in surviving ones. In extinct worlds the funnel breaks *earlier*: the woman never gets a man within
   sight, so there is no week where she is single with him near and doesn't court. G3 cannot leak what G1 and G2
   already removed.

3. **The number of founders matters for where the funnel breaks.** PF1/PF2's 80-founder worlds: encounter is largely
   solved, courtship-not-tried dominates. CP1's 20-founder worlds: encounter is the limit. The code's `perception`
   radius is the same in both; what changes is the population density on the map (`c.width`, `c.height`, founders per
   world).

4. **Co-residence (G4) is *less* of a leak in extinct worlds, not more.** G4 is partner-alive-but-apart; it only
   counts weeks a woman already has a partner. Extinct worlds have fewer partnered weeks to spend apart in the first
   place, so G4's share is lower there (13%) than in surviving worlds (23%). This matches C1's earlier finding that
   co-residence failure is a general feature of ACL couples, not specific to extinction.

5. **The physiological gates (G5, G6) are quiet.** Starving + nursing shares differ by <1 pp between cohorts;
   G6-eligible differs by ~1.5 pp; G6-pregnant by ~2 pp. Extinction is not driven by weeks where everything is in
   place and conception still does not happen.

6. **TL1's Q2 result refined.** TL1 found that at the year of the last birth, an eligible man was alive *somewhere
   in the world* in 61–68% of extinct worlds. CP1 adds the distance split that TL1's Q2 could not: across all years
   of life, in extinct worlds that man is **usually not within sight** (G2 43% vs G1 20%).

## Against the prior (frozen; kept as written)

- "G3 (mate near, still single) will be the biggest leak in extinct worlds, excess 10–20 pp": **wrong**. G3 is the
  biggest leak in *surviving* worlds and is *smaller* in extinct worlds (−18 pp). The actual bottleneck is G2
  (encounter), +19.9 pp discovery / +19.0 pp holdout.
- "G1 will be < 10% overall and only marginally larger in extinct worlds": **partly wrong**. G1 is small in surviving
  worlds (8%) but 20% in extinct worlds — an +11 pp excess, not marginal. TL1's Q2 result (mate alive somewhere in
  the world 61–68% of the time at the last birth) still holds pooled; CP1 shows that across all woman-weeks of life,
  G1 is substantial in extinct worlds.
- "G2 will show a modest excess in extinct worlds": **right in direction, wrong in size** — the excess is +19.9 pp,
  the single largest gap at any gate.
- "G4 (apart) will be a sizeable share but not the biggest excess in extinct worlds": **right** (sizeable in
  surviving, smaller in extinct, −9 pp excess, not the bottleneck).
- "G5 and G6 will show little excess": **right** (all < 2.1 pp).

Scorecard: **2 of 5** priors held as written; **1 right in direction only**; **2 wrong**.

## What it cannot show

- **G3 is a black box on saved worlds.** CP1 cannot separate courting-offered-but-refused from courting-never-tried
  (that needs a logging replay — PF1 and PF2 did it on MB1b, 80-founder worlds). What CP1 reports for G3 is only the
  share of woman-weeks where an eligible man is within sight and she is still single at year end.
- **Causation.** The bottleneck is the gate with the largest *excess* in extinct worlds. It does not show that
  removing G2's leak would save worlds. An intervention (e.g. a nature change that raises `perception`, or anchors
  bands' movement so they meet) would be a Law 126 question, evidence first; per the creator's instruction, CP1 does
  not propose one.
- **Per-week within-year dynamics.** CP1 pools to year-level then world-level. G1–G3 use snapshot counts
  (`women_single*`, taken once per year by the F1-style observer); G4–G6 use the week-level `r_*` sums. The
  arithmetic that splits r_no_partner into G1/G2/G3 assumes the snapshot ratio is representative of all weeks of the
  year (reasonable for a slow-changing demographic variable, but not a per-week measure).
- **r_partner_dead, r_low_health, r_partner_child** are not stored on these saved worlds; they fall into the residual
  bucket, which is ~1% in both cohorts in both sets. The missing stores do not change the ranking.
- **Nature version.** CP1 reads the v2 cohort TL1 read. Nature v3 (adopted today, `nature-v3`) adds `parts_first`
  teaching only; it does not touch partner finding, so the G2-encounter result is expected to carry across v2 and v3
  without re-running — but that is an expectation, not a verified claim. Any future study should be on v3.

---

## What CP1 answers, in one line

**In 20-founder worlds that go extinct, the pipeline breaks at encounter: an eligible man is alive but not within
sight 43% of fertile woman-weeks, against 23% in worlds that survive. Courtship-not-tried (PF1/PF2's bottleneck in
80-founder worlds) is *less* of a leak in extinct 20-founder worlds, because the funnel is already broken earlier.**

The creator asked for a bottleneck. CP1 locates it at **G2 (encounter)** in both discovery and holdout, and leaves
the question of whether changing anything would save worlds for a later study, as instructed.
