1. **Whose habits changes completely.** Without whole-table copying, own experience becomes the majority of what
   steers adults (S 65%, N 74%; BL0's rule would read both as "mostly learned"). Copying in S shrinks to 14%.
2. **Habits steer just as much** in every arm (choice effect 0.14–0.15): copying changed where habits come from, not how
   much they matter.
3. **People stay almost as alike.** Removing copying entirely raises the parents-versus-stranger gap only from 0.050 to
   0.058: most of the sameness is still there with no copying at all. This leans toward the council's caution (the same
   world and rewards make people alike), but the frozen reading does not say so at this sample, and it is not claimed.
4. **Extinction:** no arm differs from W (S 12, N 10, sham 12 against W 8 of 32; all p > 0.28).
