# TC1 — Does copying whole habit tables in talk make everyone alike? Findings

Frozen as `TC1-frozen` (signed off 14 October 2026). Discovery reading written before the holdout ran
(`findings_discovery.md`). Tables: `results_discovery.md`, `results_holdout.md`, from `analyse_tc1.py`.

## Validity

Both sets: zero check PASS; **W reproduces BL0 exactly in all 64 worlds** (every recorded number); one fingerprint;
I + C + E + T adds up in every arm (largest error 5 × 10⁻¹⁵); S and N differ from W in every world. Sham quiet in both
sets. **Valid.**

## The frozen reading: unresolved (stands; discovery and holdout agree)

Sameness = how much closer a child's habits at 20 are to their parents' than to an unrelated adult's (median of world
means; larger = less alike than strangers, more family-specific).

| | W (today) | N (no copying) | ratio N ÷ W [95% interval] | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| discovery | 0.050 | 0.058 | 1.17 [0.96, 1.63] | 0.011 |
| holdout | 0.050 | 0.066 | 1.32 [1.03, 1.71] | 0.003 |

Neither "the copying rule makes people alike" (N at least twice W) nor "the world makes them alike" (interval inside
0.75–1.25) holds in either set. **Frozen rule: unresolved at this sample.**

**What the numbers do show** (described; not a frozen reading): switching copying off makes families' habits somewhat
more distinct from strangers', by about a fifth to a third, consistently (p < 0.05 in both sets); but most of the
sameness remains with no copying at all. Copying adds to the sameness; it is not most of it. This leans toward the
council's caution (the same world and rewards make people alike), and the frozen rule does not let it be claimed.

## Described (both sets alike)

| | W (today) | S (speaker's situation) | N (no copying) |
|---|---|---|---|
| own experience's share of the adult habit term | 0.372 / 0.372 | **0.650 / 0.658** | **0.735 / 0.754** |
| copied share | 0.569 / 0.560 | 0.141 / 0.135 | 0 / 0 |
| choice effect (do habits steer?) | 0.140 / 0.139 | 0.146 / 0.141 | 0.147 / 0.142 |
| worlds died out (of 32) | 8 / 14 | 12 / 15 | 10 / 14 |

1. **Whose habits they are changes completely.** Without whole-table copying, own experience becomes the majority of what
   steers adults: about two thirds when only the speaker's current situation is copied (S), three quarters with no copying
   (N). By BL0's own rule, both would read "mostly learned"; today's world reads "mixed".
2. **How much habits matter does not change.** The choice effect stays at 0.14 in every arm, in both sets.
3. **Survival does not change.** No arm differs from W in extinction in either set (all sign tests p ≥ 0.29).

## Reading

Whole-table copying in talk is why ACL's people hold mostly borrowed habits (BL0), and replacing it with copying only
what the speaker is living now makes their habits mostly their own, at no cost to survival and with habits steering
exactly as much. It is not, by itself, why everyone ends up alike: most of that sameness survives even with no copying at
all, which points (unproven) at the world everyone shares.

**What it decides (the creator decides):** whether situation-specific copying (S) is a candidate for ACL's nature. Under
Law 126 it still needs its evidence review (T2) before it could be adopted; TC1 shows it is safe for survival and changes
whose habits people have. Also open: the deeper question of talk itself (a learned signal instead of direct copying).
Nothing in nature changes.
