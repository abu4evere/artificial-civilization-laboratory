# P1 — Risk pooling or spreading scarcity: findings

Read-only replays of E13R arms A and B, seeds 501–516; probe read-only in both (state hash, 20 years); all 32 replays
equal to the saved runs. Questions in `questions.md` (one amendment before any data: help is looked for in the two weeks
after a short week, since someone fed in a week is no longer short by its end); tables in `findings_tables.md`.
Descriptive; associations, not causes.

**Main caveat.** Close kin live near each other and share the same land, so "family food" also measures how good the
local land and year are. Read-only cannot separate the family's sharing from the family's shared place. Arm B differs
from A in copying as well as in sharing, so B against A is not a clean test of sharing alone.

1. **Family food protects the poorest, strongly.** Among people starting winter with under half a week of their own
   food, the share who starved to death in winter fell from 3.25% when their family also had under half a week per
   head, to 1.02% (half a week to two), to 0.13% (two weeks or more): a 25-fold gradient (A; B alike, 3.63% to 0.70%).
   Going short fell from 70% to 48%. For people holding two weeks or more of their own, family food made little
   difference.
2. **People rarely starve amid plenty.** Of 288 winter starvation deaths in A (397 in B), only 15.6% (13.6%) had a close
   kin within sight holding two weeks or more of food; the median total held by all their close kin was 11.5 (7.7);
   in 16% (23%) the kin held nothing. Deaths come mostly when the whole family is near empty.
3. **Help reaches the hungry, but rarely.** Only about 10% of adult short-weeks in winter were followed by a gift within
   two weeks. Those helped died within four weeks half as often (0.74% against 1.61% in A; 0.68% against 2.04% in B).
4. **Giving does not push givers into hunger.** At every food level, adults went short less often in the four weeks
   after weeks they gave than after weeks they did not (17% against 37% at the lowest level, in A), most likely because
   those who give are those whose food is coming in.
5. **More sharing, no better winters.** Arm B, which gives 40% more in winter: going short 47.8% against 48.8% (B lower
   in 9 worlds of 16), winter starvation deaths 0.68% against 0.49% of person-winters (B higher in 10 of 16).

## Reading

The family network does pool risk: a hungry member of a family with food is far safer than a hungry member of a family
without, people seldom starve while kin nearby hold plenty, and giving costs the giver little. But it pools almost
nothing. A family enters winter holding about a ninth of what it will need (W1), help reaches a hungry week only one
time in ten, and when people die the whole family is nearly empty. Sharing insures a family against one member's bad
week; it cannot insure it against a winter that empties everyone, and more sharing did not make winters better.

So the bottleneck is not how food is distributed. It is how much there is to distribute when winter comes, and the one
way to make that larger, the store, is the thing sharing outcompetes (S1).
