# E2 — After the four changes · Predictions (FROZEN)

**Experiment:** E2. The same 8 seeds as E1 (101–108), 200 years each, read at years 100 and 200.
**Versions:** World Bible 7.1 · world 0.3 · agent 0.3.
**Changes since E1:**
1. Reputation from tested accuracy: per-source track records; belief in a source moves from goodwill
   toward its record; reputation passed on by word.
2. More testing: told claims become things to check; turned earth counts as watching someone sow;
   reports of water and of animals seen, checked on sight.
3. Records: activity traces on the land; marks can come to mean what was done there; reading a mark
   gives a claim, or an idea for a technique, with the mark as its source.
4. Floating as an affordance: water blocks walking (the 3-tile jump is gone); far shores are noticed;
   crossing to new land is what gives a raft its worth; onlookers see it; sea trips keep their heading.

**Written:** 2026-10-02, before any E2 run, by Claude, from 30-year spot checks on 3 seeds. Not to be
edited after the results are in.

## Predictions

1. **Testing.** Tested claims average at least 20 per world-year (E1: about 5).
2. **Reputation tracks truth.** The pooled correlation between how often people are right and how
   others' records rate them is at least 0.4 (E1's accuracy-vs-trust: −0.07).
3. **Accuracy is still mostly not a trait.** The split-half correlation of a person's accuracy stays
   below 0.2, because reports of water and fresh sightings are right for almost everyone.
4. **Experts are rare.** In at most half the surviving seeds, at least one person is rated reliable
   (record 0.75 or more) by 5 or more people at year 100.
5. **Rafts get used.** Every surviving seed has at least 20 open-water crossings on something that
   floats over 200 years (E1: 0–2 over water wider than a jump). Still, under 10% of living people
   carry a raft at any yearly count.
6. **Islands isolate more.** Without the jump, mixing between founding bands at year 100 is lower than
   in E1 in at least 5 of the 7 seeds alive at year 100.
7. **Records teach techniques.** At least 10 techniques are taken up from marks in every surviving seed,
   and in at least 2 seeds a technique nobody alive knew is recovered from a mark.
8. **Place-marks mislead.** Claims whose source is a mark fail more often than they hold.
9. **Population holds.** The mean population at year 100 across seeds, counting a seed that died out as 0,
   is within 25% of E1's (46.6).
10. **Sowing still fails.** Nothing here changes who eats a crop. Harvests per world-year stay within 50% of E1's.

## Kill criterion (World Bible v7.1 §4)

Added while E2 was running, before any results were read; the only output seen was one progress line
(seed 102: 37 alive at year 100). The four changes are declared failed, and must be revisited before
anything is built on them, if any of these hold:

- tested claims stay under 10 per world-year (the testing change failed);
- reputation does not track accuracy: pooled r below 0.2 (the reputation change failed);
- no open-water crossing on something that floats happens in any seed (the affordance change failed);
- no technique is ever taken up from a mark in any seed (the records change failed);
- the mean population at year 100 falls below half of E1's (the changes broke the world).
