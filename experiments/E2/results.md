## Checks on the scored numbers

- **Correction to E1's report.** "About 5 claims tested per world-year" counted a claim once per person who had
  passed it on. Counted once per test, E1 had about 2 per world-year. E2's 33 per world-year is counted the
  same way, so the comparison above is like with like.
- **Prediction 5's 100%** came from a year when one person was left alive. Over years with 10 or more alive,
  4.9% of people carried a raft on average (47% at most). The crossings half of the prediction still failed:
  three surviving seeds had fewer than 20.
- **Why the population fell.** Not more deaths: the causes keep the same shares (illness 37%, starvation 30%,
  old age 23%, thirst 7%). Births fell by a third: 0.87 per world-year against 1.31 in E1. Not yet explained.
- **Are the "experts" better?** No. At the end, the 29 people rated reliable by 5 or more others were right 93%
  of the time; the 134 other people with 4 or more tested claims were right 92% of the time. Reputation
  separates the unreliable from the rest, but nobody is better than the rest.
- **Sowing.** The share of people who believe in sowing rose from 10% to 23% on average, and harvests doubled
  (2.7 to 5.5 per world-year). Fields standing at year end barely changed (0.38 to 0.45 per world-year).
- **Skills recovered from marks.** Six times, in seeds 103, 106 and 108, someone learned a technique from a mark
  that nobody alive knew. It was the same technique every time: striking wood with flint.
