## What the pilot shows (populations per decade, read only to describe the stop)

- **W collapses early.** Three of eight W worlds were down to a handful within the first ten to twenty years
  (seed 812: 2 people at year 10; seed 815: 4; seed 816: 31 then 4). At half-width 0.95 a year's growing weather can be
  close to zero everywhere at once: not a wider spread of ordinary years, but years with almost no growth on any tile.
- **P declines more slowly** (most P worlds lose people steadily over decades rather than in one blow): a bad quarter
  leaves three others.
- **The control itself died out in 4 of 8 worlds.** The second stop condition (5 of 8 with all arms alive) would be hard
  to meet at A2-full on 120 years even with a milder manipulation. This is ACL's baseline fragility, already seen in L1
  (whose redesigned pilot passed at exactly 5 of 8), not something U caused.

## Together with the calibration

The calibration found that ACL's land smooths the weather: the widest possible weather moves autumn food by only ~11% a
year (grassland ~37%). The pilot finds that this widest weather, applied, kills. **In ACL's present world there is no
weather setting that gives realistic food variability, and the setting that comes closest is already lethal.** The
weather and the people's food are coupled through the land in a way U's design did not anticipate: the plants' caps
damp the good years, while the rare near-zero years are deadly.

## For the creator (no choice made here)

1. **Stop U here** and record that ACL's land cannot produce grassland-like uncertainty through the weather alone.
2. **A narrower width** chosen by a new rule decided in advance (for example, the widest width whose pilot passes).
   Note: this would select the width by survival, i.e. by a people-level outcome, which the calibration was designed to
   avoid; it would also test less uncertainty still (0.70 gave CV 0.084, against 0.072 now).
3. **Make the land itself vary** (caps, regrowth, dieback responding to the year): a change to nature, a new design.
4. **First address baseline fragility** (half of control worlds die out in 120 years), which limits every
   paired-world design, including any rerun of U.
