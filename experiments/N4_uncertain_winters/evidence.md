## Sources

**Who insures what: sharing covers idiosyncratic risk, not shocks that hit everyone**
- **Ravallion, M. and Chaudhuri, S. (1997).** Risk and insurance in village India: comment. *Econometrica* 65(1).
  [doi:10.2307/2171818](https://doi.org/10.2307/2171818) (abstract read). With perfect risk-sharing, a household's
  consumption is insured against idiosyncratic risk and depends only on the aggregate risk; rural households are commonly
  thought vulnerable to village-wide shocks such as poor rains.
- **Townsend, R. M. (1994).** Risk and insurance in village India. *Econometrica* 62(3).
  [doi:10.22004/ag.econ.294664](https://doi.org/10.22004/ag.econ.294664) (abstract read; working-paper record). In three
  southern Indian villages, household consumption co-moves strongly: local insurance of each household's own shocks is
  good, if not perfect.
- **Hoogeveen, H. (2001).** Evidence on informal insurance in rural Zimbabwe (working paper) (abstract read). Households
  also rely on **buffer stocks** to shield consumption from income shocks, alongside informal insurance.
- **Clarke, D. and Dercon, S. (2015).** Insurance, credit, and safety nets for the poor in a world of risk.
  [doi:10.5040/9781472544421.ch-004](https://doi.org/10.5040/9781472544421.ch-004) (abstract read). Formal insurance
  should focus on catastrophic and serious **covariate** risks, building on informal systems.

**Uncertainty can also favour sharing and moving**
- **Lewis, H. M. et al. (2014).** High mobility explains demand sharing and enforced cooperation in egalitarian
  hunter-gatherers. *Nature Communications* 5. [doi:10.1038/ncomms6789](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6789) (abstract
  read). In a simulation, demand-sharing families that move between camps survive the **unpredictable environments
  typical of hunter-gatherers**, while sedentary and non-sharing families die out.
- **Halstead, P. and O'Shea, J. (eds.) (1989).** *Bad Year Economics: Cultural Responses to Risk and Uncertainty.*
  Cambridge University Press (record only: its existence and subject confirmed by several reviews, e.g.
  [doi:10.2307/1060647](https://doi.org/10.2307/1060647); its content not read here).

**How variable real years are**
- **Shi, Y. et al. (2014).** Field-based observations of regional-scale, temporal variation in net primary production in
  Tibetan alpine grasslands. *Biogeosciences* 11. [doi:10.5194/bg-11-2003-2014](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-11-2003-2014)
  (abstract read). Across 40 sites, above-ground production varied from year to year with a **coefficient of variation
  of 36.6%** (over four years), **synchronously across the region**; alpine grasslands were not more sensitive than other
  grassland types.
- **Ray, D. K. et al. (2015).** Climate variation explains a third of global crop yield variability. *Nature
  Communications* 6. [doi:10.1038/ncomms6989](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms6989) (abstract read). Year-to-year climate
  explains about a third of the world's crop-yield variability, more than 60% in parts of the breadbaskets.
- **Gherardi, L. and Sala, O. E. (2018).** Effect of interannual precipitation variability on dryland productivity: a
  global synthesis. *Global Change Biology* 25(1). [doi:10.1111/gcb.14480](https://doi.org/10.1111/gcb.14480) (abstract
  read). More variable rainfall **lowers** average production in wetter drylands (and raises it in the driest).
- **Huang, L. et al. (2016).** Drought dominates the interannual variability in global terrestrial net primary
  production. *Scientific Reports* 6. [doi:10.1038/srep24639](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep24639) (abstract read).

## What the evidence says

1. **The sharpening of H-L5 holds, by definition and in data.** Pooling within a group can only insure what is not
   common to the whole group: a household's consumption, under full sharing, still moves with the aggregate shock
   (Ravallion and Chaudhuri). Villages insure their members' own misfortunes well (Townsend) and are vulnerable to
   village-wide bad years. Households hold buffer stocks as well as sharing (Hoogeveen). ACL's own P1 found the same
   shape: sharing protects a hungry person whose family has food, and fails when everyone is empty. **So storage is the
   candidate answer to correlated bad years; sharing is the answer to idiosyncratic ones.**
2. **But uncertainty does not point only to storage.** For mobile foragers in unpredictable environments, a model finds
   that sharing plus moving between camps is what survives (Lewis et al.). H-L5's experiment must allow that the answer to
   unpredictable winters is more sharing and more moving, and count it as a finding.
3. **ACL is far more predictable than a real grassland.** Real above-ground production varies from year to year with a
   coefficient of variation around a third (Shi et al.: 36.6%, synchronous across a region; one study, four years).
   ACL's world varies about 7% (D1). Raising ACL's variability toward real values is anchored to a source, not tuned:
   a width giving a coefficient of variation near a third is the realistic target (to be checked against more than one
   study before any design is frozen).
4. **"Same average" must mean the same average food, not the same average weather.** In real ecosystems more variable
   weather can lower average production (Gherardi and Sala). In ACL growth is linear in the year's weather factor, but
   caps and winter dieback are not, so a mean-preserving weather change may not preserve mean food. Any design must
   check average food per tile (L1's lesson) in both arms.

## The N4 candidates, against the evidence

| | candidate | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | wider swings, same average | **anchored:** a coefficient of variation near a third is realistic (Shi et al.); correlated (synchronous) |
| U2 | rare severe winters | plausible; no rarity or severity number found in this review (still a dial) |
| U4 | patchy bad years (idiosyncratic) | **the theory predicts sharing and moving answer it** (Ravallion and Chaudhuri; Townsend; Lewis et al.) |
| U1 + U4 | same variability, correlated vs patchy | the cleanest test of the sharpened H-L5 |

## Assistant's view (the creator decides)

The evidence supports the sharpened H-L5 as a theory and gives U1 a realistic anchor. The strongest first design is
**U1 at a realistic width (correlated), beside U4 at the same total variability (patchy)**, with the unchanged world as
the control: H-L5 predicts storage (stashes, stock, autumn gathering) rises in correlated worlds more than in patchy
ones, and sharing and moving rise in patchy ones. U2 lacks an anchor for its rarity and severity. Before any freeze: a
second source on interannual variability, and a pilot with same-seed controls that measures average food per tile in
each arm.
