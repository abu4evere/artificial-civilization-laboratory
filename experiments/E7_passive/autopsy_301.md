# E7.5: Autopsy of seed 301 · what appeared in year 115

Watch-only. Seed 301 was replayed under the E7 code to year 135 by `autopsy_301.py`, with a probe that only
reads events and people. The replay matched the saved E7 run at every 10-year sample (29, 27, 28, 33, 33, 34, 34,
33, 31, 32, 37, 40, 47 people). Data: `output/autopsy_301.json`.

Seed 301 was the only E7 world, and the only world in any experiment, whose latest lasting novelty came after
year 100. This autopsy asks why.

## 1. What exactly happened in year 115?

The novelty is a **behaviour**: deliberately *making* (from a remembered recipe, not in play) a thing of one
particular kind: edge in the middle band, hardness high, nothing else. Every thing of that kind in the first
135 years was a **flint flake**: flint struck with flint (15 times) or flint struck with stone (8 times).

That is not new. The flint flake was:
- first known in **year 2**, and first made in play in **year 4** by Daiyei;
- **taught at least 24 times** between years 9 and 72, and read from marks on rock at least 18 times;
- an ordinary part of the world's knowledge for over a century before year 115.

What was new in year 115 was narrower: for the first time, three different people **deliberately made** a flake
of this middle sharpness within ten years: Mouthir (year 115), then Vetibin and Sosei.

## 2. Who?

| | Mouthir (first) | Vetibin | Sosei |
|---|---|---|---|
| age in year 115 | 18 | 44 | 8 |
| generation | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| crafting skill | 0.15 | 0.29 | 0.14 |
| notable temperament | caring (0.78), fairly bold (0.58) | curious (0.68) | hot-tempered (0.75) |
| fate | starved, year 130 | old age, year 124 | alive at year 135 |

For comparison, Daiyei, who first made the flake in play in year 4, had reached a crafting skill of 0.82.

## 3. Why those people?

Two facts together, both measured:

- **Deliberate making is very rare in this world.** From year 105 to 125 the whole world made anything from a
  recipe 0 to 3 times a year. A behaviour needs three different people within ten years to count as a
  novelty, which in a world this quiet is a matter of chance.
- **A flake's sharpness depends on the maker's skill** (`matter.strike`). A skilled striker gets a sharper flake,
  in a higher band and so a different "kind". The three makers in years 115–125 were unskilled (0.14 to 0.29).
  Their flakes came out in the middle band.

**Interpretation (not proven):** the year-115 "novelty" is most likely a duller flint flake, made by unskilled
people, recorded as new because the novelty definition separates kinds by how sharp they are. If so, it is a
sign of skill being thin, not of discovery.

## 4. Why that world?

Nothing found here makes seed 301 special in a meaningful way. Making this kind deliberately never counted as a
novelty in any other E7 world or in any baseline world. Its appearance depends on a rare coincidence (three
unskilled deliberate makers within ten years) in a world where deliberate making is rare. The evidence fits
**chance under the novelty definition**, not a world on the edge of something.

## 5. Did fired clay matter?

**No direct link.** Neither recipe involves clay. Fired clay appeared passively 36 times in seed 301 before year
115, but nothing in the flake's history touches it: it was known from year 2, taught from year 9, and remade by
people with no connection to clay found in the record. An indirect effect (for example, through how many people
survived) cannot be ruled out, because the E7 and baseline histories diverge from the first week and cannot be
compared person by person.

## What this changes

- E7's one late novelty is **not evidence that passive physics extends novelty**. E7's real effect on novelty
  after year 100 is therefore **none**.
- The novelty measure has a weakness: it can count a **loss of skill** as something new, because a worse version
  of an old thing falls into a different band. Future experiments should check every late novelty for this
  before reporting it.
- Two facts from this autopsy deserve their own follow-up:
  - deliberate making almost never happens (0–3 a year in a world of 30–47 people);
  - skill thins over generations: the founder who first made the flake reached 0.82, while the people making it
    a century later were at 0.14–0.29.
