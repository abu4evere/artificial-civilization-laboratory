**Gifts do not explain the hunger.** Gifts fell by about two fifths in both sets, but an adult who was starving under HB1
had someone holding a surplus within reach just as often as in the control (about 40% and 37% of starving adult-weeks in
both arms). **Channel not identified:** surplus and choice both fell (by about 14–19%), and their order swapped between
the sets. Meetings did not fall. The assistant's prior (meetings) was wrong, the third wrong prior in a row.

## Also measured (descriptive, consistent in both sets)

- Starving rose under HB1 **even among adults who had received a gift in the last 8 weeks** (0.043 / 0.042 against
  0.024 / 0.023): a gift did not protect as it did in the control.
- More of the gift food stayed **within a couple home** (80% in both sets, against 59% / 68%); givers' and recipients'
  homes lay closer (about 1.8 against 3.0–3.3 tiles); a smaller share went to people who were neither partner nor kin.

## Where the search stands (HF1–HF3)

HB1's extra hunger is real and repeats on every set of worlds measured (adult starvation about 1.6–1.8× the control's).
Read-only, with thresholds fixed in advance and holdouts on new seeds, it has **not** been explained by:

| check | candidate | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| HF1 | food on the land around homes ran out | not confirmed (land ~3% less full) |
| HF2 | too many children per carer; meat | neither (dependants +8–12%; meat ~0.02% of food) |
| HF3 | lost gifts | not supported (help within reach as often) |

What the three agree on is a set of **small shifts**, each too small alone: food taken in per adult about 4% lower,
adults holding a surplus about 18% fewer, gifts about 40% fewer, foraging near home about 8% poorer. In ACL a week counts
as starving when an adult falls more than 5% short of need: if many adults sit near that line, small shifts in what they
hold could tip many over it. **That is a hypothesis, not a finding**, and it is the only one these data still allow.
