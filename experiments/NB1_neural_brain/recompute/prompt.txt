Blind recompute for a study in an agent-based simulation of hunter-gatherers (ACL study NB1: can people with an inherited, evolving neural-network brain survive and improve over generations?). Please compute only from the attached files; you have not seen and should not guess any earlier result. Arms: c = baseline (rule-based minds), n = neural brain with learning within life, e = neural brain without learning (evolution only); the same 16 seeds (25401-25416) in every arm; 600 years. Files: nb1_worlds.csv (per world: extinct 1/0 and sums over years 100-600: living_weeks, deaths by starvation and killing, census_adults and census_health of adults 16-49 at year ends, child_weeks, child_deaths and child_death_starvation of children under 12, adult_weeks, gifts_kin_children); nb1_gens.csv (per world and generation: known = births whose outcome is known, reached16, adult_deaths = deaths of people who reached 16, sum_adult_age_years = the sum of their ages at death); nb1_g.csv (per n and e world: mean g at years 1, 50, 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600; g is an inherited gene that scales the old rule-based score, starting at 1); nb1_garden.csv (phase 2: genomes from generations 1, 9 and 18 of each n world put as all founders into fresh identical worlds, learning on (arm suffix L) or off (N); per world: known = children born by year 44, reached16, founder_weeks, births). Use a paired world bootstrap: 10,000 times draw the 16 seeds with replacement, use the same drawn seeds (with multiplicities) for every arm, and recompute each quantity from the drawn worlds; take the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles. Compute: (1) extinct worlds per arm; (2) the population ratio n/c and e/c = sum living_weeks / sum living_weeks; (3) for each n world, the weighted least-squares slope over generations 1 to 20 of survival to 16 per birth (reached16/known, weight known, only generations with known >= 10) and of adult lifespan (sum_adult_age_years/adult_deaths, weight adult_deaths, only generations with adult_deaths >= 10), needing at least 3 generations; then the mean slope over worlds with its bootstrap interval (bootstrap the worlds; a world without a slope is left out of that draw's mean); the same for e; (4) the mean over worlds of g at each listed year for n and e; (5) phase 2: for each source seed, survival to 16 per birth (reached16/known) in g18N minus g1N, the mean difference with a bootstrap over source seeds; and learning on minus off, pooled over generations 1, 9, 18 (pairs gXL - gXN), mean and bootstrap. The reading: 'neural people not viable' if n has more than 3 extinct worlds more than c, or the upper bound of the n/c population ratio is below 0.80; else 'improves by itself' if (the lower bound of either n slope in (3) is above 0, or the lower bound of the g18N - g1N difference is above 0) and mean g of n at year 600 is below 0.9; else 'decorative' if mean g of n stays at or above 0.9 at every listed year; else 'no improvement shown'. Report every quantity with its interval (4 or 5 decimals) and the reading. Run code if you can, loading the attached files with your code tool.
