- Rule R2 adds some making in the first decades, then making collapses at the same pace as in Arm 0. The high
  late rates in 303 and 304 come from one or two survivors in dying worlds (tiny person-weeks), the same
  artefact noted in E7.6.
- Two Arm 1 worlds died out (303 by year 100, 304 by year 76); in both, few deaths were from starvation (3 and
  5), mostly illness, old age and thirst in a population already down to a handful. Histories diverge from the
  first week under any code change, so with four seeds this cannot be attributed to R2.
- The two surviving Arm 1 worlds were larger than Arm 0 (80 vs 40; 103 vs 96) and had more starvation (60 vs 23;
  117 vs 78) and less food per person.

**Reading (per A9's decision table, first row):** the holding check was not what kept people from making. Freeing
it changed little after the first decades. The next step is a read-only autopsy of why people stop making: how
they value the things they hold, and what wins over making in the weeks they could make.
