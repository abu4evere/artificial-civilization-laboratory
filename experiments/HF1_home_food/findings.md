## The frozen verdict

**Frozen rule: not confirmed** (≥ 0.90 in both sets). The land around HB1's homes was about 3% less full than around the
same couples' homes in the control. HB1's extra hunger is real and repeats on new seeds, but **local food depletion is
not what caused it**. Under the frozen design, **HB2 (a home that moves with the food) is not designed until the cause
is found.**

## What else was measured (descriptive)

| | discovery H ÷ C | holdout H ÷ C |
|---|---|---|
| fullness on the ring 4–6 tiles out | 1.039 | 1.037 |
| food per forage action within 3 tiles of home | 0.925 | 0.914 |
| share of adult forage actions within 3 tiles of home | 1.316 | 1.299 |
| people per land tile within 3 tiles of home | 1.180 | 1.116 |
| hunts per person-year | 0.597 | 0.777 |

Couples in H foraged closer to home (about 53% of forage actions within 3 tiles, against about 40%), got somewhat less
per action there, and left the ring a short walk out a little fuller. Hunting fell in both sets (by about 40% and 22%).
The pattern before starvation deaths (fullness around those who starved against all adults) pointed different ways in
the two sets and is not read.

## Reading (caps)

- HB1 did not starve its people by emptying the land around their homes. Whatever made them hungry lies elsewhere.
- **Leads for the next read-only check (not findings):** (1) **meat**: hunting fell in both sets; (2) **mouths to feed**:
  HB1's couples had more children (HB1, `explore.md`), and in ACL carers feed dependants first, so more children per
  carer may leave adults short even with the same food on the land; (3) the small drop in food per forage action near
  home (about 8%) applied to more of the foraging.
- The assistant's prior (depletion confirmed) was wrong; recorded as such.
