# How real foragers learn to use tools (evidence notes for TU1, 21 October 2026)

Why: TU1 asks how ACL's people come to their first use of a hard thing on embedded food (on their own, after being
told, or after inheriting the item) and whether use spreads. These notes say what is known about real foragers, to read
TU1's result against, written before its data. **Notes only.** Each source says how much was read.

## Sources

### 1. Lew-Levy, S., Reckin, R., Lavi, N., Cristóbal-Azkarate, J. and Ellis-Davies, K. (2017). How do hunter-gatherer children learn subsistence skills? A meta-ethnographic review. *Human Nature* 28: 367–394. **Full text read earlier for CA3** (`../CA3_opportunity/evidence_teaching.md`); summary confirmed by record.
- Learning starts in infancy: parents take children foraging and give them **toy versions of tools**. In early and
  middle childhood, children learn in **multi-age playgroups through play, observation and participation**. Direct
  teaching of complex skills (hunting, complex tool making) comes mostly in adolescence, from adults who are not
  necessarily parents.

### 2. Lew-Levy, S., Andersen, M. M., Lavi, N. and Riede, F. (2022). Hunter-gatherer children's object play and tool use: an ethnohistorical analysis. *Frontiers in Psychology* 13: 824983. **Abstract/record.**
- 434 observations of children's toys and tools from 54 hunter-gatherer societies: most are used in play, and most are
  full-sized or miniature versions of adult tools.

### 3. Garfield, Z. H., Garfield, M. J. and Hewlett, B. S. (2016). A cross-cultural analysis of hunter-gatherer social learning. In *Social Learning and Innovation in Contemporary Hunter-Gatherers* (Springer). **Abstract/record.**
- 982 ethnographic texts on 23 societies (eHRAF): the most often reported transmission is **vertical** (from parents)
  and **oblique** (from other adults); teaching (demonstration, storytelling, general) accounts for 58% of the coded
  data.

### 4. Hewlett, B. S. and Roulette, C. J. (2016). Teaching in hunter-gatherer infancy. *Royal Society Open Science* 3: 150403. **Abstract/record** (already in CA3's notes).
- Aka infants are taught, often by demonstration and by being given objects, from the first year.

## What to read TU1 against (hypotheses, written before its data)

- Real tool skill is mostly **social**: watched, played with, shown, given, within families and playgroups; rarely a
  lone discovery. TU1's prior is the opposite for ACL (mostly own discovery), because ACL has no toy versions, no
  playgroups that share tools, no handing over of items, and opinions of items pass only in some talks.
- If TU1 finds first uses mostly own discovery and little spread, the gap with real foragers would point at those
  missing routes (handing things over, children handling adults' tools), not at tools being useless. Any of these
  would be a Law 126 question, and some (customs of giving tools to children) are culture and must not be written in.
