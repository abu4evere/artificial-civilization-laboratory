## Reading

**Valid. By the frozen rules: outcome A.** The manipulation check failed (winter did not get leaner per person, and a
week of foraging yielded the same), so the null on the frozen test (0 of 3 criteria met on the six paired seeds) says
nothing about lean seasons, only that removing winter growth did not make one in ACL. The frozen sentence applies:
failure to create delayed-return behaviour is not evidence that lean seasons are irrelevant.

**A flaw in the design, admitted.** The manipulation check measured plant food on land *per person*. L1 shrank the
populations it was measuring (final median 6 against 30 over all seeds; 13 against 32 on paired seeds), so food per
person rose even though the land grew less. The pre-registration should also have measured food per tile. The
population confound that Amendment 1 guarded against in the outcomes struck the manipulation check itself.

**Post-hoc observation (not a frozen finding): the leaner winter was absorbed by fewer people, not by different
behaviour.** Populations settled at a lower density at which each person's winter was as abundant as before (food per
forage week 22.2 against 21.7), so per-person scarcity never arrived and nothing in how people lived needed to change.
This matches the human evidence that the lean season sets how many people the land can carry (Zhu et al. 2021, in N3's
evidence review). It suggests that in ACL, as among real foragers, a steady lean season is answered by density, not by
storage; a lean season people could not adjust to by numbers (unpredictable bad winters, N3's L5) would be a different
test.

Extinctions were common in both arms (C 6 of 16, L 7 of 16), so only six seeds were paired. Children's winter
starvation is printed above but not interpreted: the frozen gate requires the links before it to hold.
