# GPT's view: what after CB1? (22 October 2026; GPT, not a verified source)

The creator asked for GPT's view first. Asked in the ACL ChatGPT project, with CB1's results. Condensed; the
substance is kept.

**Recommendation: C first.** Investigate *why* couples are apart, read-only. Then decide on B (cumulative culture or
delayed-return strategies). Do not run CT2 yet.

**Its reasons:**
- Separation is common (61%), but CB1 does not show it is the main cause of the missing births. MC1 raised births
  without a detectable survival benefit.
- A read-only breakdown tests a concrete mechanism cheaply, without changing nature. MC1 and HB1 show that changing
  proximity has trade-offs.

**What it would scope:**
- Break apart-time down by cause, using code-level reasons where possible: foraging in different places, caregiving,
  camp movement, other activities, or unexplained.
- Measure how long separations last, how often partners reunite, and whether apart weeks cost eligible weeks.
- Compare C and K1 paired at world level.
- Audit the eligibility logic and the event order.
- Keep it descriptive. If the saved logs cannot identify causes, say so rather than infer them from location.

**Ranking:** C (narrow and time-bounded) > B (choose by evidence strength and mechanism clarity, not by which promises
the most survival) > A (CT2; weak theory, would not validate loneliness as a mechanism).

**Main risk:** do not turn "partners are apart" into "partners must be kept together". Separation may serve food
gathering or caregiving; a natural mechanism can produce trade-offs rather than maximise births. If the logs cannot
tell the causes apart, stop and move to B.
