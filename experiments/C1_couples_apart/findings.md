# C1 — Why couples drift apart: findings (discovery confirmed on the holdout)

Read-only. Questions frozen as `C1-questions`. Control code, A2-full, first 40 years. Discovery seeds 501–516
(`results_discovery.md`, `findings_discovery.md`); holdout seeds 1101–1116 (`results_holdout.md`), run after the
discovery write-up and its confirmation thresholds were committed. Valid in both: observer read-only, every replay
reproduces F1's populations, code unchanged. Population beside every measure: tables in the results files.

## Confirmation (thresholds written before the holdout data)

| ≥ a quarter of apart weeks at exactly 3 tiles | 41.1% | 42.5% | **confirmed** |

## Findings

1. **ACL's couples are apart about half the time** (48–49% of couple-weeks, years 0–39), in the unchanged world.
2. **Two in five apart weeks are at exactly 3 tiles** (41–43%): too far to conceive (the code's `camp_radius` is 2),
   too near to feel any pull back (the pull toward a partner starts only beyond `reach`, 3). This gap is a property of
   two thresholds written separately in the code, not a behaviour of the people.
3. **Foraging splits couples.** Each partner picks their own foraging tile (the best of a few random tiles within
   reach); in about 60% of splits the mover was foraging, and in half both moved.
4. **Beyond reach, the pull is present but outbid by food.** "Join" is among the top options in three quarters of
   partner-weeks and chosen in about one in six; foraging is chosen in more than half. Affection is high (0.78) and
   hunger moderate (0.40).
5. **Reunions are often chance:** about a third are forage moves that happen to land near the partner.
6. More apart in winter (56–57%) than summer (42–43%), and when affection is low (78–83%); children under 6 make
   little difference (47–48% against 51–53%).
7. **Correction to F2, made here:** per couple, partners were out of reach 57.8% of the time in the control worlds and
   65.1% under perfect finding (`reconcile.md`); F2's "20% → 46%" was mostly more women having partners.

## What it means (caps)

- **Nothing in ACL's nature keeps a couple together while they forage.** Real forager couples co-reside (Hill et al.
  2011, in N5); ACL's live within reach of each other about half the time, by chance as much as by intent.
- C1 is read-only: it shows where the separation comes from in the code, not what would happen if it were changed.
- It bears on F1 and F2: births need partners within 2 tiles, so a couple apart half the time has, at most, half the
  weeks in which a child can be conceived.

## For the creator (no choice made)

Candidates, each needing evidence (rule 17), a feasibility check (rule 12) and sign-off:
- **The threshold gap:** the two distances (conception within 2; pull beyond 3) were set separately; whether they
  should agree is a question about nature, to be anchored to what "living together" means for real foragers.
- **Couples forage apart:** whether partners should weigh being near each other when choosing where to forage (real
  foragers are commonly described as foraging apart by day and returning to a shared camp, which would make a **shared
  camp**, not side-by-side foraging, the realistic unit; **this is a lead from general ethnography, not from a source
  read in this project, and must be checked in an evidence review before it is used**).
- **The pull loses to food:** its strength against hunger, if anything, should come from evidence, not from making
  couples stay together until births rise.
