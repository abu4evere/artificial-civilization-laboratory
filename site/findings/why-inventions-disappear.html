Why inventions disappear in an artificial civilization

ACL's people invent things from their first weeks: carriers, rafts, fired clay, a vessel that can hold water. Yet almost none of it accumulates. Three studies traced where the chain breaks.

The chain

For a discovery to become part of a people's life, it has to be invented, be useful, be passed to someone else, be kept, be built on, and be passed on again. ACL's chain audit followed every useful invention in dozens of replayed worlds through those links.

Where it breaks: the first hand-over

11% / 10%

of useful inventions were ever learned by anyone but their inventor (two independent sets of worlds). About nine in ten first hand-overs were teaching.

99%

of the inventions lost before any lesson were pushed out of the inventor's memory within a season: a person can remember how to make twelve things, and a new invention starts out valued at nothing.

1 in 12

When something made from other made things could have been taught, the listener knew how to make its parts about one time in twelve.

Four in five of the inventions never passed on were made by children, who play and invent the most. The ones that did spread were held about five times as long and shown to about twice as many people.

What comes next

The next experiment changes, as a test of nature and not of history, how memory chooses what to forget and whether a lesson can start with the parts of a craft. Its plan was frozen before any data.
