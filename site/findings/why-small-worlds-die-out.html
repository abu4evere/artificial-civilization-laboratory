Why small simulated societies die out

In ACL's standard world, twenty founders on a 40 × 40 map, about a third of worlds die out within 120 years. Not in famine or war: in quiet.

Births stop first

In every one of the 22 worlds that died out among 64 studied, births stopped 40 to 65 years before the end and the people aged out. In the last 30 years, women of childbearing age had no partner in every single week. Worlds that would die were identifiable decades ahead from their population at year 20.

Search or size?

A diagnostic experiment gave the same worlds perfect partner finding. Weeks without a partner fell from 55% to 11% and deaths of worlds from 22 to 7. In the 7 that still died, single women had no eligible man anywhere at all: the pool was simply empty. Both search and size matter.

Smaller bands, more mixing, same loneliness

With 80 founders, very few worlds die. Splitting the same 80 into 4, 8 or 16 bands did not change survival (13, 8 and 7 of 256 worlds died out, within chance). Smaller bands met far more: couples across different families rose from 6% to 54%. Yet the share of time women spent without a partner stayed at about 0.46, and births did not change. Meeting more people was not the same as finding someone. A follow-up audit is now tracing where partner finding stops.
