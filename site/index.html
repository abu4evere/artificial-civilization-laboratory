What ACL is

The Artificial Civilization Laboratory is an artificial civilization simulation: an agent-based world of hunter-gatherers in which nature is programmed and culture is not. People have bodies, hunger, cold, fear, love, grief, curiosity and memory. Stone can be struck, wood floats, seeds grow. There is no list of inventions, no reward for being clever and no script. Whatever they come to make, believe or pass on, they find for themselves, and they learn their habits by trial and error in their own lifetimes.

ACL uses no large language models. A model trained on human writing already knows what a village, a trade and a law are; if its agents built one, nobody could tell whether civilization emerged or was remembered.

The mission

ACL is not trying to recreate Earth, or to replay human history. Its mission, in its creator's words, is to create a world with minimum laws, and enough time, to produce an autonomous and cognitive mind: someone who grows from their own life, and from what earlier generations left behind, into more than anyone was given at the start. Inside the project that hoped-for person has a name: Bob. Bob does not exist yet.

Real human evidence is used as the referee, not the blueprint: every law of nature is checked against how real people live, so that nobody can quietly tune the world toward the answer they want. Which of those laws a mind truly needs is one of the questions ACL is built to answer.

Where ACL is now

The road to the mission, in levels. Each one is marked only by what the experiments have shown.

About 29% of the way, counted by levels (updated 25 October 2026).

  1. 0

    A lawful world done

    Nature is written as laws: bodies, materials, seasons. Every world replays exactly, and every instrument is checked to change nothing. The evidence

  2. 1

    Life goes on partly

    People find food, pair, raise children and outlive their founders on their own. But small worlds are fragile: about a third die out within 120 years. The evidence

  3. 2

    People learn by themselves done

    With no list of inventions, people make carriers, rafts, fired clay and vessels, by trial and error in their own lifetimes. The evidence

  4. 3

    Knowledge spreads partly

    Teaching happens, but about nine in ten useful inventions are never learned by anyone except their inventor. The evidence

  5. 4

    Knowledge outlives its maker and builds up testing

    Being tested now: a thousand-year study asks whether what people learn survives their deaths, and whether tools matter more as the centuries pass.

  6. 5

    Open-ended growth not yet

    New things built on old things, again and again, with no ceiling.

  7. 6

    Autonomy not yet

    Minds that set their own goals, beyond the drives nature gives them.

  8. 7

    Bob not yet

    A mind with autonomy that builds on what it and others learned: the mission accomplished.

The later levels are much harder than the early ones, so this is a map, not a countdown. A level moves only after a finished study whose key numbers have been recomputed independently; a level can also move back. 100% means Bob exists.

The question

Can civilization emerge from nature alone, without anyone writing its history? And if it cannot yet, what exactly stops it? ACL answers that one step at a time, with controlled experiments on hundreds of simulated worlds.

What it has found so far
