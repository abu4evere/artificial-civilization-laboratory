What ACL is

The Artificial Civilization Laboratory is an artificial civilization simulation: an agent-based world of hunter-gatherers in which nature is programmed and culture is not. People have bodies, hunger, cold, fear, love, grief, curiosity and memory. Stone can be struck, wood floats, seeds grow. There is no list of inventions, no reward for being clever and no script. Whatever they come to make, believe or pass on, they find for themselves, and they learn their habits by trial and error in their own lifetimes.

ACL uses no large language models. A model trained on human writing already knows what a village, a trade and a law are; if its agents built one, nobody could tell whether civilization emerged or was remembered.

The question

Can civilization emerge from nature alone, without anyone writing its history? And if it cannot yet, what exactly stops it? ACL answers that one step at a time, with controlled experiments on hundreds of simulated worlds.

How it is tested

Latest experiment · October 2026

A kinder way to forget made things worse

People could remember how to make only 12 things, so new inventions were pushed out within a season. In MR1 they instead forgot only what they had not used for two years. New inventions were kept at first, but people ended up remembering far less, and fewer inventions were passed on: about 8 in 100 instead of 11 in 100. By the rule fixed before the run, the idea was rejected, and the wrong prediction stays in the record.

Then: GR1 protected only a new invention's first season. It looked like a small gain, but on twice as many new worlds (GR2) it made no difference, so it was not adopted. A read-only study (LU1) found why: only about 6 in 100 lessons about useful inventions are taken up. Most fail because a learner's chance of taking one is usually below one in three, and a third are about things the learner already knows.
