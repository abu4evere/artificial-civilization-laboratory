Artificial Civilization Laboratory (ACL)

Twenty people wake up on a set of islands knowing nothing. No tools, no fire, no language, no rules. Nobody writes their history. What do they build?

See the code and records The book

What ACL is

The Artificial Civilization Laboratory is an artificial civilization simulation: an agent-based world of hunter-gatherers in which nature is programmed and culture is not. People have bodies, hunger, cold, fear, love, grief, curiosity and memory. Stone can be struck, wood floats, seeds grow. There is no list of inventions, no reward for being clever and no script. Whatever they come to make, believe or pass on, they find for themselves, and they learn their habits by trial and error in their own lifetimes.

ACL uses no large language models. A model trained on human writing already knows what a village, a trade and a law are; if its agents built one, nobody could tell whether civilization emerged or was remembered.

The question

Can civilization emerge from nature alone, without anyone writing its history? And if it cannot yet, what exactly stops it? ACL answers that one step at a time, with controlled experiments on hundreds of simulated worlds.

What it has found so far

Inventions die with their inventor

About nine in ten useful inventions are never learned by anyone else. Most are forgotten within a season.

The book

The World That Nobody Wrote, Part One: How I Tried to Build a Civilization Instead of Simulating One tells the story of the project: how the worlds were built, what their people did, and what was predicted wrong. As a Kindle ebook and a paperback on Amazon.
