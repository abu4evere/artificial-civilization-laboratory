# The World That Nobody Wrote — new spine (outline, DRAFT for the creator's approval)

**Status:** proposal only. The current book (112 chapters, about 43,000 words, eight Parts) is unchanged and will be
tagged `book-v1` before any rewriting starts.

## The idea in one sentence

*"I set out to see whether a civilization could appear if no one wrote it. Here is what that attempt has cost, what it
has shown, and where it stands now."*

The experiments become the chapters of that struggle instead of its table of contents. Their full detail moves to an
appendix, **The Record**, so nothing that makes ACL credible is lost.

## What changes, in short

| today | new |
|---|---|
| organised by kind of content (Dream, Philosophy, Mistakes, Debates, Discoveries, Lives, Science, Future) | organised as **one story in eight movements**, in the order things happened |
| Part III, the experiment chronicle, is over half the book; every study is narrated in full | each movement tells its studies as **one struggle**; the study-by-study detail goes to **The Record** |
| people appear mostly in Part VI ("Lives") | **the people lead each movement**; findings explain their lives |
| the rules of method have their own Part | each rule appears **at the moment a failure bought it** |
| 6 Creator's Notes; Chapter 0 not yet written | **Chapter 0 and one Creator's Note per movement**, in the creator's own words |
| "Discoveries" and "Debates" are separate Parts | each discovery sits where it was found; the open debates close the book |

## Rules for the new book (unchanged from today, plus two)

1. Every number still comes from the repository; each movement points to its studies in The Record.
2. Every person is a person who lived in a seeded world; every event in their stories is in the record.
3. **New:** what a person thought or felt is quoted only from the record (the mind reader, the brain scan, the
   historian): for example Riyei's "lonely" and "glad to be near Kibok", with seed, year and week. Nothing is imagined.
4. **New:** Creator's Notes are only the creator's own words. The assistant may ask questions and tidy wording, never
   write the substance. Where a note is not yet written, the book leaves a marked space, as it does today.
5. Failures and wrong predictions stay, told as part of the trying, not as scored items.

## The spine

### Prologue — Chapter 0: Why This Project Exists *(creator)*
The questions already in the manuscript: JARVIS and the bigger questions; when emergence became the thing; what came
before ACL and felt unsatisfying; where "culture is written by nobody" came from; why a world and not another AI
product; what a reader should take away.

### Movement I — The Dream *(from today's Chapters 1–12)*
The question; why existing simulators did not answer it; the founding rule and the vows that follow from it (no
teleology, no special inventions, an indifferent world, uses must be discovered). Condensed: the vows are told as
**promises the creator made to the worlds**, and the "known compromises" as the first honest admissions.
- **Creator's Note:** where did the founding rule come from, and what would breaking it have cost you?

### Movement II — The First Long Histories *(Chapters 13–18; Lives: 94 The Raft, 98 Two Peoples, 99 Ludol)*
The first worlds run for decades, then a thousand years. Things appear that nobody wrote: rafts, marks, feuds, two
peoples on neighbouring islands who never met in a thousand years. Then the **freeze**: after the first burst, nothing
new for centuries.
- **Faces:** the raft invented and forgotten at the same moment; the two islands; Kaileir and Ludol.
- **Creator's Note:** what did you feel watching the first thousand-year world, and then the freeze?

### Movement III — Why Novelty Dies *(Chapters 19–23; Lives: 95 Beimak, 96 Reibik, 97 The Autopsy, 100 Mouthir;
Discoveries 89 The Degenerative Loop, 90 The Memory Ceiling)*
Richer matter did not help. The autopsies: things were made once and lost; skills thinned as they passed down.
- **Faces:** **Beimak**, who made the only fired clay in a thousand years and never thought of it again; **Reibik**, whose
  watertight carrier travelled through four generations and changed in the telling; **Mouthir**, whose flake came out
  duller than the ancestor's.
- **Rules bought here:** autopsies before interventions; measurement must not touch the world.
- **Creator's Note:** the moment a child holding clay changed how you saw the problem.

### Movement IV — Winter, and the Tools That Barely Mattered *(Chapters 24–28, 39–52; Chapter 45 The People Who Do
Not Store; Discovery 91 A World That Does Not Need Tools)*
The crucible, freeing the hands, why nobody makes anything, winter and autumn, the winter ledger, three gates to
storing, who receives the food, the world without a lean season, the crop that feeds someone else, the land that would
not vary. One finding gathers them: **this world barely needs tools**; living hand to mouth works.
- **Faces:** the people who do not store; a child lost to winter (Chapter 32).
- **Creator's Note:** realising the world itself, not the people, might be the problem.

### Movement V — Learning to Look Ahead *(Chapters 29–33; Discoveries 84, 92)*
Feeling winter in autumn, a faint lesson, whose life a store saves, the first planners, and a lesson that undoes itself.
- **Faces:** the first planners; the child lost to winter, if better placed here.
- **Creator's Note:** what "looking ahead" means to you, and why it was so hard to give them honestly.

### Movement VI — Making Behaviour Spread *(Chapters 34–38; Discoveries 85, 88)*
Stories of a hard season, the planners seen and respected and not copied, following those who do well, and the
replication: **what spreads is a habit, not a plan**.
- **Rules bought here:** frozen predictions; controls and many worlds; the experimenter as the likeliest cheater.
- **Creator's Note:** the moment you almost relaxed the method, and why you did not.

### Movement VII — Why Worlds Die *(Chapters 53–72)*
The worlds that grew old; if everyone could find everyone; couples apart half the time; a home that did not move and
the hunger it caused; a camp that moves; how many worlds a test needs; nowhere to go and no time to go there; months,
not years; one band alone; and the world-size experiment (WS1, running now). The thread, told as one search: **people
could not find each other.**
- **Faces (with real mind data):** **Kibok and Riyei**, brother and sister, the last two people of seed 1112, in their
  own recorded thoughts; **Vadumis**, thirty-six and single, whose habit of courting was still zero; **Thatai**, worried
  about her child Yeigol, who died of starvation while we watched. The brain scan enters the book here.
- **Rules bought here:** power before design; a sham in every test; corrections in the open (the HF1 stale cap, the F2
  misreading, the G1/S1 names).
- **Creator's Note:** what it was like to keep finding that the world was too small, and the moment you said so.

### Movement VIII — Where We Stand, and What Still Feels Impossible *(Part IV debates 73–81, Part VII 101–108,
Part VIII 109–112; the next-phase plan)*
What has been shown: culture appears without being written. What has not: culture that keeps going and builds on
itself. The open debates as the questions still argued over. The rules as the price the project paid. The road ahead:
survival first, then learning by watching, then seasons from real environments, then belonging.
- **Creator's Note:** why keep going.

## How it would be done (each step for the creator's approval)

1. Approve this outline (changes welcome).
2. Tag the current book `book-v1`.
3. **Pilot: rewrite Movement VII first** (its data is freshest, and the mind viewer can supply real thoughts for Kibok,
   Riyei, Vadumis and Thatai), together with The Record's entries for its studies.
4. The creator reads it; if the tone is right, the other movements follow, one at a time.
5. Creator's Notes: the assistant sends the questions for each movement; the creator answers in their own words (Uzbek
   is fine); the assistant shapes them lightly and shows the result before it goes in.
6. From then on, new results go into The Record at once, and into the story only when they change the story.
