# Voice pilot: Chapter 11 (PILOT: the voice direction is accepted; this chapter is not approved for publication)

**Rules used**
1. Short sentences, one idea each. Plain words.
2. Questions that lead the reader, where the project really asked them.
3. **The book's "I" is the creator, and only says what the creator has said.** Every first-person line below is marked
   with its source: your interview answers in `drafts/creators_note.md` (Q1–Q11) or your own decisions. Nothing about
   your feelings is invented.
4. Every rule, number and limit in the original stays. Nothing is dropped.

## Now

> This is the working form of the founding rule, and the question asked before any new system is added (method rule
> M7): does this create a condition that people can use, or does it quietly script the social result?
>
> Rather than asking how to create trade, ask what conditions make exchange useful. Rather than asking how to create
> government, ask what pressures make coordination valuable. Rather than asking how to make people fill vessels with
> water, ask what in the world could ever show them that a vessel holds water.
>
> **What it prevents.** Intervening on the wrong side of the line. When something does not emerge, the temptation is
> always to add it. This principle says: add the condition, never the outcome, and if the condition is not enough, find
> out why before adding anything else.
