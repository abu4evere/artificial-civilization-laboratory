# Prologue fix (DRAFT for the creator; nothing applied)

**Why.** The prologue of Part One states a cause more firmly than the record does. Your own note (Chapter 103) says: "His
behavior alone doesn't tell me exactly why he never filled it ... The record gives me the puzzle, not automatically the
explanation."

**What the record measures** (`experiments/E6_novelty/autopsy.md`, lines 40–46 and 61):
- Reibik stood at fresh water holding the vessel for 102 adult-weeks and never filled it.
- Filling it was never among his top four options; he chose foraging, giving food, talking and going to people.
- In ACL's rules, the pull to fill something grows with how much thirst a person has known. Reibik had known almost none,
  and the pull was about 0.04, against 0.3–1 for ordinary choices.
- Thirsty people elsewhere in that world had no vessel.

**What it does not show:** what Reibik "wondered", or that a low pull is the whole reason. That is a reading of the
mechanism, not something the record proves.

## The paragraph now (Part One, the prologue's fifth paragraph)

> It was not that Reibik could not. It was that nothing inside ever asked. Reibik had lived beside water and had almost
> never been thirsty, and someone who has never been thirsty does not wonder what a hollow thing might be for. Out on the
> dry land, meanwhile, other people went thirsty. They had never seen a vessel. Reibik had never known thirst.

## Proposed replacement

> It was not that Reibik could not. The record shows what Reibik weighed instead: foraging, giving food, talking, going to
> find people. Filling the vessel never came near the top. In that world, the urge to fill a thing grows with how much
> thirst a person has known, and Reibik had lived beside water and had known almost none; the urge was a small fraction
> of an ordinary one. Out on the dry land, meanwhile, other people went thirsty. They had never seen a vessel.

The next line stays as it is (it states only what was observed):

> The person holding the vessel was never thirsty. The thirsty people had no vessel.

Optional, one sentence added after it, in the spirit of your note:

> Whether that small urge is the whole reason, the record cannot say. It gives the puzzle, not the answer.

## What changes
- Removed: "nothing inside ever asked" and "someone who has never been thirsty does not wonder ..." (claims about an inner
  life and a general law that the record does not hold), and "Reibik had never known thirst" (the record says almost
  none).
- Added: what was measured (his choices; the urge and why it was small), and, optionally, the limit of what it shows.
- Chapter 103 in both books already gives the numbers (0.04 against 0.3–1); it is left as it is.
