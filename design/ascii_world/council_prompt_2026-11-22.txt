Design question only (how a viewer looks; nothing about ACL's science). I am building a viewer that draws ACL's hunter-gatherer world as text characters, after an Instagram reel of a solo indie game (@ztryfu, inspired by @ansenjeo's ASCII game) where a 3D night scene is turned into text. In the reel every screen cell is one character (about 140 columns across), black between characters, each character coloured by the picture under it: an isometric night village, dark blue-grey ground with noisy grass characters, a building whose roof edges are bright outlines, warm orange lit arched windows, a big round teal tree, a purple fence with small yellow lamps, one walking figure; it reads like a real shaded 3D picture made of text. My pipeline now: Canvas2D draws an isometric terraced tile world at half resolution (biome colours, trees as shaded circle clusters, people as small rectangle figures with legs that swing, fires, heaps) plus a second map in which every pixel says what it is (grass, leaf, cliff side, head, body, fire, animal, sea); a WebGL2 shader cuts the screen into 8x12 px cells, takes 5 samples per cell, keeps the most important kind, lights it (night tint and desaturation, a fire light map, people keep their colour), and picks one of 8 characters from a row for that kind by brightness (grass ' .,,;;"', leaves ' .:+o%&8', head ' .:oO0@@', body ' .:;!|IY', cliff ' .::;=||'), sea ripples as ~ and - with a moon streak, rain as /, a vignette and a faint glow; the camera follows one person closely. It is readable now, but it still looks more like a roguelike map than the reel's 'shaded picture made of text'. Question: what are the 5 most effective, concrete changes to get the reel's look with this pipeline (for example edge detection for outline characters, how to choose characters, colour grading, glow, palette, cell size, dithering, lighting, what to draw in the picture itself)? Short and practical, please; say which one matters most.
