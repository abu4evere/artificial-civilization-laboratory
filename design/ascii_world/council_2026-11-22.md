# Council on the text-style viewer (22 Nov 2026), design only

The creator: "ask council if u need help with it". The same question (`council_prompt_2026-11-22.txt`) went to GPT and
Gemini, each in a new chat. Opinions, not sign-off. (Summaries in our words, with their rankings.)

## Gemini's opinion (not sign-off)
1. **Directional edge characters (matters most):** a Sobel filter on the kind map and/or brightness; where an edge
   crosses a threshold, replace the kind's character with | - / \ by the gradient's angle, and brighten that cell by
   about 20–30%. Roguelikes draw shapes inside cells; the reel replaces boundaries with line characters.
2. **Grade hue apart from value:** plain tint and desaturation crush the night to grey-blue; keep shadow saturation at
   40–60%, shadows toward indigo, highlights toward cyan or amber; fire on a tight red → orange → yellow-white ramp.
3. **Ordered dithering** (4 × 4 Bayer, about half a character step) before picking the character, so smooth gradients
   do not become bands of one character.
4. **Bloom on bright light only**, many taps wide, added on top; keep the gaps between characters pure black.
5. **Shade the picture itself:** key light and cast shadows in Canvas2D (top-left rims, dark ground under trees and
   feet); character ramps sorted by ink coverage; slightly smaller cells (6 × 10 or 7 × 11) for about 140–160 columns.

## GPT's opinion (not sign-off)
1. **Characters from the picture, not from the object (most important):** brightness, edge strength and edge direction
   pick the character (flat dark: space . ; horizontal edge _ - =; vertical | ! I; diagonal / \; dense bright # % @;
   soft texture : ; +). The kind map only sets importance (keeps small people), not the alphabet: object alphabets are
   what make it look like a roguelike.
2. **Make the Canvas2D scene look like a small 3D render:** dark sides, lighter tops, directional moonlight on tree
   crowns, strong contact shadows, bright rims, distance fading; one fixed light direction. Test: blur the picture;
   if it still looks 3D, the text will.
3. **A restricted night palette:** most of the world blue-grey and desaturated; orange only for fires, lamps, windows;
   teal and purple sparingly; deep shadows almost black.
4. **Edge-aware sampling and subtle dithering:** more samples per cell (3 × 3: average for fill, maximum for edges);
   dithering anchored to the world, not the screen, to avoid shimmer; 130–160 columns.
5. **Selective bloom and composition:** bloom only the brightest; pull the camera back so a person sits inside a larger
   scene with a focal point (a fire, a tree) and foreground silhouettes. Quick test: ignore the kind map and convert by
   brightness and edges only.

## Where they agree and differ (the assistant, as council)
- **Agree:** edges as line characters are the biggest lever; better shading in the picture itself; pure black gaps
  with glow only on real lights; ordered dithering; about 140–160 columns.
- **Differ:** GPT would drop the per-kind alphabets (the "clearer" version the creator asked for an hour earlier);
  Gemini keeps them and adds edges. Colour: GPT wants a restricted grey-blue world with orange reserved for fire;
  Gemini wants richer saturated shadows. Camera: GPT says pull back.
- **Decision (the assistant, design only):** two styles with a switch. "Reel" (default): characters from brightness
  and edges, kinds only for importance, as GPT says; edges and dithering as both say. "Clear": the per-kind characters
  with the key, plus the edges (Gemini's version). Night grade between the two: grey-blue but with indigo shadows; fire
  the only warm light. The camera pulls back a little (6 tiles across).
