Design question (how ACL is shown on screen; nothing about its science or rules). ACL is an agent-based world of hunter-gatherers where nature is programmed and culture is written by nobody: the creator writes only the laws (bodies, hunger, tiredness, seasons, weather, plants and animals, fire, materials, making things, talk, kinship), never the history; everything people do, learn, make and pass on emerges by itself. The world: 40 x 40 tiles of islands with real height and biomes (sea, beach, plains, forest, hills, mountains, snow, rivers), about 20 to 40 people at a time over many generations, one step per week, animals (rabbit, deer, bison, bear, fish), fires, heaps and stores, crops, named bands, a chronicle of firsts (for example 'the first fire made by people'), each person's current thought, and a scan of their small learning brain. The long-term goal is 'Bob': an autonomous mind that builds on what it and others learned; the brain is now built in a small lab world and ACL is its final exam. ACL's people have no faces, and the viewer avoids drawing anything the world does not contain (no houses, no invented tools). Today there are: (a) a dark 'observatory' web page: a relief map from real height and biome, people as small coloured marks by band, charts, a chronicle, and a click on anyone shows their state and a brain scan; (b) an older pixel-art story page; (c) a new experiment drawing the world in text characters after a viral indie game reel, which the creator now feels does not fit ACL. The audience: the creator watching their world day to day, the research record, and later a public website and short videos. Question: what visual design best fits ACL's spirit and purpose? Please give one top recommendation (a clear direction, not a menu), why it fits ACL specifically, what it looks like in concrete terms (view and camera, palette, how land, people, bands, fire, birth and death and the passing of generations are shown, type, motion), what it must never do (anything that would misrepresent a world nobody wrote), and one runner-up. About 300 words, please.
