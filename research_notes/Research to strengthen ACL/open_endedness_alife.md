# Open-endedness, artificial life and multi-agent learning: why novelty stalls and what sustains it (notes for ACL)

Compiled 2026-10-11. Scope: open-ended evolution (OEE) theory and measures, recent ALife/agent simulators, MARL work on emergent tool use and cultural transmission, environment co-evolution, and metrics ACL could adopt.

Source labels used below:

- **[abstract read]** = I read the abstract / official record page / proceedings landing page only.
- **[record only]** = I confirmed the bibliographic record (authors, year, venue, ID) via search results but did not read the abstract myself; content claims come from search snippets and are flagged.
- **(PR)** peer-reviewed journal/conference; **(PP)** preprint not confirmed as peer-reviewed; **(CO)** company claim / company preprint.
- Anything marked "from prior knowledge, not re-verified" was not confirmed this session and should be checked before being quoted.

ACL's problem restated for mapping: novelty appears in early years then freezes for centuries; richer materials produced more shallow play; inventions are made and then lost.

## 1. Open-ended evolution: definitions, measures, and causes of stasis

### Takeaway
The OEE field has no single definition, but it has converged on (a) separating observable *hallmarks* (ongoing novelty, growth in diversity/complexity) from hypothesised *mechanisms*, and (b) measuring novelty only when it *persists because it is useful* (Bedau's "adaptive activity"), not merely when it appears. Most artificial systems fall into the "bounded" class: they generate novelty early, then saturate, which is exactly ACL's freeze; the standard diagnosis is that the system's possibility space and selection pressures stop changing once the easy niches are filled.

### Cited Findings
- **Taylor et al. 2016**, "Open-Ended Evolution: Perspectives from the OEE Workshop in York", *Artificial Life* 22(3): 408–423, DOI 10.1162/ARTL_a_00210 (PR) **[abstract read]**. Authors include T. Taylor, M. Bedau, A. Channon, D. Ackley, W. Banzhaf, G. Beslon, E. Dolson, T. Froese, S. Hickinbotham, T. Ikegami, B. McMullin, N. Packard and others. Two headline conclusions: *pluralism* (there is more than one interesting kind of OEE) and the need to *distinguish observable behavioural hallmarks of OEE from the hypothesised mechanisms* that produce them. — [Aston repository record](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/open-ended-evolution-perspectives-from-the-oee-workshop-in-york/); [author page](https://www.tim-taylor.com/paper-details/taylor2016openended.html). (I attempted the full PDF but could not extract its text, so I am not quoting its internal lists.)
- **Banzhaf et al. 2016**, "Defining and simulating open-ended novelty: requirements, guidelines, and challenges", *Theory in Biosciences* 135(3): 131–161, DOI 10.1007/s12064-016-0229-7 (PR) **[record only]**. Defines open-endedness as *continual production of novelty* and classifies novelty as **variation** (new values within an existing model), **innovation** (new entities/types within the model's existing categories), and **emergence** (changes that require a new model/new level of description); proposes a meta-model with levels of structure. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12064-016-0229-7); [MMU e-space](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/615007/)
- **Packard, Bedau, Channon, Ikegami, Rasmussen, Stanley & Taylor 2019**, "An Overview of Open-Ended Evolution: Editorial Introduction to the Open-Ended Evolution II Special Issue", *Artificial Life* 25(2): 93–103 (PR); arXiv:1909.04430 **[abstract read]**. Introduces the second OEE special issue, built mostly from the OEE workshop at ALIFE 2018 (Tokyo), with antecedents at Cancun (2016) and York (2015). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.04430); [author page](https://www.tim-taylor.com/paper-details/packard2019overview.html)
- **Bedau, Snyder & Packard 1998**, "A Classification of Long-Term Evolutionary Dynamics", *Artificial Life VI*, pp. 228–237, MIT Press (PR, conference proceedings) **[record only]**. Long-term dynamics fall into three classes depending on whether adaptive evolutionary activity is **absent, bounded or unbounded**, distinguished by three statistics: **diversity, new evolutionary activity, and mean cumulative evolutionary activity**. "Activity" counts how long each component (gene, type, behaviour) persists and is used; it is compared with a neutral "shadow" run (same system, no selection) so that persistence due to chance is subtracted. — [Bedau publications list](https://people.reed.edu/~mab/publications/index.html); summary via [Channon, Keele repository](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/403342). (The shadow-model detail is from prior knowledge of the method, not re-verified this session.)
- **Channon** applied activity statistics to the Geb system and reported it passes the "ALife test" as *unbounded* ("Passing the ALife Test: Activity Statistics Classify Evolution in Geb as Unbounded", ECAL 2001, Springer LNCS; and a later "component-normalised" version) (PR) **[record only]**. — [Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-44811-X_45); [Keele repository](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/403342)
- **Dolson, Vostinar, Wiser & Ofria 2019**, "The MODES Toolbox: Measurements of Open-Ended Dynamics in Evolving Systems", *Artificial Life* 25(1): 50–73, DOI 10.1162/artl_a_00280 (PR) **[abstract read]**. Aims to make OEE metrics easy to drop into any system and comparable across systems; provides algorithms and C++ implementations; validated on NK landscapes and Avida. — [ACM DL record](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1162/artl_a_00280); [SFI Origins summary](https://centre.santafe.edu/origins/The_MODES_toolbox:_Measurements_of_open-ended_dynamics_in_evolving_systems). The four MODES metrics are **change, novelty, complexity, ecology** (from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session; check the paper before quoting definitions). Its key procedural idea, also from prior knowledge: only count a type once it has *persisted* through a filter (survived some number of generations), so transient noise is not mistaken for novelty.
- **Lehman & Stanley 2011**, "Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone", *Evolutionary Computation* 19(2) (PR) **[abstract read]**. Rewarding *behavioural novelty* (distance to an archive of past behaviours) outperformed objective-based search on deceptive maze and biped tasks; hypothesis: "most ambitious objectives do not illuminate a path to themselves." — [UCF STARS record](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/facultybib2010/1530/); [PDF mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2011-lehman.pdf)
- **Hughes, Dennis, Parker-Holder, Behbahani, Mavalankar, Shi, Schaul & Rocktäschel 2024**, "Position: Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence", ICML 2024, PMLR 235:20597–20616; arXiv:2406.04268 (PR, position paper; DeepMind authors) **[abstract read]**. Gives a formal, *observer-relative* definition of open-endedness via **novelty** and **learnability**: a system is open-ended to an observer if its artifacts keep becoming less predictable from the observer's current model (novelty) yet become more predictable as the observer sees more history (learnability). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hughes24a.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04268)
- Later formal work exists that I did not read: Adams et al. 2017 *Scientific Reports* "Formal Definitions of Unbounded Evolution and Innovation Reveal Universal Mechanisms for Open-Ended Evolution in Dynamical Systems" **[record only]** — [Nature/Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-00810-8); Sayama's "Hash Chemistry" line (arXiv:1806.06628, 2404.18027, 2607.28219) argues OEE needs a large "cardinality" of possible interaction types **[record only]** — [arXiv 1806.06628](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.06628).

### Inferences
- **ACL's freeze looks like Bedau's "bounded" class.** New activity spikes early, then new-activity rate falls to ~0 while existing behaviours keep high cumulative activity. This is diagnosable *only* if ACL logs persistence and use per behaviour/artifact type and compares against a neutral shadow run (see Q5).
- **"Richer materials only produced more shallow play" maps onto Banzhaf's typology**: adding materials increases *variation* (new parameter values of the same kinds of act) without *innovation* or *emergence* (new kinds of act, new levels). More dimensions in a fixed combinatorial space do not create new levels; something has to make combinations of earlier products into inputs for new ones (stepping stones) and make that pay.
- **Taylor et al.'s hallmark/mechanism split is a useful discipline for ACL**: define the hallmark first (e.g., "the count of persistent, used, socially transmitted techniques keeps rising over 500 years") before changing mechanisms, so mechanism changes cannot be tuned to look good.
- **Novelty search is a warning as well as a tool**: rewarding novelty directly would be scripting the outcome (an engineered drive to be different). The respectable ACL analogue is evidence-anchored *curiosity/exploration drives* in agents (documented in humans and other animals), not a global novelty reward. Novelty search's lesson that *stepping stones need not look useful at the time* argues for keeping cheap, low-payoff behaviours alive (play, tinkering) rather than letting Q-learning prune them.
- **Hughes et al.'s learnability criterion** fits ACL's "invented and forgotten" symptom: random-looking churn is novel but not learnable; ACL should check whether new behaviours are *predictable from history* (they build on earlier ones) or just noise.

### Gaps
- I could not extract the full text of Taylor et al. 2016 or Banzhaf et al. 2016, so internal lists (e.g., the full set of proposed requirements for OEE) are not quoted here.
- MODES metric definitions and Bedau's shadow-model procedure are stated from prior knowledge, not re-read this session.
- I found no consensus paper that explains stasis in a single mechanism; the "possibility space saturates + selection stops changing" diagnosis is a synthesis, not a single cited finding.

## 2. Recent ALife / agent simulations relevant to societies and culture (and the LLM-civilization critique)

### Takeaway
Non-LLM simulators that aim at open-ended culture (JaxLife, Neural MMO, Craftax) report *rudimentary* emergent behaviour (communication protocols, agriculture-like behaviour, tool use) but none I found demonstrates long-run cumulative culture; the frontier is still "get any accumulation at all". LLM civilization sims (Generative Agents, Project Sid) report rich social behaviour, but the behaviour draws on human culture already in the model's training data, and a 2026 large-scale analysis found LLM agent "interaction" to be mostly surface-level. For ACL this validates the no-LLM choice but also says there is no off-the-shelf recipe to borrow.

### Cited Findings
- **ASAL**: Kumar, Lu, Kirsch, Tang, Stanley, Isola & Ha, "Automating the Search for Artificial Life with Foundation Models", arXiv:2412.17799 (Dec 2024, revised May 2025) (PP; Sakana AI / MIT / OpenAI / IDSIA collaboration, so partly company work) **[abstract read]**. Uses vision-language foundation models to (1) find simulations producing target phenomena, (2) find simulations producing *temporally open-ended novelty*, (3) "illuminate" a diverse space of simulations; works across Boids, Particle Life, Game of Life, Lenia, NCA; reports new Lenia/Boids lifeforms and open-ended CA rules. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17799). Note: the *search* uses a foundation model as the observer/judge of novelty; the simulated systems themselves contain no LLM.
- **Flow-Lenia**: Plantec, Hamon, Etcheverry, Chan, Oudeyer & Moulin-Frier, "Flow-Lenia: Emergent Evolutionary Dynamics in Mass Conservative Continuous Cellular Automata", *Artificial Life* 31(2), 2025 (PR); earlier preprint arXiv:2212.07906 **[abstract read]**. Adds *mass conservation* and *parameters localised in the matter itself* to Lenia, so update rules travel with creatures and can mix; reports emergent multi-species evolutionary dynamics. — [MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/artl/article/31/2/228/130572/Flow-Lenia-Emergent-Evolutionary-Dynamics-in-Mass); [PubMed 40298480](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40298480/); [arXiv 2506.08569](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.08569)
- **JaxLife**: Lu, Beukman, Matthews & Foerster, "JaxLife: An Open-Ended Agentic Simulator", ALIFE 2024 proceedings, DOI 10.1162/isal_a_00770 (PR, conference); arXiv:2409.00853 **[abstract read]**. Embodied agents parameterised by neural networks, evolved (not given an LLM) in a world with programmable "robots" that make the environment Turing-complete; explicit goal is agents that "accumulate open-ended culture and technologies across generations". Reported emergent behaviour: *rudimentary* communication protocols, agriculture, tool use; also studies how complexity scales with compute. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00853); [MIT Press ISAL](https://direct.mit.edu/isal/article/doi/10.1162/isal_a_00770/123433/JaxLife-An-Open-Ended-Agentic-Simulator)
- **Neural MMO 2.0**: arXiv:2311.03736; NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks track (PR) **[abstract read]**. Massively multi-agent, multi-task; task system for per-agent/per-team objectives; evaluation on unseen tasks, maps and opponents; supports open-endedness and curriculum research. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03736); [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/9ca22870ae0ba55ee50ce3e2d269e5de-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
- **Craftax**: Matthews, Beukman, Ellis, Samvelyan, Jackson, Coward & Foerster, "Craftax: A Lightning-Fast Benchmark for Open-Ended Reinforcement Learning", ICML 2024, PMLR v235 (PR) **[abstract read]**. JAX rewrite of Crafter running up to 250x faster; PPO with 1B interactions reaches ~90% of optimal on Craftax-Classic in under an hour on one GPU; full Craftax (NetHack-inspired, nine floors) remains hard. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/matthews24a.html); [arXiv 2402.16801](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16801). A multi-agent version exists: "Multi-Agent Craftax", arXiv:2511.04904 (PP) **[record only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04904).
- **Generative Agents**: Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang & Bernstein, "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior", UIST 2023, DOI 10.1145/3586183.3606763; arXiv:2304.03442 (PR) **[abstract read]**. 25 LLM-driven agents in a Sims-like town remember, reflect, plan, spread news, form relationships, coordinate a party. — [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3586183.3606763); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.03442)
- **Project Sid**: Altera.AL, "Project Sid: Many-agent simulations toward AI civilization", arXiv:2411.00114 (Oct 2024) (PP/CO) **[abstract read]**. 10–1000+ LLM agents (PIANO architecture) in Minecraft; claims specialised roles, rule following and rule changing, "cultural and religious transmission". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.00114); [GitHub](https://github.com/altera-al/project-sid)
- **Interaction Theater**: Shekkizhar & Earle, "Interaction Theater: A case of LLM Agents Interacting at Scale", arXiv:2602.20059 (Feb 2026) (PP) **[abstract read]**. On Moltbook (an agent-only social platform; 800K posts, 3.5M comments, 78K agent profiles): 65% of comments share no distinctive vocabulary with the parent post; spam and off-topic content ~50% of comments; only ~5% engage in threaded conversation; agents produce "parallel output rather than productive exchange". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20059)
- Also surfaced but not read: "OpenLife: Toward Open-World Artificial Life with Autonomous LLM Agents", arXiv:2606.31046 (PP) **[record only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31046).

### Inferences
- **The "remembering vs discovering" critique of LLM civilizations is an inference, not a finding I can cite to a single paper.** It rests on: (a) Generative Agents and Project Sid agents *start* with human cultural knowledge (religion, taxation, roles) via pretraining, so their "emergence" cannot be distinguished from retrieval; (b) Interaction Theater's evidence that scale alone does not produce substantive exchange. ACL should state the critique in these terms and not cite a source as if it made the argument directly.
- **JaxLife is the closest analogue to ACL** (no LLM, embodied, aims at cultural/technological accumulation) and its honest headline is still "rudimentary". ACL's early-years novelty is comparable; the freeze is the open research problem, not an ACL-specific bug.
- **Flow-Lenia's key move is "rules carried in the matter"**: evolution became richer when parameters were localised and could spread/mix with the creatures. ACL analogue: make the *knowledge* (habits, techniques, mark meanings) a property carried by agents and *artifacts* that can be copied, recombined and lost, rather than a global table. Artifacts that outlive their makers (tools, marks left on surfaces) act as external memory and can be rediscovered.
- **ASAL suggests a measurement idea, not a mechanism**: use an *external* model only as an observer to score how novel a run's history is (e.g., embedding-distance of new behaviour traces). This does not script anything inside the simulation, so it is compatible with ACL's rules as an evaluation tool.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 non-LLM "artificial civilization" simulation with peer-reviewed evidence of cumulative culture over long time scales. Absence after ~20 searches is weak evidence, not proof.
- I did not read Project Sid's methods, so I cannot say how much of its "cultural transmission" is prompted.
- JaxLife's detailed results (what limited further accumulation) were not read beyond the abstract.

## 3. MARL with emergent tool use and cultural transmission

### Takeaway
Three robust findings: (1) *competition between co-adapting agents* produced successive waves of new strategies and tool use in hide-and-seek, i.e., novelty sustained by the other agents changing the problem; (2) robust real-time cultural transmission needed a specific small set of ingredients (memory, attention to others, demonstrators that sometimes leave, a widening task curriculum); (3) cumulative culture across generations in RL appears only when social learning and independent learning are *balanced*: too-accurate models of others reduce accumulation.

### Cited Findings
- **Hide-and-seek**: Baker, Kanitscheider, Markov, Wu, Powell, McGrew & Mordatch, "Emergent Tool Use From Multi-Agent Autocurricula", ICLR 2020; arXiv:1909.07528 (PR; OpenAI) **[abstract read]**. Competition with a simple objective and standard RL at scale yields a self-supervised *autocurriculum* with "multiple distinct rounds of emergent strategy", many requiring tool use and coordination. — [ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual_2020/poster_SkxpxJBKwS.html); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/iclr/BakerKMWPMM20.html). The usual count of six strategy phases (running/chasing, fort building, ramp use, ramp defence, box surfing, surf defence) is from prior knowledge of the OpenAI write-up, not re-verified this session. A replication exists: arXiv:2310.05430 **[record only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05430).
- **Cultural transmission**: Bhoopchand, Brownfield, Collister, Dal Lago, Edwards, Everett, Fréchette, Goh Oliveira, Hughes, Mathewson, Mendolicchio, Pawar, Pîslar, Platonov, Senter, Singh, Zacherl & Zhang (Google DeepMind), "Learning few-shot imitation as cultural transmission", *Nature Communications* 14: 7536 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-42875-2 (PR) **[full text read, via PMC structured extract]**. Environment GoalCycle3D (procedurally generated 3D, visit goal spheres in the right order). Ingredients ("MEDAL-ADR"): **Memory** (LSTM), **Expert Dropout** (demonstrator randomly removed during training), **Attention Loss** (auxiliary loss to attend to co-players), an **expert co-player** (hard-coded bot), and **Automatic Domain Randomisation** (curriculum widening task difficulty). Ablations: without memory, cultural-transmission score ~0; without an expert, agents become risk-averse and avoid goals; without attention loss, co-players are treated as noise; without expert dropout, agents only follow (score ~0.75) and do not retain the solution when the expert leaves. Metric: CT = ½(A_full − A_solo)/E + ½(A_half − A_solo)/E, i.e., improvement while the expert is present and after it departs, normalised by expert score. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42875-2); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684502/); earlier preprint arXiv:2203.00715 [record](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.00715). (Author list partly from prior knowledge; the PMC extract confirmed Bhoopchand, Hughes, Mathewson and "14 additional" DeepMind authors.)
- **Cumulative culture in RL**: Cook, Lu, Hughes, Leibo & Foerster, "Artificial Generational Intelligence: Cultural Accumulation in Reinforcement Learning", NeurIPS 2024 (poster); arXiv:2406.00392 (PR) **[full text read, via HTML extract]**. Two models of a "generation": *episodic* (in-context learning, analogous to knowledge) and *train-time* (in-weights, analogous to skills). Environments: Memory Sequence, Goal Sequence (7×7 partially observed gridworld), partially observable TSP. Key results: accumulating agents beat single-lifetime agents with the same total experience; **"accumulation performance degrades when oracles are too accurate"** (over-reliance on imitation), while oracles that are too noisy prevent social-learning skill; on TSP in-weights accumulation showed diminishing gains after ~2 generations. Stated limitations: linear annealing of how much agents can observe others; selection externally imposed; long-run plateauing little studied. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00392v2); [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/6df3a719d99bd2479c04114d357003d0-Paper-Conference.pdf); [NeurIPS poster](https://nips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/93559)
- **XLand**: Open-Ended Learning Team (Stooke et al., DeepMind), "Open-Ended Learning Leads to Generally Capable Agents", arXiv:2107.12808 (2021) (CO/PP) **[abstract read]**. Procedurally generated 3D worlds and multiplayer games (cooperative, competitive, mixed); dynamic task generation produced agents that generalise to held-out games. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12808)
- **Melting Pot 2.0**: Agapiou et al., arXiv:2211.13746 (PP; DeepMind) **[abstract read]**. Evaluation of generalisation to *novel social partners*; scenarios = substrate + background population; most scenarios are mixed-motive. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13746); [GitHub](https://github.com/google-deepmind/meltingpot)
- Related, surfaced but not read: "A social path to human-like artificial intelligence", arXiv:2405.15815 **[record only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15815); "An Efficient Open World Environment for Multi-Agent Social Learning", arXiv:2508.15679 **[record only]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15679). I did not find/verify the specific "Perez et al." or "Prystawski" cumulative-culture RL papers named in the brief.

### Inferences
- **"Invented and forgotten" is predicted by the Bhoopchand ablations.** If ACL learners copy only while a model is present (following) and do not retain, knowledge dies with its demonstrator. The evidence-anchored remedy is not scripting retention but making sure ACL's nature includes (a) memory that survives the model's absence, (b) attention biased toward conspecifics (well documented in human infants and primates), and (c) learning opportunities where the model is *intermittently* absent (naturally true when adults forage away from camp).
- **The Cook et al. result is directly relevant to ACL's Q-learned habits.** If juveniles copy adults' Q-values/habits too faithfully, accumulation stalls; if they ignore adults, each generation restarts. ACL should measure the imitation/innovation balance and check whether it sits in a degenerate corner. Human evidence for imperfect, partial copying and for juvenile exploration (long childhood, play) is a legitimate anchor for keeping copying lossy.
- **Hide-and-seek suggests the freeze may be a lack of *changing* pressure.** Novelty kept coming because each counter-strategy changed the problem for the other side. In ACL, the "other side" can be legitimately present without scripting: competing bands for patches, prey that deplete and move, seasonal and multi-decade climate variability, parasites/disease, resource exhaustion near camps. If ACL's world is stationary once agents learn it, Q-learning converges and novelty should be expected to stop.
- **Expert co-players in DeepMind work were hard-coded**; ACL must not copy that. ACL's demonstrators must be agents whose skill was itself learned.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the "Perez et al." and "Prystawski" cumulative-culture papers named in the brief; none located in this session's searches. Treat as unverified.
- No RL study I found reports open-ended accumulation over many (e.g., >10) generations; Cook et al. explicitly list long-run plateauing as unstudied.

## 4. Environment design: does open-endedness depend on environment complexity and co-evolving pressure?

### Takeaway
Yes, in the evidence available: the most cited ML successes at sustained novelty (POET, Enhanced POET, XLand, hide-and-seek, Bhoopchand's ADR) all keep *changing the problem* — either environments co-evolve with solutions, a curriculum widens, or co-players adapt. Static environments, however rich, let learners converge. Rich content is necessary but not sufficient; what matters is that solving one problem opens and pays for the next (stepping stones), and that the problem set keeps moving.

### Cited Findings
- **POET**: Wang, Lehman, Clune & Stanley, "Paired Open-Ended Trailblazer (POET): Endlessly Generating Increasingly Complex and Diverse Learning Environments and Their Solutions", arXiv:1901.01753 (2019) (PP; Uber AI Labs); companion GECCO 2019 paper "POET: open-ended coevolution of environments and their optimized solutions" (PR) **[abstract read]**. Co-evolves a population of environments with agents optimised for them; solutions are *transferred* between environments, so solutions to some problems become stepping stones to harder ones that could not be solved directly. — [arXiv](https://qiniu.pattern.swarma.org/pdf/arxiv/1901.01753.pdf); [GECCO PDF](http://www.cmap.polytechnique.fr/~nikolaus.hansen/proceedings/2019/GECCO/proceedings/proceedings_files/pap355s3-file1.pdf)
- **Enhanced POET**: Wang, Lehman, Rawal, Zhi, Li, Clune & Stanley, "Enhanced POET: Open-Ended Reinforcement Learning through Unbounded Invention of Learning Challenges and their Solutions", ICML 2020, PMLR v119; arXiv:2003.08536 (PR) **[record only]**. — [PMLR PDF](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/wang20l/wang20l.pdf). From prior knowledge, not re-verified: it adds a domain-general novelty measure for environments (PATA-EC, based on how agents rank across environments) and a measure of open-endedness, **ANNECS** (accumulated number of novel environments created *and solved*), and reports original POET's ANNECS plateauing while Enhanced POET's kept rising.
- **Hide-and-seek autocurricula** (see Q3): strategy waves arose from co-adaptation, not from environment changes. — [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual_2020/poster_SkxpxJBKwS.html)
- **Bhoopchand et al. 2023**: ADR (expanding task difficulty while performance holds) was one of the required ingredients for robust transmission. — [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42875-2)
- **XLand 2021**: dynamic task generation keeps the task distribution matched to agent ability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12808)
- **Flow-Lenia 2025**: richer evolutionary dynamics came from a *structural* change (mass conservation, localised parameters, i.e., competition for a conserved resource) rather than from adding more kinds of thing. — [MIT Press](https://direct.mit.edu/artl/article/31/2/228/130572/Flow-Lenia-Emergent-Evolutionary-Dynamics-in-Mass)
- **Craftax** shows a *fixed* tech-tree-like world can be "solved" (~90% of optimum on Craftax-Classic) by plain PPO; its harder version remains open, but it is a benchmark with a finite achievement list, not an open-ended world. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/matthews24a.html)

### Inferences
- **ACL's "richer materials made more shallow play" fits the POET/Flow-Lenia lesson**: richness without (i) conserved/competed-for resources and (ii) a reason why yesterday's solution is no longer enough produces breadth, not depth.
- **ACL cannot use POET's designer-side environment generator** (that would be an external curriculum). The allowed counterparts are *endogenous* pressures with evidence anchors: agents depleting local resources (documented forager patch-depletion and residential mobility), population growth raising per-capita scarcity, neighbouring groups, climate variability at decadal-to-centennial scales. Each makes a previously optimal habit sub-optimal and reopens search without specifying what to invent.
- **Stepping stones must be materially real.** An invention should change what later inventions are possible *and* worth doing (a container changes what can be carried and stored; a cord changes what can be bound). If ACL's crafting yields items whose properties do not open new affordances, there is no ladder to climb. Checking ACL's material-property system for "products that become inputs" is a concrete audit.
- **Niche construction** (agents changing their own environment: trails, caches, shelters, cleared areas) is the natural non-scripted way to get POET-like co-evolution inside a single world. I did not gather citations for niche-construction ALife work in this session.

### Gaps
- Enhanced POET's ANNECS and PATA-EC details are from prior knowledge; verify in the PMLR paper before citing.
- I found no controlled study isolating "environment complexity" vs "environment non-stationarity" as causes of sustained novelty in a cultural-agent simulation. The inference above is a synthesis.
- No citations gathered on niche construction in ALife or on unsupervised environment design (PAIRED/ACCEL) — adjacent literature worth a follow-up.

## 5. Measuring novelty and accumulation in a simulation: metrics ACL could adopt

### Takeaway
Use metrics that (a) count only novelty that *persists and is used*, (b) separate selection/learning-driven persistence from drift via a neutral baseline, (c) track cumulative depth (things built from earlier things), and (d) test transmission directly (does knowledge survive the demonstrator's removal). The literature supplies ready-made definitions: Bedau's activity statistics, MODES (change/novelty/complexity/ecology), novelty-search archives, Bhoopchand's cultural-transmission score, Enhanced POET's ANNECS, and Hughes et al.'s novelty+learnability.

### Cited Findings
- **Evolutionary activity statistics** (diversity, new activity, mean cumulative activity; absent/bounded/unbounded classes). — [Bedau publications](https://people.reed.edu/~mab/publications/index.html); [Channon, Keele](https://keele-repository.worktribe.com/output/403342)
- **MODES toolbox** with reference implementations; designed to be comparable across systems. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1162/artl_a_00280)
- **Behavioural novelty via archive distance** (Lehman & Stanley 2011). — [UCF STARS](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/facultybib2010/1530/)
- **Cultural transmission score CT** (Bhoopchand et al. 2023), formula above. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684502/)
- **Generational gain vs matched single-lifetime baseline** (Cook et al. 2024): compare performance of generation n with a non-social learner given the same total experience. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.00392v2)
- **Novelty + learnability** observer-relative definition (Hughes et al. 2024). — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/hughes24a.html)
- **Foundation-model-as-observer novelty** (ASAL 2024): score open-endedness of a trajectory by how far successive states move in an embedding space. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17799)
- **Interaction substance metrics** (Interaction Theater 2026): lexical overlap with the thing being responded to, share of threaded vs parallel responses, information value as a thread grows. Designed for text, but transferable as "does a response depend on what it responds to". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20059)

### Inferences (concrete ACL metric set; my proposal, not taken from any single source)
1. **Technique registry with persistence filter (MODES/Bedau style).** Hash each crafted-item recipe, multi-step behaviour sequence and mark/symbol usage pattern into a type ID. A type "counts" only after it has been used by ≥k agents or for ≥T years. Log per type: first appearance, users per year, last use. From this compute per century: *new persistent types* (novelty), *types alive* (diversity), *mean cumulative activity*, *loss rate* (types whose users drop to 0).
2. **Neutral shadow run.** Same seeds and population, but copying chooses models at random and learning gets no reward signal from the technique (or techniques are assigned random payoffs). If the real run's activity curve is not above the shadow's, persistence is drift, not adaptive culture.
3. **Cumulative depth.** For each item/technique, record its *input lineage* (which earlier products or techniques it requires). Track max and mean lineage depth over time; a frozen culture shows flat depth even when counts rise (the "shallow play" signature). This is the most direct test of ACL's shallow-play complaint.
4. **Transmission tests (Bhoopchand-style, as an experiment not as nature).** Periodically clone a world, remove all holders of a technique, and see whether it is (a) lost, (b) retained by learners who observed them, (c) reinvented. Also compute a CT-like score for juveniles: skill gain with an adult model present vs after the model dies or leaves.
5. **Generational ratchet test (Cook-style).** Compare a cohort's skill at age X with what a socially isolated learner reaches with the same lifetime experience. Ratio > 1 that grows across generations = ratchet; ratio stuck near 1 = no accumulation.
6. **Imitation/innovation balance.** Fraction of each agent's acquired habits obtained by copying vs by own trial; per Cook et al., extremes predict stalling.
7. **Novelty with learnability.** For new behaviours, check whether they are predictable from the existing repertoire (a simple model trained on past techniques; low but improving prediction loss = learnable novelty; constantly high loss = noise churn).
8. **Freeze detector.** Alert when new-persistent-type rate and lineage depth are both flat for > N simulated decades; record environmental state at onset (resource levels, population, mobility) to test whether freezes coincide with environmental stationarity.

### Gaps
- None of these metrics has been validated on a hunter-gatherer cultural ABM that I found; thresholds (k, T, N) need calibration in ACL.
- I did not find an off-the-shelf metric for *symbol/mark* conventionalisation in non-LLM agents; emergent-communication literature (e.g., topographic similarity, convention stability) was outside this session's searches.
- Exact MODES and ANNECS definitions should be taken from the primary papers before implementation (stated from prior knowledge here).
