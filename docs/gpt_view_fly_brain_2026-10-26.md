# GPT's view: a fly brain in ACL? (26 October 2026). GPT's opinion, not sign-off.

Asked in the creator's main ChatGPT chat, at the creator's request, after the assistant described the open FlyWire
connectome (the complete adult fruit-fly brain wiring), its simple neuron-model simulation, and virtual fly bodies.

**Verdict: study the fly brain and borrow principles; do not put a whole FlyWire brain inside ACL's people.** "Borrow
principles of how brains learn, remember, navigate and decide; don't copy a fruit fly's entire evolved nervous system
and expect cumulative intelligence to appear." It may be more relevant to Bob than another material or season.

**What the research shows (GPT's summary; not checked by the assistant):**
- FlyWire (Dorkenwald et al., Nature 2024): about 139,000 neurons and 54.5 million synapses from one fly. An
  anatomical map, not a full description of what the neurons do.
- Shiu et al. (Nature 2024): a simple spiking model of it predicted parts of feeding and grooming circuits, some
  confirmed in experiments. Not a recreated fly mind.
- NeuroMechFly: virtual fly bodies whose walking controllers were trained by reinforcement learning, not run from the
  connectome.
- GPT also mentioned:
  - a company, Eon Systems, reporting in March 2026 a connectome-driven virtual fly (a company claim, with no
    long-term learning);
  - a June 2026 Nature paper linking the brain with the nerve cord.
  **Both are unverified by the assistant.**

**Why not a whole fly brain:**
- Wrong senses: fly vision, smell and taste, against ACL's abstract world.
- Wrong body and actions: wings and legs, against forage, make, teach and court.
- Wiring is not learning: plasticity, hormones and dynamics are missing.
- Cost: 40 people would need about 5.6 million simulated neurons, for 1,000 years.
- Deeper: a fly brain is evolution's answer for being a fly, not a blueprint for a cumulative mind. Flies learn, but
  have no cumulative culture. It would not touch M1's problem: knowledge must be found, kept, passed on and improved.

**What to borrow:**
1. The mushroom body: associative learning, linking patterns of circumstances to good or bad outcomes, with
   reward-driven plasticity and memories on several timescales. **GPT's first choice:** Bob's core requirement is
   becoming different through experience.
2. The central complex: an inner sense of direction and routes. Interesting, but not ACL's bottleneck now.
3. Memories with different roles and timescales, instead of one flat 12-slot list. Relevant to LS1, but **design
   nothing until LS1 shows where knowledge actually leaks.**

**Is it honest under ACL's rules?** Yes, if it gives a capacity to learn rather than preloaded knowledge.
- **Legitimate nature:** a general mechanism that learns associations; memory that strengthens useful associations and
  lets others fade; an inner sense of direction.
- **Unsuitable:** innate fly circuits with fly-specific responses.
- **Forbidden:** recipes, inventions, language, solutions.

**A possible future study (GPT's concept only, not approved):** brain-inspired learning, with three arms:
- **C:** today's mind;
- **F:** plus a small mushroom-body-like learner;
- **N:** the same extra memory and computation without the mechanism, so a gain cannot be just "more capacity".

The primary test: does earlier experience improve later decisions in related but new situations, and does the
advantage disappear when the mechanism is disabled? Not "does population rise". Also measure its costs (interference,
false associations).

**Order:**
1. Finish LS1.
2. Review the learning code against fly-brain principles, read-only.
3. Design the smallest change, with a causal hypothesis.
4. Build it on an isolated branch, with zero checks.
5. Freeze only with the creator.
6. Accept either result.

A better individual learner does not prove cumulative culture.

**To the creator (GPT):** "I like it because nature may already have discovered some very simple principles for
turning experience into knowledge… a fruit fly doesn't need our kind of civilization to learn." It ties back to the
creator's aeroplane analogy (feathers or lift).
