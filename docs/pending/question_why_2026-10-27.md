# Pending question for GPT and Gemini: how can ACL's people learn to ask "why"? (not yet sent; Chrome disconnected)

The creator's question (the same text to GPT and Gemini; Claude is council): how could ACL's people learn to ask "why"
themselves, that is, build cause-and-effect understanding, without us giving them answers?

## Facts from the code
- **(a)** People learn the worth of each **kind** of made thing from using it: `knowhow.judge_item` moves
  itemval[kind] towards felt = factor(item, use) − 1, faster when proved wrong. A brand-new kind starts at worth 0,
  whatever its properties.
- **(b)** Every made thing has 10 real physical properties (edge, reach, hard, contain, warm, float, tight, plastic,
  fuel, flex). They decide how much it helps each use (forage, hunt, wood, stone, fiber, fight, carry, cold). Nobody
  learns anything about properties.
- **(c)** There are expectations. A big gap between expected and felt becomes a "puzzle", which pulls attention and
  makes play pick materials related to the puzzling thing (random, not a controlled test).
- **(d)** Told claims can be checked against experience, through a record of who was right.

## The plan in plain words
1. expect (exists);
2. notice surprise (exists);
3. look for the cause (missing);
4. test by changing one thing (half: today it is random);
5. keep the reason as a rule about properties, such as "sharp things cut", and use it to guess the worth of new things
   (missing);
6. share reasons (missing).

## The proposed order
1. **A read-only audit first:** do puzzles today ever lead to a useful discovery? How much worth is lost because new
   kinds start at 0?
2. **Then one law at a time,** starting with learning the worth of properties rather than only of named kinds. For
   example: a person also keeps a value per property × use, updated from the same felt experience, and a new kind's
   starting worth is guessed from its properties.
