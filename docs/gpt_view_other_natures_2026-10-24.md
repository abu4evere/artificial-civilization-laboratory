# "Why should our laws apply to them?" The creator's question, GPT's view, and the mission (24 October 2026)

**The creator's question, in their words:** "It feels like we are trying to recreate humans inside ACL. Why should our
laws apply to them? Why do we have to create a world identical to ours? Is it because we humans developed and almost
know our history, and we do not know other ways to create a world where agents can act with autonomy and cognition?"

**The creator's statement of the mission (same day, in their words):** "Our mission is not to recreate Earth with the
same human history but to create a world with minimum laws and a period that produce an autonomous and cognitive mind."

**The assistant's answer (summary):** partly yes: Earth is the only known world that made minds, so we cannot tell its
essential features from its accidents; but anchoring to human evidence keeps the creator's hand out (no tuning), makes
results checkable, and may let Bob fit our world one day; the creator's own "planes don't copy birds; they found lift"
says the goal is the minimum conditions, not a copy; proposal: keep Earth-anchored ACL as the main track and later test
"other natures", one deep law changed at a time, frozen and never tuned.

## GPT's view (GPT's opinion and its reading of sources, in summary; sources not yet checked; not sign-off)

- **The real question:** are we discovering the conditions that make autonomous intelligence possible, or building an
  ever more accurate model of prehistoric humans and hoping intelligence appears? Different projects. Even a perfect
  model of foragers would not necessarily produce Bob.
- **Corrections to the assistant's answer:** human civilization is not our only example of learned culture (chimpanzees,
  New Caledonian crows, humpback whales, even bumblebees learn socially with very different bodies, so some capacities
  can be built many ways); scarcity, death, sexual reproduction and particular emotions are **hypotheses, not known
  necessities**; anchoring makes assumptions **accountable**, not automatically correct.
- **Revised list of candidate conditions:** strong candidates: consequences that make actions matter (not necessarily
  survival); a world with learnable regularities; chances to act and get feedback; memory that changes later behaviour;
  social transfer of information; variation and improvement; several individuals (for culture between people). Not
  established as necessary: scarcity, death and finite lives (an agent that never dies could still build on its own
  discoveries), two sexes, seasons and particular materials, the exact feelings, the 12-recipe memory. Evidence that
  culture can accumulate without survival pressure: Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008 (artificial languages grew more
  structured through repeated learning and passing on).
- **What science says:** cumulative culture = novelty, social learning, improvement, repeated over time (Mesoudi &
  Thornton 2018); **transmission fidelity** is the key lever (Lewis & Laland 2012), which fits ACL's own finding
  (CA1–CA3) that knowledge dies at the first hand-over; population structure and connectivity shape what is available
  (Derex & Mesoudi 2020); open-ended evolution needs more than long runs (Taylor 2015). The abstract chain: variation →
  learning → transmission → retention → improvement. No human body appears in it.
- **"Other natures" is sound only as counterfactual experiments, not as a search for a more successful world.** What
  replaces anchoring: internal consistency; causal isolation (one stated change; check what else moved); invariants kept;
  the **same** measures in every nature (persistent learning, transfer, retained value, improvement, transfer to new
  conditions), never redefining progress per world; reproducibility. One-change tests show context-dependent causes,
  not universal necessities.
- **First alternative to test: a world without seasons** (cleanest; holds food input at the annual average). Not yet:
  no death (population confounds), inherited knowledge (bypasses the learning bottleneck), one sex (changes kinship,
  partners and demography at once).
- **Risk of becoming a game: yes,** if natures are changed until agents look interesting and "intelligence" is redefined
  afterwards. Guards: each nature states its causal principle, what changes, what stays, what supports and what
  contradicts it, what is inconclusive; Earth-anchored nature stays the reference; an alternative's settings are never
  quietly folded into the baseline.
- **To the creator directly (GPT):** making their world more human-like is not the same as making their minds more
  capable. A less human world whose people remember, learn from each other, improve and keep improvements would be
  stronger evidence for Bob than a perfectly human world that never improves. And the missing ingredient may not be in
  the environment at all, but in the relationship between the individual and its experience.
- **Order (GPT):** finish BD3, run the v6 millennium study, then design one carefully isolated alternative nature
  (no seasons first).

## Source check by the assistant (25 October 2026, sleep mode; search results and abstracts)

- **Kirby, Cornish & Smith 2008**, PNAS 105(31):10681–10686 (https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0707835105): artificial
  languages passed through generations of learners became easier to learn and more structured, with no intentional
  design. **Confirms GPT** (cumulative structure without survival pressure).
- **Lewis & Laland 2012**, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 367(1599) (https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rstb.2012.0119):
  a simple model without changes in brain architecture; small changes in transmission fidelity can produce cumulative
  culture. **Confirms GPT.**
