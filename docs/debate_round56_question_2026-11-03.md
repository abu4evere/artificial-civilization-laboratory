## The assistant's argument
A table of habits (situation × action, a few dozen situations) cannot become Bob. Bob must:
1. **build on what he knows:** combine knowledge, and use a lesson in a new situation (generalisation);
2. **imagine:** predict outcomes before acting, which is how a mind can choose a future it wants;
3. **hold a goal for weeks** while meeting daily needs (internal state and memory).

Every known mind with its own drive and cumulative learning (corvids, apes, humans) has a learning, predicting,
generalising brain. It need not be a "neural network" in the usual AI sense. The smallest mechanism that does these
three jobs is preferred, so ACL stays explainable. It is necessary, but not sufficient: open actions and goal formation
are also needed.

**The risk the assistant sees:** neural learners are data-hungry. An ACL person makes one choice a week, about 2,000
in a life, so a neural brain could learn **worse** than the table within one lifetime. Possible answers:
- finer time steps;
- brains shaped by evolution (an inherited readiness to learn, not inherited knowledge);
- learning from watching others.

The cost: worlds that are perhaps 10–100 × slower, and harder to explain.
