## The question (sent as one paragraph)
Council round 65 (ACL, 5 November 2026): after HE1. HE1 (final, recomputed blind by GPT and Gemini; v13 / the law / sham, 32 new seeds, 200 years, years 100-200) tested the law 'habits learn only from experienced outcomes' (no look-ahead in the habit update, the existing setting discount 0). Not supported: harm. The hunger guard was raised: starvation deaths per 1,000 living-years 4.65 -> 7.64, ratio 1.643 [1.467, 1.842] (limit 1.25); deaths by killing rose from 4 to 216 (steal chosen about 9x as often); adult health -1.58 [-2.39, -0.81]; yet living-weeks +33% [22%, 45%], no extinction, M3 x7 and makes x6.1; remakes still 1 of 36,284 new kinds. So the habit look-ahead (r + 0.8 x max over next week's situation's 19 habit values; 32 coarse situations) does two jobs: LD1 showed it marks making down through a familiarity bias (rarely visited situations keep near-start values, so being well fed next week is valued least), and HE1 shows it also protects against hunger and against harming others. Earlier related tests (all frozen, not adopted): ES1, an expected look-ahead over the habit softmax, primary calibration worse, but making x5.2, population x1.21; ES2, a look-ahead by the person's real next choice (expected over the whole choice), calibration 40% better but one per-action guard (rest), making x3.0, population x1.16, no extinction; PB3's P+, memory with a two-week return, chains below no look-ahead. Questions: (1) Which look-ahead keeps the protective part and removes the familiarity bias, as a general law and not tuning: (a) next week's value = the person's own mean experienced r in that situation (no bootstrap, so no inflation, and a few visits suffice), (b) a max only over habits actually experienced in that situation, (c) revisit ES2's real-next-choice expectation with harm guards (starvation, killing) as primary, (d) a visit-weighted max, or (e) something else? (2) Should the next study instead go to remaking (value at invention: a new kind valued by its own observable results; a read-only audit first), since even 36,284 inventions gave one remake? Or run both? (3) Your estimate (0-100%) that your recommended next study is valid and informative. Please answer briefly, point by point.
