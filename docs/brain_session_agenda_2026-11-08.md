# Bob's brain: starting points for the first session with the creator (8 November 2026)

**What this is:** a one-page summary of what ACL already knows about its people's minds, and the questions only the
creator can choose between. **Nothing here is decided.** The brain work starts together (CLAUDE.md, "Direction").

## In plain words
The world (v17, frozen) now lets people make far more than they do. Their minds hold them back in three ways:
1. **They rarely make anything.** Grown people spend about 0.0014 of their weeks making (VW1).
2. **They never make an invention again.** Remaking within 5 years is 0.0127–0.0150 of new kinds (OD1). In CI1,
   every kind made again by its inventor was made again by a child.
3. **They don't keep a goal across weeks.** They choose afresh every week. A held aim alone did not help (CI1's IN arm).

## The strongest lead we already have, never followed up: the "habit trap" (PB3, 5 November)
- People's habits learn by a one-week look-ahead: q ← q + rate × (r + 0.8 × max next q − q) (`config.discount` 0.8).
- **PB3 found this look-ahead is what traps them:**
  - **without it, people made 6.6 times as much and invented 9 times as many new kinds;**
  - living-weeks were 1.166 [1.028, 1.321];
  - gather-then-make chains finished about 7.5 times as often.
- PB3's own reading: it is not a plain fault (the rule does what its comment says). So changing it is a mind law, to test
  with a council, a frozen test, a sham and a confirmatory run.
- **Remaking was still 0 even then** (0 of 11,348 kinds). Something else blocks it.

## Other mind results already in hand
- **PB2: memory steering** (choosing by recalling similar past weeks) worked: making-chains about ×6, living-weeks +28%.
  It was not better than simply removing the look-ahead.
- **PB1b:** **learning from others' weeks** is the big gain, especially for new kinds (+0.28).
- **AM1, AM2:** the habits that keep people alive pull them away from finishing chains.
- **VW1, WP1:** wanting the parts more does nothing.
- **LG (v15):** children can remake. That was the one remaking gain so far.

## From the research (background only)
- **Keeping a plan versus rethinking it** (Schut, Wooldridge & Parsons 2004): rethinking often wins in a fast-changing
  world, holding on in a slow one. Real agents decide when to reconsider.
- **Long practice** (Dallos 2021): real crafts take years of repetition. In ACL a recipe is learned in one moment, and
  practice does not make it better.
- Notes: `docs/research/goals_and_failures_2026-11-08.md`.

## Questions for the creator (one at a time is fine)
1. **Start with the habit trap?** Test PB3's lead as a mind law: how much next week's prospects should count, or a
   different way of looking ahead. It is the biggest effect we have ever seen on making.
2. **A goal that lasts:** should a person hold an aim across weeks and rethink it only when something changes or
   fails, as the research suggests? A plain held aim (IN) was not enough.
3. **Learning from mistakes:** when something fails, should it change what they try next, not only what they believe?
4. **Practice:** should doing a recipe again make it more reliable, so repetition is worth something?
5. **Remaking:** why is nothing ever made again, even with 9 times the inventions? This may need its own read-only look
   first.

Each would go the usual way: council, frozen plan, a test in a copy of v17 with a sham, blind recompute, then the safe
path.
