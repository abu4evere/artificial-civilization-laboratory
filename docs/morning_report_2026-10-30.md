   - 98% of lost knowledge is lost when people die;

2. **ACL people die too young.** A count on 6 test worlds: adults die at a mean age of 42; only 11% of adult deaths
   come at 60 or later. Real foragers' adults often reached their 60s and 70s.
3. **Your ageing problem, settled as you asked.** GPT wanted the small step (the !Kung, the toughest well-measured
   forager group); Gemini wanted the forager average and called the small step cherry-picking. Both then agreed to
   **test both side by side** (90% each). **AG1** compares today's nature, the small step, the forager average and a
   do-nothing control, over 1,000 years. Success means adult deaths by age come closer to real foragers', with no harm
   to survival. The choice is made by realism and safety, never by which one raises skill (that would be tuning).
   **Making it part of nature stays your decision**, after a confirmatory run.
4. **Research, as you asked:** new notes in `docs/research/`:
   - just *watching* someone make stone tools teaches little; being *taught*, and especially with words, teaches much
     more (Morgan et al. 2015);
   - forager children learn by play, watching and joining in; adults teach complex tool making in adolescence
     (Lew-Levy et al. 2017);
   - hunting skill peaks around 30–35 (Koster et al. 2020), and ACL adults die soon after that age today.
