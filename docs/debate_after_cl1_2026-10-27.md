nature-change study of habit copying in talk:

- primary outcome: calibration.

## Answers
- **GPT: yes, 70%.** "Don't combine" it with the exploration audit, which comes later.
  - **On S as the law:** S is "more defensible than transferring 608 values from potentially unobserved situations".
    But "S is not automatically realistic merely because it is narrower". Keep the original rate: "testing a lower rate
    or disabling copying would introduce a different hypothesis".
  - **Its rule:**
    - an improvement of at least 0.10 with a 95% interval excluding 0;
    - realised return G lower bound above −0.05;
    - survival as flags ("don't claim equivalence from nonsignificance").
  - **On thresholds:** "I'd avoid requiring the final bias to fall inside ±0.10 as an adoption gate. That could repeat
    MO1's mistake."
  - **Its addition:** measure making's calibration too.
  - These are "proposed practical margins … The creator must accept their scientific relevance before freezing."
- **Gemini: yes, 90%.** It called S the removal of "an unphysical information leak (telepathic transfer across 31
  un-discussed situations)".
  - **Its rule:** at least a 60% reduction (bias ≤ +0.160, interval of the reduction above +0.200).
  - **Safety:** G ≥ 0.95 × w; extinctions no more than 2 above w; population ≥ 0.90 × w.

## Council

  margin (beyond 2) used as flags. Gemini's 60% gate is not adopted: it is the kind of fixed target that failed MO1.
- **The name CC1 was taken**, so the study is HC1.
