# How forager children come to make and use tools: an evidence review (draft, 26 October 2026)

**Why:** LS1's frozen branch ("Q3's largest share is chosen"; `experiments/LS1_where_knowledge_goes/findings.md`): each
generation does not use what it could make. Next: an evidence review of how forager children come to make and use tools
(apprenticeship, necessity, worth), **before any change to nature.** Written in sleep mode: a draft for the creator,
not a plan. Nothing is frozen or changed.

**Sources** are from the assistant's knowledge. Titles, years and claims must be checked against the papers before any
use (marked "to check").

## 1. What LS1 found in ACL

- People **can** make (feasible rose to 0.80 of adult weeks) and **weigh** making (0.81 of those), but **choose** it
  about 1 time in 800 after year 100 (1 in 40 at the start). Foraging wins 0.68 of the time.
- Almost every tool used was made by its user (0.98 in decades 2–4). Only 0.013 were made by someone else.
- After losing their last forage tool, people had one again within a year 0.59 of the time at the start, and 0.07
  after year 400.
- Crafting skill at 16 falls by cohort (0.45 → 0.04), with practice (39 → 2.5 weeks).
- Children take up 0.41, then 0.27–0.29, of their carers' useful recipes.
- Of lessons taken, the share later made falls from 0.34 to 0.04.

## 2. What the forager evidence says (to check)

1. **Children learn by a mix of watching, play, taking part, access to objects, imitation and teaching,** in
   proportions that change with age and society. Teaching is often brief, a gesture or a correction, but caregivers
   also give tools and arrange learning, even for infants. (Lew-Levy et al. 2017, *Human Nature*, 58 publications
   reviewed; Hewlett et al. 2011, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*; Hewlett & Roulette 2016, Aka caregivers.) *Corrected
   after GPT's review: the first draft said "mostly by watching", which overstates it.*
2. **Children get real tools early.** In many forager groups, toddlers handle knives, digging sticks and small bows.
   Adults rarely stop them. (Lancy, on "playing with knives"; Hadza boys with small bows, Crittenden and others.)
3. **They also get child-sized and well-made tools, made or given by others,** before they can make them (Gidra
   children given well-made bows: "opportunity scaffolding", Lew-Levy et al. 2020; 434 observations of children's
   miniature and adult-like tools in 54 forager societies, Lew-Levy et al. 2022). Parents or older children often make
   the first ones, and the child later remakes and repairs them. (Riede et al. 2018 on play objects; Lew-Levy et al.
   2020 on child-to-child teaching among Hadza and BaYaka.)
4. **Learning happens in mixed-age playgroups.** Older children are the main models after infancy. Early learning
   comes from parents, and later learning from others. (Hewlett et al. 2011; Lew-Levy et al. 2020.)
5. **Skill pays off late.** Hunting returns rise into the 30s. Children's production is low but subsidised by adults,
   so practice continues even though it doesn't pay yet. (Gurven, Kaplan & Gutierrez 2006; Kaplan et al. 2000, the
   embodied-capital view.)
6. **Necessity and worth are visible.** Children forage some of their own food where it is easy (Hadza: Blurton
   Jones, Hawkes, Crittenden). They see the tool's worth every day in adults' hands.

## 3. Where ACL differs (candidates, not conclusions)

| evidence | ACL now (from LS1) | candidate gap |
|---|---|---|
| Tools pass between people, especially to children | 0.98 of tools used were self-made; 0.013 made by others | A child rarely holds a tool it did not make, so it never feels a tool's worth before it can make one well. |
| Practice continues although it does not pay yet | Making loses to foraging at the choice; practice falls by cohort | The worth of making is learned from one's own, weak early results, so it may never become worth choosing (a low-skill trap). |
| Learning by watching and doing in mixed-age groups | Lessons taken 0.03–0.13; taken lessons later made 0.34 → 0.04 | Lessons give the recipe, not the habit of using it. |
| Skill and worth are judged over years | Habits look a few weeks ahead (discount 0.8 a week) | Delayed pay-offs barely count. |

## 4. What would keep ACL honest

Any change would be a **condition**, never an outcome. Possible kinds, for the creator to weigh, not proposals:
- **Holding before making:** carers able to hand tools to children, as one more giving, not a rule to give.
- **Worth learned over longer times:** fly-brain gaps 1–2 (`docs/fly_brain_review_2026-10-26.md`).

Rules for any design: no named tools or recipes; frozen with the creator; a control; zero checks; the primary test is
whether children who hold tools early later **choose** to make, not population.
