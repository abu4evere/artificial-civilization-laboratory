# Holding a goal across steps: published work bearing on the creator's next choice (2 November 2026)

**Why:** UP1 and WP1 show that ACL's people cannot rebuild their own late inventions. Making the parts wanted (WP1)
does not help. One candidate left is **a goal kept across weeks**. This note gathers what bears on it before the
creator decides. **A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

**Labels:**
- **[R]:** read here, with the level stated.
- **[G]:** the assistant's background, **not read here**; check before relying on it.

## Sources
1. **[R] Cowan 2001**, "The magical number 4 in short-term memory: a reconsideration of mental storage capacity",
   *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 24:87–114, doi 10.1017/s0140525x01003922.
   - **Read:** the abstract (Europe PMC).
   - **What it says:** across many tasks, "a single, central capacity limit averaging about four chunks". Cowan also
     proposes a capacity limit for the focus of attention.
2. **[G] Sutton, Precup & Singh 1999**, "Between MDPs and semi-MDPs: A framework for temporal abstraction in
   reinforcement learning", *Artificial Intelligence* 112:181–211, doi 10.1016/s0004-3702(99)00052-1.
   - **Read:** the record only (Crossref, no abstract). The local textbook draft (`books_local`) has no chapter on
     options.
   - **[G] Background, not read here:** an "option" is a temporally extended course of action, with a rule for when it
     can start, a policy, and a rule for when it ends. A learner can value options like single actions, which is a
     standard way to give an agent multi-step behaviour without scripting the steps.

## What this suggests for ACL (the assistant's reading; for the creator)
- **A goal kept across weeks could be written as a general condition, without naming any invention:**
  - the person keeps one unfinished make target, chosen from their own values as today;
  - while it is open, its missing inputs draw attention, as `thought.steer` already does for one week;
  - it ends when made, when given up after some weeks, or when replaced.

  This is close to an "option": the person, not the law, chooses the target. Cowan's limit suggests keeping **very few**
  open goals; one or a few would be realistic.
- **Two cautions:**
  1. **It is a new kind of state,** a stored intention, so it is a deeper change than WP1. It needs a careful frozen
     test in a copy, with a sham, and adoption only with the creator.
  2. **Measure it against the creator's own capacities 5 and 7:** planning, and goals formed and revised. It should be
     judged by whether people **choose and revise** goals, not only by whether more inventions are remade.
- **More carrying room** is the other candidate. It needs forager evidence first: how many tools a person carries. Two
  searches today found only a bare record (Shott 1986). That search continues.
