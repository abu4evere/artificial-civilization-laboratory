## 1. Deutsch, *The Beginning of Infinity*
**[B] The core ideas:**
- All knowledge, in genes or in minds, grows by **variation and selection**. In people, the variation is
  **conjecture** and the selection is **criticism** and test.
- Good explanations are "hard to vary": change a detail and they stop explaining. Their mark is **reach**, solving
  problems beyond those they were made for.
- **Fallibilism:** there are no authoritative sources of knowledge (ch. 1, terminology).
- **Lamarckism**, the inheritance of improvements acquired in a lifetime, is "a mistaken evolutionary theory" (ch. 4,
  terminology). He calls it the counterpart of the mistaken view of knowledge as built up from observations.
- **Ch. 7, artificial creativity:** artificial general intelligence has not progressed because "we do not understand
  how creativity works".
- **Ch. 13, choices:** a decision is not just picking among existing options by a fixed formula. Its most important
  element is "the creation of new options".
- **Ch. 15, the evolution of culture:**
  - A culture is a set of shared ideas, memes.
  - **Static** societies hold still by memes that disable their holders' criticism.
  - **Dynamic** societies are dominated by memes that work through the holders' criticism.
- **Ch. 16, the evolution of creativity:**
  - We see others' actions, but not the rules behind them, so we must re-create the rules. That is creativity.
  - Creativity first evolved **to copy memes faithfully**, and was later turned to creating new knowledge.
  - Imitation copies behaviour; human transmission copies the knowledge behind it.

**Meeting the Qur'an:**
- **Against blindly following forefathers.**
  - **[S]** Q 2:170: "Rather, we will follow that which we found our fathers doing." Even though their fathers
    understood nothing" (SI). The verb is from the root of ʿaql.
  - **[S]** Q 43:22–23: "we found our fathers upon a religion, and we are in their footsteps".
  - **[C]** Like Deutsch's static societies, these verses reproach inherited custom held without understanding.
- **Critical, repeated looking.**
  - **[S]** Q 67:3–4: "do you see any breaks? … return [your] vision twice again" (SI); AH: "Look again! Can you see
    any flaw?… Look again! And again!"
  - **[C]** This is close to the attitude of criticism Deutsch and Feynman describe, applied to creation.
- **Born knowing nothing; no inherited learned content.** Q 16:78 (in the IQ1 report) and Deutsch's rejection of
  Lamarckism both point, by different roads, against ACL's law that newborns inherit half their parents' learned
  habit values. **[C]** That gives H1 a second, independent motivation.

**Where they part (stated plainly):**
- **The source of knowledge.** In 2:170 and 5:104, inherited custom is contrasted with **"what Allāh has revealed"**:
  revelation is the alternative to blind custom. Deutsch's fallibilism accepts **no authoritative source at all**,
  revelation included, and he lists static, tradition-bound societies among the failures.
- **The Qur'an urges reasoning and looking** (the ʿaql verbs; 67:3–4), but it does not make criticism the final
  arbiter of revealed truth.
- This is a real difference of epistemology. **It is not something ACL can or should settle.** ACL can take the
  shared part: minds that do not blindly copy, that look again and test.

## 2. Feynman, *The Character of Physical Law*
**[B] The core ideas:**
- Physical laws are "a rhythm and a pattern between the phenomena of nature which is not apparent to the eye, but
  only to the eye of analysis" (lecture 1).
- Laws are "simple things, although complex in their actual actions" (p. 33–34).
- "Nature uses only the longest threads to weave her patterns": a law found in a small place holds across the
  universe.
- **His method** (p. 156): guess a law, compute its consequences, compare with experiment. "If it disagrees with
  experiment it is wrong", however beautiful the guess, however smart or famous its author.

**Meeting the Qur'an:**
- **[S]** Q 54:49. SI: "Indeed, all things We created with predestination"; AH: "We have created all things in due
  measure".
- **[S]** Q 25:2: "created each thing and determined it with [precise] determination" (SI).
- **[S]** Q 55:7: "And the heaven He raised and imposed the balance" (SI).
- **[C]** These speak of a measured, ordered creation, an order Feynman's laws describe. **A caution:** the two
  translations of 54:49 already show the range. *Qadar* is read in classical theology chiefly as **divine decree**
  (SI's "predestination"), not as natural law. Reading such verses as statements of physics is a modern
  interpretation, and a contested one. It is not used here as evidence.

**For ACL:**
- **ACL's mission matches Feynman's picture:** few, simple laws whose actions are complex.
- **His rule is ACL's frozen-study rule.** "It does not make any difference how beautiful your guess is". It applies
  equally to guesses inspired by scripture, as the creator required: inspiration, not proof.
- **A criterion for judging laws ([C]):** prefer laws that are simple and have **reach** (Deutsch). A law earns its
  place if it explains phenomena beyond the one it was written for.

## 3. Capra & Luisi, *The Systems View of Life*
**[B] The core ideas:**
- **Life is autopoiesis,** self-making: a network that keeps producing its own parts while keeping its pattern.
- **"Mind is a process!"** (§12.1): "mind and consciousness are not things but processes".
- **The Santiago theory** (§12.2.1):
  - cognition is tied to autopoiesis;
  - a living system couples to its environment through repeated interactions;
  - "The environment only triggers the structural changes; it does not specify or direct them". Living systems are
    **autonomous**;
  - a structurally coupled system is a learning system.
- **Organisms make their environments** (§7.8, quoting Lewontin): "Organisms do not experience environments. They
  create them."
- **Consciousness** (§12.5) is presented as emerging at higher levels of cognitive complexity. There is a guest essay
  "On the primary nature of consciousness"; **it was not read.**
- **Science and religion** (§13.3, §13.7):
  - They separate **spirituality** (inner growth, connectedness, "awe and wonder") from **religion** (an "organized
    attempt to understand and interpret spiritual experience", at risk of dogma).
  - They hold that tension arises when the two are confused, and from "fundamentalists on both sides". Scientists too
    can forget that their models are approximate.

**Meeting the Qur'an:**
- **[S] + [C]** The root of ʿaql occurs 49 times in the Qur'an, **always as a verb**, never as the noun (IQ1
  report). Capra & Luisi's "mind is a process, not a thing" is the same shape of idea, reached by biology. This
  convergence is the clearest in the three books. It is structural, not a claim that either supports the other.
- **The limit:**
  - Capra & Luisi's naturalistic account of consciousness sits against Q 17:85: "The soul is of the affair of my
    Lord. And you have not been given of knowledge except a little."
  - IQ1's rule holds whichever view is right: ACL cannot establish consciousness or *ruh* from behaviour.
- **On their spirituality/religion distinction:** it is their framework, not an Islamic one. Islam does not usually
  separate "spirituality" from revealed religion in this way. It is recorded as their view, not adopted.

**For ACL:**
- **Autonomy in their sense** (the environment triggers, the structure decides) is close to what ACL's people already
  are: their own state determines how they respond.
- **The weakest point** is that ACL's people do little to **make their own environment**. They leave marks, fires
  and crops, but the world mostly happens to them. That is a direction worth noting, not a proposal.

## Across the three books: what ACL might take
1. **H1 is strengthened.** Q 16:78 ("knowing nothing"), developmental science (core *structure*, not content; the IQ1
   report's addendum) and Deutsch's rejection of Lamarckism all argue against inheriting learned values. Next after
   KL1: H1 as a test in a copy. Adoption is the creator's decision.
2. **A new gap for Bob: making options, not only choosing among them** (Deutsch ch. 13).
   - ACL's people choose by a softmax among options their nature lists. They never create a new kind of option.
   - For autonomy ("choosing their own goals"), that may be the deepest missing piece.
   - **[C]** A general law here would be hard to write without scripting outcomes. It is a question for the council,
     not a proposal.
3. **Transmission as re-creation** (Deutsch ch. 16), set against what KL1 has just found.
   - In ACL, teaching and copying pass recipes **with errors**, and an error can revive a lost kind (KL1, operational
     note 2).
   - That is variation; forgetting what one judges useless is a crude selection.
   - **[H]** A read-only question for later: are the error-made variants better or worse than the kinds they came
     from, and do the better ones spread?
4. **Mind as process, and reach as a test.** Feynman and Deutsch give a criterion for any new law: simple, with
   reach beyond its target. Capra & Luisi give a framing for the mind ACL hopes for: a process coupled to its world,
   not a stored thing. The verb-only ʿaql fits that framing.
5. **What none of the books can give ACL:** the *ruh*, faith, and the source of revealed knowledge. These stay outside
   what a simulation can model or test (IQ1 report, F).

## Honest assessment
- **Most useful:**
  - Deutsch's chapters 4, 13 and 16: anti-Lamarckism, option creation, creativity as re-creation. They bear directly
    on H1, on Bob's autonomy, and on what KL1 found.
  - Feynman's method: it confirms how ACL tests laws.
- **Most striking convergence:** the verb-only ʿaql with "mind is a process".
- **Least usable:** Capra & Luisi's ecology and economics chapters, and the parts of Deutsch on politics and the
  multiverse.
- **The real disagreement,** on authority and revelation (Deutsch), is recorded and not resolved. It is outside ACL's
  method.
- **Uncertainty:** most chapters were read through summaries and selected sections, not in full. A claim about a
  chapter's argument beyond what is quoted here should be checked against the full text first.
