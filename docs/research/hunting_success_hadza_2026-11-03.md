# How often does a hunt succeed? Hadza figures for HR1 (3 November 2026)

**Why:**
- HN1 found ACL's hunts never fail for lack of prey; they fail at the roll (5.4% success).
- Round 54 moved F7 (per-species catch rates, provenance unknown) into HR1, a calibration study of success and meat per
  kill against evidence.
- HR1 needs anchors for success by prey size.

**A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

## Source
**Hawkes, O'Connell & Blurton Jones 2014,** "More lessons from the Hadza about men's work", *Human Nature*, doi
10.1007/s12110-014-9212-5.
- **Read:** the full text (the authors' e-offprint, from the University of Utah site).
- **What it is:** a reply to Wood & Marlowe 2013, setting the two groups' Hadza hunting data side by side (its Table 2,
  "Hunting success rates"). Most figures come from Hawkes et al. 1991 and 2001b, which were not read here.

## The figures (Table 2 and its text)
| what | figure | basis |
|---|---|---|
| large animals (adult weight ≥ 40 kg) taken per hunter-day, year-round | 3.4% (71 in 2,076 hunter-days; 29 hunter-days per carcass) | Hawkes et al. |
| the same, the other team's sample | 2.0% (46 in 2,297; 50 hunter-days per carcass) | Wood & Marlowe 2013 |
| large animals, daytime encounter hunting only | 2.2% per hunter-day (46 hunter-days per carcass); 3.17 kg live weight per hunting-day | Hawkes et al. |
| large animals taken per **encounter** (experiment) | 0 of 19 (< 5.3%) | 28 focal hunter-days |
| small animals (≤ about 5 kg) encountered per hunter-day | 5.9 (164 in 28) | the same experiment (1990, men paid to take small game) |
| small animals taken per **encounter** | 7.3% (12 of 164) | the same |
| small animals taken per hunter-day | 42.8% (12 in 28) | the same; 6.7% in ordinary follows (5 of 75 days) |
| small animals, kg per experimental hunting-day | 0.41 | the same |
| best seasonal rate for large game | about one carcass every two hunter-weeks | late dry season, both samples |

**Also in the text:** women collected tubers at roughly 2 kg an hour.

## What it suggests for HR1 (the assistant's reading)
1. **Per encounter, success is low for every prey size:** 7.3% for small animals and under 5.3% for large ones. A
   rabbit catch rate of 0.40 per attempt (ACL's unused table) is not supported as a per-encounter figure.
2. **Per day, small game is caught far more often than large game,** because small animals are met several times a
   day. It is not because each pursuit succeeds more often.
3. **ACL's hunt is one action a week at a tile where prey stands** (HN1: never without prey). Its natural comparison is
   per hunter-**week**, not per encounter.
   - At the Hadza's 3.4% a day, a week of large-game hunting succeeds about 21% of the time (1 − 0.966^7). This is
     the assistant's arithmetic; it assumes days are independent.
   - ACL's 5.4% per hunt is well below that.
4. **Meat per kill is the other half.** Hadza large game averaged 89.8 kg delivered per animal in one camp (Wood &
   Marlowe's data, as quoted in the paper), against ACL's deer 22 and bison 55 food. Before any comparison, HR1 must
   say what one unit of ACL food is in kilograms or kilocalories.
5. **The Hadza case is men's big-game focus,** which the paper argues is not the most provisioning strategy. HR1's
   anchor should be the return ratio (hunting against foraging per unit of time), with success per hunter-week as a
   second check. It should not be any single society's prey choice.
