# Carrying and toolkits: evidence for the "more carrying room" option (2 November 2026)

**Why:** after UP1 and WP1, one option left for the creator is more carrying room. ACL people carry at most 3 made
things (`item_limit`). This note gathers what bears on it. **A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

**Labels:** what was read is stated for each source.

## Sources
1. **Kuhn 1994**, "A Formal Approach to the Design and Assembly of Mobile Toolkits", *American Antiquity* 59:426–442,
   doi 10.2307/282456.
   - **Read:** the abstract (Crossref record, partly paraphrased by the fetch tool).
   - **What it says:** the design of transported artifacts and toolkits in mobile populations depends on "portability
     and potential utility". By its calculations, transported kits should hold mostly **small finished tools**, and
     larger size is rarely worth it for durability or multiple uses.
2. **Kline & Boyd 2010**, "Population size predicts technological complexity in Oceania", *Proc. R. Soc. B*
   277:2559–2564, doi 10.1098/rspb.2010.0452.
   - **Read:** the abstract (Europe PMC).
   - **What it says:** around European contact, Oceanic islands with small populations had less complicated marine
     foraging technology. Demography plays "an important role in generating cumulative cultural adaptation".
3. **Shott 1986** (*J. Anthropological Research* 42:15–51) and **Collard, Kemery & Banks 2005**: no abstract found today.
   Nothing is claimed from them.

## What this suggests for ACL (the assistant's reading; for the creator)
- **No number was found** for how many made things a forager carries, so the evidence does not yet support "4 or 5"
  over "3".
- **Kuhn's frame is portability: size and weight, not a count.** ACL's things have no weight. They are limited by a
  count of 3, which is a crude stand-in.
- **A general version of option C** would make carrying limited by **how much** the things weigh or take up, from
  their properties, instead of how many there are. That is a larger change than raising 3 to 4, and closer to the
  physics.
- **Kline & Boyd** point the other way, to population and contact as drivers of complex technology. That supports
  KL1's and SC1's view that know-how dies with lone holders in small, separate groups.
- **Overall:** the evidence for option C is thin today. The goal-across-weeks option
  (`docs/research/goals_across_steps_2026-11-02.md`) is the one more directly tied to UP1's mechanism.

## Addendum (7 November 2026): a real count of one person's carried kit
**Chlachula D, Marko A-M, Moník M, Cristiani E, … Novák M (2025).** Tracking the Hunter: A Study of the Personal Gear
of a Gravettian Hunter-Gatherer from Milovice IV. *Journal of Paleolithic Archaeology* 8: 24.
doi 10.1007/s41982-025-00228-z (open access).

**What was read:**
- **Word for word**, through a web-fetch agent: the abstract, introduction, background and the first part of the
  methods.
- **Only as machine summaries** (unverified): the results and discussion.

**Read word for word:**
- **The find.** A cluster of **29 blades and bladelets**, some retouched and some not, found in 2021 at Milovice IV
  (Moravia). It is dated to 30,250–29,550 cal BP.
- **Why it is one person's kit.** The authors judge it a **personal toolkit carried on hunting trips** from the tight
  packing and the aligned pieces. They think it was probably bundled in a case of perishable material.
- **How it was used.** It was maintained regularly and sometimes modified. The use of small and broken pieces points to
  thrifty use when stone was scarce on trips.
- **What it was for.** The abstract names cutting, scraping and drilling, and a substantial share of pieces with
  projectile fractures.

**From summaries only** (treat as unverified):
- 18 pieces are retouched and only 10 are complete.
- Wear traces are on 25 of 29 pieces.
- Most of the stone came from more than 130 km away.
- Small broken parts and spalls were kept and reused. The phrase used is "stored as reserve components".

**Bearing on ACL** (the assistant's reading):
- **One real person's carried kit held about 29 small made pieces.** ACL's people carry **3 made things** of any kind.
  The evidence is a single find, but it is a concrete count, an order of magnitude above 3.
- **The pieces are small,** so this fits Kuhn's frame: carrying is limited by size and weight, not by a count.
  It also fits the curation note (`curation_and_parts_2026-11-07.md`): people keep parts, sometimes as reserves.
- **This bears directly on MP1** (where made parts go). If MP1 finds parts dropped at the carry limit, this is the
  evidence the council would weigh next: a carry limit by size or weight, from the things' own properties, not a
  higher count.
