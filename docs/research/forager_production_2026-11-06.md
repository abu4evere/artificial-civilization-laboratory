# How much do foragers produce against what they eat, by age? (research note, 6 November 2026, evening)

For HR1's open question 4: is ACL's foraging too generous? A forage-week yields 20.77 food (HN1), about 3 weeks of an
adult's need (1 food = one adult-day). **A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

## Sources and what was read
1. **Koster, McElreath et al. (2020).** "The life history of human foraging: cross-cultural and individual
   variation." *Science Advances* 6: eaax9070.
   - **Read: the full main text** (not the 40 MB supplement).
   - About 23,700 hunting records from 1,821 hunters at 40 sites.
2. **Kaplan, Hill, Lancaster & Hurtado (2000).** "A theory of human life history evolution: diet, intelligence, and
   longevity." *Evolutionary Anthropology* 9: 156–185.
   - **Not opened:** the PDF is offered only as a file download.
   - **Figures below are from search-result summaries only.**

## What they say
- **Hunting skill by age (Koster 2020, read):**
  - the average hunter peaks at **about 33 years**, with a flat peak: **89% of peak skill at 18**, and still above 89%
    until 56;
  - an 80-year-old keeps about two-thirds;
  - site peaks run from about 24 (Matsigenka, Wola) to 37 (Aché) and 45 (Valley Bisa);
  - some hunters have half the skill of others in the same community;
  - none of the 40 societies relies only on hunting.
- **Production against consumption (Kaplan 2000, search summary only):**
  - Aché men produced about **5,590 kcal a day**, and Hiwi men about **3,490**, against about 3,400 consumed by adult
    men;
  - prime adult men produce about 1.0–1.6 times what they eat;
  - children consume more than they produce for about the first third of life;
  - foragers get about 60% of their food energy from hunted foods.

## What it suggests for HR1 (not a finding)
1. **ACL's "a forage-week gives 3 weeks of food" is not comparable as it stands.**
   - ACL people take one action a week and do not forage every week.
   - The comparable quantity is **food acquired per adult-week, over all weeks** (any action), against need, by age
     and sex. That is a net production curve, as Kaplan et al. draw it.
   - Only that comparison says whether foraging is "too generous".
2. **The meat share is the starkest gap:** about 60% of forager energy is hunted, and ACL's is near 0 (HN1: one
   hunting week in about 7,650 adult weeks).
3. **A read-only first step for HR1 (proposed, "FP1")**, on v15:
   - **Measures:** food acquired per person-week by age band and sex, by source (forage, hunt, gifts, theft, stash);
     consumption (need) per person-week; net production by age; the share of adult weeks spent on each action.
   - **Reference:** Kaplan et al.'s net production curves and the 60% hunted share. Their exact values must be read
     from the paper before freezing, which needs the creator's OK to download it, or another open copy.
   - **Use:** decide which side is off, foraging, hunting or the action budget, before any calibration is frozen.
