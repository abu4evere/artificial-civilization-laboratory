# Research note: do hunter-gatherer children make their own tools? (6 November 2026)

**Why now:** RF1 found that 1,062 of 1,246 new kinds are invented by people not yet grown (in play). In ACL only the
grown are offered "make again" (`mind.decide`, `if grown`; childhood lasts to age 12). Is that rule in line with what
is known about hunter-gatherer children?

## Source
**Lew-Levy, S., Reckin, R., Lavi, N., Cristóbal-Azkarate, J., & Ellis-Davies, K. (2017). How do hunter-gatherer
children learn subsistence skills? A meta-ethnographic review. *Human Nature*, 28(4), 367–394.** PubMed 28994008;
PMC5662667.
- **Read:** the search record, and the full text **as summarised by a fetch tool** (PMC page, 6 November 2026). The
  assistant did not read the full text line by line. The points below come from that summary and should be checked
  against the paper before any number is used.

## What it says (paraphrased)
- **Method:** a meta-ethnography of 58 publications on how hunter-gatherer children learn subsistence skills.
- **Infancy:** parents make toy versions of tools (fishing lines, baskets, digging sticks, spears, bows and arrows) in
  several societies (Batek, Gidra, Kaytetye, Chabu, Aka, Hadza, San).
- **Making their own tools:** by about age four or five, Batek, San and Kaytetye children make their own tools. For
  example, they build the small bows and arrows they use to hunt birds and lizards.
- **Learning:** in early and middle childhood, children learn in multi-age playgroups, through play, observation and
  participation. By late childhood most are proficient food collectors.
- **Teaching:** complex skills (hunting large game, sophisticated tool manufacture) are taught in adolescence, and
  learning continues through adulthood.

## How it bears on ACL (a suggestion only; a frozen study decides)
- ACL lets children invent in play, but never "make again" until age 12. The evidence suggests children make (simple)
  tools themselves from about 4–5, and complex manufacture comes later.
- **A general law in line with this:** a child may make again what they know, as an adult does. Any limit would come
  from general abilities the world already has (skill, strength, what the recipe needs), not from a named tool or an
  age cut-off chosen for an outcome.
- **Caution:**
  - children's making costs time that, in ACL, could cost food (LH1 and HE1: releasing making raised starvation);
  - a test needs harm guards, especially on child deaths and neglect;
  - the summary must be checked against the paper.
