# Composite tools and the mind that makes them: published work bearing on UP1 (2 November 2026)

**Why:** UP1 found that new inventions in ACL are nested: made from other made things. Nobody can remake them, because
the made parts are not in hand, and people cannot hold a goal across steps (make A, then B, then the whole).
Archaeologists have long tied composite tools to the same mental abilities. **A source suggests; only a frozen study
decides.**

**Labels:** what was read is stated for each source.

## Sources
1. **Wadley, Hodgskiss & Grant 2009**, "Implications for complex cognition from the hafting of tools with compound
   adhesives in the Middle Stone Age, South Africa", *PNAS* 106:9590–9594, doi 10.1073/pnas.0900957106.
   - **Read:** the abstract (Europe PMC).
   - **What it says:** making compound glue for hafting involved deliberate changes in pH, drying near fires, and
     workability. "Some of the steps required for making compound adhesive seem impossible without multitasking and
     abstract thought."
2. **Lombard & Haidle 2012**, "Thinking a Bow-and-arrow Set: Cognitive Implications of Middle Stone Age Bow and
   Stone-tipped Arrow Technology", *Cambridge Archaeological Journal* 22:237–264, doi 10.1017/s095977431200025x.
   - **Read:** the abstract, through the Crossref record's summary.
   - **What it says:** using a bow-and-arrow set as a group of interdependent tools, about 64,000 years ago, expresses
     a new cognitive step. People could conceive of tool sets whose parts depend on each other, and decide more
     flexibly.

## What this suggests for ACL (the assistant's reading; for the council and the creator)
- **The pattern matches UP1.**
  - ACL's people can make a composite thing once, by chance in play.
  - They cannot make it **again on purpose**, because that needs the parts first. That is a chain of steps held toward
    a goal.
  - These authors read the making of composite tools as evidence of exactly that kind of mind: multitasking, and
    planning across interdependent steps.
- **The direction of the inference matters.**
  - In the archaeology, composite tools are taken as **signs** of a planning mind.
  - In ACL, the question is the reverse: **what minimal condition** would let such a mind, and such tools, arise
    without being written in?
  - UP1 shows the missing piece concretely. It is not the invention, which happens; it is **repeating it**, which needs
    the parts gathered in order.
- **Candidates for a frozen nature-change test in a copy** (the creator decides; none names an invention):
  1. **A goal kept across weeks:** an unfinished make target that persists and draws its parts.
  2. **The wanted-part bridge reaching more than one level back.**
  3. **More carrying room,** only if forager evidence supports it.

  The exploratory check now running (`experiments/UP1_uptake_of_new_kinds/explore/check_missing.py`) should show which
  of these the data point to.
