# Forager time and energy budgets, for EG1 (the energy gate) — research notes, 7 November 2026

Why: play mode and council round 75 asked whether ACL's weekly energy rule is a fair model of fatigue. EG1 measures how
often it stands between a starving person and food. These notes are the evidence beside it. A source suggests; only a
frozen study decides.

## Kraft et al. 2021 — **full text read**
Kraft, T. S., Venkataraman, V. V., Wallace, I. J., Crittenden, A. N., Holowka, N. B., Stieglitz, J., Harris, J.,
Raichlen, D. A., Wood, B., Gurven, M. & Pontzer, H. (2021). The energetics of uniquely human subsistence strategies.
*Science* 374, eabf0130.

Read from the author-hosted PDF (gurven.anth.ucsb.edu); 15 pages, text extracted with pypdf.

**What it reports**
- **Time on food.** Hunter-gatherers spend **2.6 to 7.0 hours a day** on subsistence (pooled sexes; 14 forager
  populations). Hadza men and women work about **50 and 40 hours a week**; other hunter-gatherer men and women about
  **33 and 28 hours a week** (their table S2). The other great apes spend 7–8 hours a day.
- **Energy and return.** Humans spend more energy but less time on subsistence than the other great apes, and acquire
  much more energy per hour.
- **Eating on the way.** Hadza men and women eat about **80% and 20% of their daily energy needs while out of camp**,
  which "can rapidly provide energy to sustain further food acquisition".
- **Days with no food.** The share of days in which a person produces no food "can be high" (their fig. S5). They
  conclude that humans are more prone to fatigue and starvation than the other apes when returns fail. Sharing, division
  of labour and help with children are how foragers survive this.
- **Time saved.** The time that higher return rates free up gives "more leisure time for social interaction and social
  learning", which they argue was critical for cumulative culture.

**Bearing on ACL**
- **Food work is daily and steady.** Real foragers spend time on food on most days of the week, not one week in two.
  ACL's grown are active in 36–45% of weeks (FP1), which only matches if a forage week is read as "a week's worth of
  food in a burst".
- **A fair comparison is of time shares, not weeks.** Roughly 28–50 hours a week is 25–45% of waking time (at about 16
  waking hours × 7 days = 112 hours).
- **This matters most for EG1's starving weeks.** A hungry forager eats while foraging, and that funds more effort. In
  ACL, a starving person below the cost is offered only rest; they cannot forage a little and eat on the spot.
- **Failure and sharing are the human answer.** Sharing, a division of labour and help with children are how foragers
  meet the high risk of days without food. In ACL, a lone parent's thin margin (PLAY1) is the same risk, with only gifts
  to meet it.
- **Leisure links to Bob.** The authors tie time saved from food to social learning: the same link as ACL's thin
  margins limiting making and teaching (PLAY1, limit 15).
