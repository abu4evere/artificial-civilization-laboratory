# From individual learning to cumulative culture to richer minds: mechanisms with research support (2 November 2026)

**The creator's question** (2 November 2026): "What mechanisms are supported by research that allow individual
learning to become cumulative culture, and cumulative culture to support increasingly sophisticated cognition—without
scripting the result?"

**Labels:**
- **[R]:** read in this project, with the level stated (abstract, or the section named).
- **[G]:** the assistant's general background. It was **not** read here and should be checked before it is relied
  on.
- **[ACL]:** what ACL's own studies found.

The evidence on these points is mixed; disagreements are marked.

## The short answer
Research points to a **loop of conditions**, not one trick:
1. learners who copy **how**, not only **what**;
2. **teaching**;
3. **choosing whom to learn from**;
4. **enough people, connected in the right way**;
5. know-how held by **many heads or in durable media**, so it survives deaths;
6. **variation** from individual trial and imperfect copying, filtered by results;
7. then the culture that accumulates **trains the minds** that carry it (language, imitation and mind-reading as learned
   tools), which makes the next round of transmission better.

None of these needs the result written in. Each is a **condition** a world can provide.

## A. From individual learning to cumulative culture
1. **Copying the process, not just the product.**
   - **[R]** Tennie et al. 2009 (abstract): chimpanzee traditions stay in a "zone of latent solutions" that
     individuals could reach alone, through mostly product-oriented copying. Human culture "ratchets" because social
     learning is process-oriented.
   - **[ACL]** HA1: handing objects (the product) carried no know-how, 0.1%. Teaching (the process) passes know-how
     on (KL1: 13.5% of gains).
2. **Teaching.**
   - **[R]** Dean et al. 2012 (abstract): only children, not chimpanzees or capuchins, reached the higher stages of a
     cumulative task. Their success went with teaching by verbal instruction, imitation and prosociality.
   - **[R]** Hewlett et al. 2011 (§4a): among forager children, teaching occurs from about 12 months, though less often
     than observation and imitation.
   - **[ACL]** AW1: being taught nearly doubled crafting skill; watching passed little.
3. **Choosing whom to learn from (success, prestige, conformity).**
   - **[R]** Tennie et al. 2009 names conformity and sanctions.
   - **[R]** Derex & Boyd 2016 (abstract): learning from successful individuals cuts diversity within fully connected
     groups.
   - **[G]** Theories of biased transmission (Boyd & Richerson): **not read here.**
   - **[ACL]** E13R tested following a better-fed neighbour.
4. **Population size and structure.**
   - **[R]** Henrich 2004 (abstract): when the pool of interacting learners shrinks, simple skills survive and complex
     ones are lost.
   - **[R]** **Disputed:** Vaesen et al. 2016 (abstract) find such models need implausible conditions, and conflict
     with the record.
   - **[R]** Derex & Boyd 2016: **partly** connected groups keep diverse solutions and reach complex ones that fully
     connected groups never produce.
   - **[R]** The Bookey summary of Henrich's *The Secret of Our Success* ("collective brains"; the Polar Inuit relearned
     lost skills on meeting another group). **This is a summary, not Henrich's text.**
5. **Storage that outlasts individuals.**
   - **[ACL]** KL1: 61% of recipe kinds live in one head, and those die with the person, which is 90% of all loss.
     Marks taught the living (8% of gains) but revived lost know-how only 2 of 99 times.
   - **[R]** Kirby et al. 2008 (abstract): what is passed on is shaped by how learnable it is.
   - Redundancy (many holders) and durable media are both forms of storage.
6. **Variation, then selection by results.**
   - **[R]** Deutsch, *The Beginning of Infinity* (chapter summaries): knowledge grows by conjecture and criticism;
     copying is itself re-creation (ch. 16).
   - **[ACL]** KL1: errors in teaching re-created lost recipe kinds (95 of 99 markable revivals). ACL's own individual
     learning and value-based dropping act as a filter.
   - **[G]** "Guided variation" and the ratchet need copying fidelity high enough that improvements are not lost
     faster than they are made (Lewis & Laland and others). **Not read here.**
7. **People shape their environment, and it scaffolds learning.**
   - **[R]** Capra & Luisi §7.8, quoting Lewontin: organisms "create" their environments.
   - **[ACL]** ACL's people leave marks, fires and crops, but do little else to build their world.

## B. From cumulative culture to more sophisticated minds
1. **Cognitive gadgets.**
   - **[R]** Heyes 2019 (précis abstract): imitation, mind-reading and language are mechanisms built in development
     by general-purpose learning, from social input. They spread "because the holders had more students, not just more
     babies".
   - **Disputed:** Spelke & Kinzler 2007 (abstract) argue for innate core systems (objects, actions, number, space).
   - **[C]** Both can be partly right: innate structure, culturally built tools.
2. **Transmission demands creativity, and creativity then makes new knowledge.**
   - **[R]** Deutsch ch. 16 (summary): we see actions, not the rules behind them, so copying requires re-creating
     the rules. Creativity evolved for faithful copying, then served invention.
3. **Language becomes structured through passing it on, and structured language is a tool for thought.**
   - **[R]** Kirby et al. 2008 (abstract): structure appears "without any intentional design".
   - **[C]** That tool-for-thought step is the assistant's inference.
4. **Culture and genes shape each other.**
   - **[R]** The Bookey summary of Henrich ("What are big brains for?"): brains grew in a world of culture.
     **Summary only.**
5. **Development is graded and social.**
   - **[R]** Q 16:78 with Ibn Kathir: born knowing nothing; faculties "develop gradually".
   - **[R]** Hewlett et al. 2011: mostly parents under 5, then others.
   - **[R]** Salali 2019 and Lew-Levy 2022: a gradient, not an age line (`docs/research/children_ages_making_*`).

## C. Without scripting the result: rules ACL already follows, and a few to add
- **Write conditions, not outcomes:**
  - general learning;
  - transmission channels with real limits and **errors**;
  - population size and structure;
  - lifespans and deaths;
  - storage media that wear away;
  - costs.
  - No named inventions, no target skills, no "intelligence" switch. (ACL's hard rule.)
- **Make the learning tools learnable where possible** (Heyes). ACL now writes teaching, watching and reading as laws.
  A deeper version would give only general capacities (attending, demonstrating, linking signs), and let *how* people
  learn from each other be shaped by culture. **This is a long-term question for the council, not a proposal.**
- **Let people make new options, not only choose among given ones** (Deutsch ch. 13). This is the gap most tied to
  autonomy. Writing it without scripting is hard; it is still open.
- **Judge by test, not by fit** (Feynman's rule; Epstein & Axtell: a rule that grows a pattern shows it *can* arise,
  not that it did). Frozen predictions, controls, blind recomputes.
