# How much of foragers' food comes from hunting? (3 November 2026)

**Why:**
- TR1 found hunting gives about 0.001% of ACL's food, and AF1 found people rarely make anything.
- The older review `experiments/FX1_extracted_foods/evidence.md` found ACL's hunting returns about 4% of foraging, where
  the Aché get about the same from each.
- The question for the council (round 53) is whether to calibrate hunting to evidence.

**A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

## Source
**Cordain, Miller, Eaton, Mann, Holt & Speth 2000,** "Plant-animal subsistence ratios and macronutrient energy
estimations in worldwide hunter-gatherer diets", *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* 71(3):682–692, doi
10.1093/ajcn/71.3.682, PMID 10702160.
- **Read:** the abstract only (Europe PMC record).
- **What it says:** "whenever and wherever it was ecologically possible, hunter-gatherers consumed high amounts (45-65%
  of energy) of animal food." Also: "Most (73%) of worldwide hunter-gatherer societies derived >50% of subsistence from
  animal foods, whereas only 14% derived >50% from gathered plant foods."
- **A caveat, from the assistant's background knowledge and not read here:** the analysis rests on the Ethnographic
  Atlas, its "animal foods" include fishing, and its figures have been debated. Warm-climate foragers are often more
  plant-reliant.

## Together with the sources already in the older review (read there; see its labels)
- **Kaplan et al. 2000,** via Kaplan, Gurven & Winking 2009: about 60% of calories hunted.
- **Hill et al. 1987** (Aché): men's foraging, mostly hunting, returned 1,339 calories an hour; women's, mostly
  gathering, 1,221.

## What it suggests (the assistant's reading)
- **Animal food is a large share of real forager diets.** In ACL it is almost none.
- **The council should weigh the anchor's choice:**
  - the **share of the diet** depends on climate and ecology;
  - the **ratio of hunting to foraging returns per unit of time** (Aché about 1.1) is a narrower, more mechanical
    target.
