# A small brain that learns a model of its world and practises in its own "dream" (4 November 2026; the brain road)

**Why:**
- The brain design's first part is a predictor (a world model).
- Round 56's data problem is that a person makes about 2,000 weekly choices in a life.

**A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

## Source
**Ha & Schmidhuber 2018,** "Recurrent World Models Facilitate Policy Evolution", NeurIPS 2018 (oral), arXiv 1809.01999.
- **Read:** the abstract only (the arXiv page).
- **What it says:**
  - **The model:** a generative recurrent network learns, without rewards, a compressed model of the environment over
    space and time.
  - **The controller:** compact, simple policies are trained **by evolution** on the model's features.
  - **The "dream":** the agent was also trained **entirely inside an environment generated by its own world model**,
    and the policy then transferred back to the real environment.

## What it suggests for ACL (the assistant's reading)
1. **The shape matches the brain design.** A recurrent world model (memory over time) plus a **small** decision part.
   The decision part could be shaped by evolution across generations, as the creator wants brains to grow.
2. **An answer to the data problem: practice in imagination.** A person could rehearse options in their own world
   model ("dreaming") between real weeks, which multiplies learning from few real choices. In ACL this would be a
   general ability (imagining), never a named skill.
3. **A caution:** if the world model is wrong, practice in the dream learns the model's mistakes. The source's own
   point is that the policy has to transfer back to the real environment. Tests in ACL would have to check exactly
   that.
