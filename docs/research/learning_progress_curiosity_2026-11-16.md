# Learning-progress curiosity (research note, 16 November 2026)

**Why:**
- Mind Lab's silent-room line (WQ, WI, WG2) found that the brains never fall silent but never invent anything either.
- Dreams improved inside themselves but never reached waking life, and a dream "question" was not pursued above chance.
- W3's curiosity is staleness (re-check what has not been seen lately), so in a bare room it only makes the brain
  patrol.
- The other kind of curiosity is wanting to learn: seeking what one is getting better at.

**What was read:** search results and the abstract-level summaries of the paper below (the IEEE record and archive
pages). Nothing from the full text.

## Oudeyer, P.-Y., Kaplan, F., & Hafner, V. V. (2007). Intrinsic motivation systems for autonomous mental development. *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation* 11(2), 265–286.
- **"Intelligent Adaptive Curiosity":** an intrinsic drive that pushes a robot toward situations where its learning
  progress is greatest, the decrease in its own prediction error.
- **It favours situations neither too predictable nor too unpredictable.**
- In their experiments (one a physical robot on a baby play mat with objects it can learn to manipulate), the
  robot's activities grow more complex by themselves, and stage-like development self-organises without
  supervision.

## Bearing on Mind Lab (a source suggests; only a frozen study decides)
- **In a truly silent room, learning progress goes to zero once the room is learned.** So a learning-progress brain
  should answer silence with silence, unlike W3's staleness curiosity (WQ: restless patrolling).
  - That is a sharp, testable contrast.
- **The paper's robot had a play mat: things whose response to its actions could be learned.** So the fair test of
  "will it invent activity?" is a room with one learnable toy, where its actions change something it can predict
  better with practice.
  - The question then is whether a learning-progress brain plays with the toy until mastered, then loses interest
    (stage-like), where a staleness brain keeps patrolling.
