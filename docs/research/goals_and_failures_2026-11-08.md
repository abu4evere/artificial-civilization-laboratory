# Keeping a goal, and learning from a failed one: background for the brain session (8 November 2026)

**Why:** VW1, PM1 and MP1 suggest that ACL's remaining wall is in the mind. People do not keep a goal across weeks,
and they rarely make anything (`experiments/VW1_value_of_wholes/findings.md`). The creator's aim for the brain work: "a
mind capable of discovering how to succeed … The important ability isn't never making mistakes. It's learning from
them."

**This is background reading for the session with the creator, not a design.** Nothing here is decided. A source
suggests; only a frozen study decides.

## Schut, Wooldridge & Parsons 2004 — **full text read** (extracted from the PDF locally)
Schut M, Wooldridge M, Parsons S (2004). The theory and practice of intention reconsideration. *J. Exp. Theor. Artif.
Intell.* 16(4).

**The problem:** an agent holds intentions, "states of affairs that the agent has committed to bring about". If a plan
fails, it "will typically replan". But the agent must also sometimes reconsider the intention itself, for example when
it "is doomed never to be realized" (abstract).

**The earlier result** (Kinny & Georgeff 1991, as reported in this paper), on two kinds of agent:
- "bold agents, which never pause to reconsider their intentions before their current plan is fully executed";
- "cautious agents, which stop to reconsider after the execution of every action".

They found that "a cautious agent outperforms a bold agent in highly dynamic environments". My inference, not their words: in a
slow world, holding on pays.

**This paper's contribution:** reconsideration is treated as a choice made at run time, either by deliberation
scheduling or by a model of partial observation (a POMDP). This "pushes the choice of commitment level from design
time to run time". The authors write that "neither approach dominates the other".

**What it suggests for ACL (not a decision):**
- ACL's people choose afresh every week; nothing in v17 commits them to an aim across weeks.
- The literature's middle ground is **an intention that persists, with a reconsideration policy**. That can be the
  person's own rule, learnable, reconsidering when the world changes or when progress stalls.
- **ACL's own record:**
  - CI1's IN arm, "a held aim that keeps its pieces", was tested on v14: no harm, no effect, +0.0033 [−0.0004,
    +0.0068];
  - WP1's reading named "a goal kept across weeks" as the missing piece.

  So a held aim alone was not enough. **The open question for the session:** was IN too narrow in what it kept, or does
  an aim need a way to reconsider and replan when a step fails?

## Schank & Leake 1989 — **not read** (scanned PDF without a text layer); record and search summaries only
Schank RC, Leake DB (1989). Creativity and learning in a case-based explainer. *Artificial Intelligence* (a search result's title reads "ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE 353"; volume and pages not checked).

From search summaries only (not checked against the paper):
- in Schank's dynamic memory, learning follows an **expectation failure**;
- the failure prompts an explanation, which can be stored and later adapted.

**For ACL:** people already feel surprise when the world does not match what they expected (`inquiry.surprised`, used
when sparks or embers do something unexpected). Whether a surprise changes what they *try next*, and not only what they
believe, is a question for the brain session. Reading the paper itself would need OCR, or another copy.
