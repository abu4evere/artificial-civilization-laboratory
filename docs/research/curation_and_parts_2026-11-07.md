# Keeping tools and parts ready ("curation"), for the next wall (7 November 2026)

**Why:** LW1 showed that knowing is not making. No one but the inventor makes a new kind, and a side look in play mode
suggests that deep kinds stall on **made parts not in hand** (`experiments/PLAY1_inside_acl/journal.md`; not data; RF1:
pursuit fails, pieces lost or used). What do people do with parts and tools between uses? A source suggests; only a
frozen study decides.

## Hardy & Sillitoe, Internet Archaeology 14, section 4.9 "Storage and discard"
**What was read:** the whole section, through a web-fetch agent's report (https://intarch.ac.uk/journal/issue14/3/4.9.html,
last updated 2003). The full title, the year and the other sections were not opened.

**Who:** the Wola, highland New Guinea, with flaked chert tools. They are **horticulturalists, not foragers**: they are an
anchor for keeping things, not for a forager economy.

**What it reports:**
- **Kept for later.** Unused flakes and cores were sometimes kept for years. Small flakes rode in a man's string bag
  (earlier, rolled in bark cloth). Large flakes and cores were put away in remembered places: a house corner, under the
  eaves, at the foot of trees, just under the ground. Used flakes were often kept as material to knap again.
- **Caching was regular**, of nodules and flakes, used and unused.
- **Throwing tools away carelessly was seen as irresponsible:** people could step on them, and the owner might want the
  chert again.
- **In 117 artefacts handed to the authors,** none had clearly been thrown away for good. Most were found where they had
  been stored, under or beside houses, or at the foot of trees.
- **Brody (1981), British Columbia,** as cited there: the clutter in front of houses that looks discarded is really a
  small store of spare parts, kept nearby to be found, changed and used again.
- **Binford's (1977, 1979) distinction,** as summarised there:
  - **curated tools** are made before they are needed, used for many tasks, carried and recycled;
  - **expedient tools** are made, used and thrown away at once;
  - how much a group curates is one measure of its **"planning depth"** (Binford 1989).
- **The Wola fit neither type cleanly.** They made simple tools from local stone, but stored, cached and hafted.

## Seen only as search-result snippets (not read)
- Binford 1979, "Organization and formation processes: looking at curated technologies": curation as tools made in
  anticipation, carried, maintained and recycled. Toolkits prepared in advance suit mobile foragers who cannot predict
  need.
- Kuhn 1994, "A formal approach to the design and assembly of mobile toolkits" (American Antiquity): record only.

## Bearing on ACL
- **Keeping parts is a general ability, not an invention.** People everywhere keep tools, spare parts and raw material
  for later, in a known place or on the body. ACL's question is whether its people can **hold a made part for a whole
  they want, and find it again**. RF1 found that the pieces are lost or used up. Play mode found deep kinds stalled on
  parts not in hand.
- **It fits the evidence's own words for the goal:** curation as **planning depth**. That is Bob's autonomy, read in
  things.
- **For the council:** a read-only audit first.
  - Where do the made parts of deep kinds go? Do they wear out, get used in something else, get set down and lost, or
    get given away?
  - And does ACL's `ground_items` let a person find their own set-down part again?
  - Only then a law, if any: for example, keeping a part one values for a wanted whole, with no named thing and no
    recipe.
