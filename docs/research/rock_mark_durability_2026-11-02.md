# How long do marks scratched into rock last? An anchor for F6 (2 November 2026)

**Why:** the rules review found F6 (`docs/rules_review_2026-11-02.md`). ACL's mark depth mixes a 0–1 scale with a
0–100 scale, and the docstring's promise that "rock marks last generations" holds only through that fault. At the
intended scale, a stone-scratched mark on rock lasts about 17 years. A fix should anchor rock wear to evidence.
**A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.**

## What was found (labels: what was actually read)
- **Pillans & Fifield 2013,** "Erosion rates and weathering history of rock surfaces associated with Aboriginal rock
  art engravings (petroglyphs) on Burrup Peninsula, Western Australia, from cosmogenic nuclide measurements",
  *Quaternary Science Reviews* 69:98–106, doi 10.1016/j.quascirev.2013.03.001.
  - **Read:** the Crossref record only, with no abstract. ScienceDirect refused access.
- **Watchman, Taçon & Aubert 2014,** the correspondence on that paper, *Quaternary Science Reviews*, doi
  10.1016/j.quascirev.2013.10.004.
  - **Read:** the Crossref record only.
- **Figures from a web search summary, not read in any source; to be verified before use:**
  - rock surfaces retreat at about **0.05–2 mm per thousand years on granite** and 1–10 mm on metamorphic rock exposed
    to rain;
  - engravings "could have survived for 30 ka" on durable rock in the best conditions;
  - open-air Pleistocene engravings survive mainly on the most resistant rocks, such as quartzite.

## What it suggests (the assistant's reading, pending a source read in full)
- **The scale:** a scratch a fraction of a millimetre deep, on rock that loses about 1 mm per thousand years, would
  last **centuries**, not 17 years.
- **ACL's rock wear rate looks far too fast for physics:** depth × 0.998 a week halves a mark in about 7 years. If an
  anchored rate is adopted, marks on rock would last far longer.
- **The consequence:** knowledge kept in marks could then outlive its makers (KL1: marks revived lost know-how 2 times
  in 99). That is a direction toward Bob's cumulative mind, reached by honest physics rather than by a script.
- **Before FX2 uses any number:**
  - read a source in full: a weathering-rate paper on open access, or a rock-art conservation review;
  - convert the rate to ACL's depth units, with the stated assumption that depth 1.0 ≈ 1 mm.
