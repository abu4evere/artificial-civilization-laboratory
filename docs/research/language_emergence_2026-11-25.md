| Source (read) | What it says | For ACL |
|---|---|---|
| Skyrms 2010, *Signals: Evolution, Learning, and Information* (Oxford; publisher and search summaries) | in Lewis's signalling games, a working code (a "signalling system") arises with no prior agreement, by simple evolutionary or reinforcement-learning dynamics; meaning appears as the players settle on one of many possible codes | stage 1 (alarm-like calls) needs no clever brain: plain learning from consequences, if talking pays |
| Lazaridou, Peysakhovich, Baroni 2017 (ICLR; arXiv 1612.07182; abstract) | two networks invent a code in a referential game "out of the need to communicate" | the same, with neural brains |
| Kirby, Cornish, Smith 2008, "Cumulative cultural evolution in the laboratory", *PNAS* 105(31): 10681-10686 (abstract) | artificial languages passed from learner to learner "evolve in such a way as to maximize their own transmissibility": over generations they become easier to learn and more structured | stage 3 (combinations, the first grammar) needs TRANSMISSION across generations, ACL's deep time, and learners who learn from each other |
| Lowe, Foerster, Boureau, Pineau, Dauphin 2019, "On the Pitfalls of Measuring Emergent Communication" (arXiv 1903.05168; abstract) | reward going up with a channel is not proof of communication: messages can correlate with the speaker's actions without changing the listener's | ACL must test that a signal CHANGES what the listener does (e.g. block the signal and the listener's behaviour should change) |

## What follows for ACL's goal (proposals)
1. Stage 1 is the cheapest: a meaning-free signal action plus a situation where telling pays (a predator, food far
   away); the measure is not survival alone but whether the listener's actions depend on the signal (Lowe et al.).
2. Stage 3 needs generations of learners copying signals from each other (Kirby et al.): the brain road's "learning from
   others" work (W3d-W3f, W3q) is part of the language road too.
3. Decoding (the mind-evidence line): match each signal to the speaker's situation and inner state, then test it by
   blocking or swapping signals.
