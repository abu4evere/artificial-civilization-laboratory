# Sources behind the Mind Lab tests of 13–15 November (checked 15 November 2026, sleep mode)

On 14–15 November the assistant cited several works from memory while explaining the Mind Lab results (WN to WF).
Each was looked up on the night of 15 November.
- **What was read:** for every entry, **record or abstract only** (publisher, PubMed or Semantic Scholar pages and
  search summaries). No full text was read.
- A source suggests; only a frozen study decides.

## Verified as cited
1. **Elman, J. L. (1993).** Learning and development in neural networks: the importance of starting small.
   *Cognition* 48, 71–99. doi:10.1016/0010-0277(93)90058-4. *Record and abstract.*
   - Recurrent networks learned complex sentences when the input or capacity started limited; a fully "adult" network
     failed.
   - Cited for WG (growing) and WL (leaky memory).
2. **Newport, E. L. (1990).** Maturational constraints on language learning. *Cognitive Science* 14, 11–28.
   *Record and abstract.*
   - The second hypothesis there is the "less is more" idea: children's cognitive limits may help language learning.
   - Modelled in Goldowsky and Newport (1990).
   - Cited for the creator's leaky-memory idea.
3. **McClelland, J. L., McNaughton, B. L., & O'Reilly, R. C. (1995).** Why there are complementary learning systems in
   the hippocampus and neocortex. *Psychological Review* 102, 419–457. *Record and abstract.*
   - Fast hippocampal learning, then reinstatement interleaved into slow neocortical learning.
   - **The idea behind WF's nightly replay.**
4. **Wilson, M. A., & McNaughton, B. L. (1994).** Reactivation of hippocampal ensemble memories during sleep.
   *Science* 265, 676–679. *Record only.*
   - Replay of waking activity during sleep. Supports WF's "replay at night" reading.
5. **Oh, J., Guo, Y., Singh, S., & Lee, H. (2018).** Self-Imitation Learning. *ICML*, PMLR 80, 3878–3887;
   arXiv:1806.05635. *Abstract.*
   - Imitating the agent's own past good trajectories helps exploration.
   - WY's "that worked!" is a cousin of this (WY weights by surprise; SIL uses returns above a value estimate).
6. **Rogers, A. R. (1988).** Does biology constrain culture? *American Anthropologist* 90, 819–831. *Record and
   abstract.*
   - "Rogers's paradox": at equilibrium, social learning need not raise the population's fitness.
   - Cited for WE and WT (copiers).
7. **Lyons, D. E., Young, A. G., & Keil, F. C. (2007).** The hidden structure of overimitation. *PNAS* 104,
   19751–19756. *Abstract.*
   - Children copy an adult's unnecessary actions on a new object.
   - Cited when the creator said humans cannot be parrots ("humans parrot in smaller ways").
8. **Maguire, E. A., et al. (2000).** Navigation-related structural change in the hippocampi of taxi drivers. *PNAS*
   97, 4398–4403. *Abstract.*
   - Posterior hippocampus larger in London taxi drivers, growing with years of driving.
9. **Cowan, N. (2001).** The magical number 4 in short-term memory. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 24, 87–114.
   *Record.* Cited for "about 4 things at once".
10. **Koenig, M. A., & Harris, P. L. (2005).** Preschoolers mistrust ignorant and inaccurate speakers. *Child
    Development*. *Abstract.*
    - N = 119; selective trust was significant at age 4, not at age 3.
    - **Correction to the assistant's wording on 14 November** ("around ages 3 to 5"): in this study the effect was
      clear at 4. Later work places early signs even younger; that work was not checked.
11. **Kagan, B. J., et al. (2022).** In vitro neurons learn and exhibit sentience when embodied in a simulated
    game-world. *Neuron.* *Record and summaries.*
    - About 800,000 neurons; learning within about five minutes of play.
    - "Sentience" there means responding to the environment, not feeling.
    - Cited when the creator asked about biological neurons.

## New and relevant (found while checking)
12. **Rohde, D. L., & Plaut, D. C. (1999).** Language acquisition in the absence of explicit negative evidence: how
    important is starting small? *Cognition* 72, 67–109. *Abstract.*
    - **A counter-result to Elman:** in their simulations, starting with limited memory or simplified input was not
      needed, and it hindered learning once the language was made more natural.
    - **For Mind Lab:** WL and WL2's gain from forgetting may depend on the task (here, the same boxes shown again and
      again). It should be tested in a richer task before it becomes a design rule.
13. **Enquist, M., Eriksson, K., & Ghirlanda, S. (2007).** Critical social learning: a solution to Rogers's paradox of
    nonadaptive culture. *American Anthropologist* 109, 727–734. *Abstract.*
    - "Critical social learning" (copy, and learn individually if the copy fails) beats pure social learning and
      usually pure individual learning.
    - It is stable unless transmission is unfaithful, the environment is highly variable, or social learning is
      costly.
    - **For Mind Lab: this is close to the brain recipe that emerged** (learn from the teacher, act from one's own
      knowledge, search and keep what works when the copy fails: WL2, TB, WY, WF). A frozen test could compare it
      directly with the pure strategies, in groups (WP's setting).
