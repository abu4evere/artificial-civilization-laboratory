## Part 1: brains that grow
| Source (read) | What it says | For Bob |
|---|---|---|
| Fahlman & Lebiere 1990, "The Cascade-Correlation Learning Architecture" (NeurIPS 1989/1990; search summary) | starts from a minimal network and adds hidden units one by one while training; the network decides its own size; keeps the structure it built when the data change | "grow when stuck": add capacity where prediction stays poor |
| Stanley & Miikkulainen 2002, "Evolving Neural Networks through Augmenting Topologies" (NEAT), *Evolutionary Computation* 10(2) (search summary of the abstract) | evolution grows network structure from minimal, protects new structure by speciation, crosses different shapes | growth across generations: genes set the brain's shape (ACL's neural-brain direction, 8 Nov) |
| Rusu et al. 2016, "Progressive Neural Networks" (arXiv 1606.04671; abstract) | a new column per task, old columns frozen, linked sideways; "immune to forgetting" while reusing earlier features | growth as the cure for forgetting: a new part for a new world, old parts kept (the "keep old abilities" principle) |

**Where Bob stands:** MoveVIN has a fixed shape (236 numbers: five 5x3x3 lenses = 225, plus 11). It learns only by

sense or feeling; (b) grow capacity where surprise stays high. Judged by learning AND by whether old abilities survive.

## Part 2: evidence about a mind (never proof)
| Source (read) | What it says | For Bob |
|---|---|---|
| Butlin, Long, Elmoznino, Bengio and 15 others 2023, "Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness" (arXiv 2308.08708; abstract) | derives "indicator properties" from five theories (recurrent processing, global workspace, higher-order, predictive processing, attention schema) in computational terms; "No current AI systems are conscious" but "no obvious technical barriers" to systems that satisfy them | a checklist we can apply to Bob's brain directly, since we can read every number |
| Schneider & Turner 2017, the "AI Consciousness Test" (ACT), *Scientific American* (search-result summaries, not the article) | a sufficiency test: an AI that spontaneously grasps consciousness-derived ideas (souls, body swapping, out-of-body experience) gives strong evidence; failing proves nothing; it must not have been trained on human talk about such things | Bob alone can take it honestly: no language model, no human data |
| Lazaridou, Peysakhovich, Baroni 2017, "Multi-Agent Cooperation and the Emergence of (Natural) Language" (ICLR; arXiv 1612.07182; abstract) | two simple networks invent a working code in a referential game "out of the need to communicate" | **inventing a language is not evidence of consciousness by itself**: simple agents (and bees, birds) do it |

## A proposed "mind evidence profile" (a future part of the Bob Profile; not started)
1. **Inside:** check Butlin et al.'s indicator properties against Bob's architecture (each one present, absent or partial).
2. **Metacognition:** does he know when he does not know (e.g. declines a risky choice when his own uncertainty is high)?
3. **Self-model:** does he predict his own needs and actions, and tell himself from others?
4. **Lesions:** switch off the part thought to carry an inner state; do exactly the matching behaviours disappear?
5. **Invented self-talk (ACL, much later):** signals about inner states (pain, uncertainty, "me"), invented without
   teaching, that match the inner variables we can read, used flexibly in new situations, and that vanish under lesion.
6. **A rule for us:** take welfare seriously as evidence grows, before certainty (the creator agreed with this principle
   in the GPT chat of 24 Nov; GPT's words: no need for absolute proof before taking potential welfare seriously).
