## The Undergraduate Complexity Research (UCR) program

- **What:** 10-week, in-person summer research at SFI (Santa Fe, New Mexico), a project of your own with an SFI mentor.
  The 2026 program ran May 31 – August 8.
- **Paid:** $7,000 stipend, free housing and meals, and travel support.
- **Who can apply:** current undergraduates, **two-year colleges included** (BMCC qualifies), who will still be enrolled
  for at least one more term after the summer. No research experience needed. Priority usually goes to juniors and
  seniors, but anyone may apply. Programming experience is "favorably considered". **Must be authorized to work in the
  US** (international students at US schools usually join through CPT).
- **When:** applications usually open in **early November and close in mid-January**; decisions by email in March. The
  2027 details appear on the page in November 2026.
- **What to send:** a CV or résumé; a **one-page research statement with three research ideas** (clear question,
  plausible approach, why the approach fits); **one recommendation letter** from a professor or mentor; unofficial
  transcripts.
- **Fit:** ACL is exactly the kind of work they describe (adaptive systems, emergence, agent-based models, rigorous
  methods). Its frozen-plan method answers their question "how do you frame a question and approach it?" directly.

Page: https://www.santafe.edu/engage/learn/programs/undergraduate-complexity-research

## Free courses on Complexity Explorer (complexityexplorer.org)

Most useful for ACL, in order:
1. **Introduction to Agent-Based Modeling** (a Summer 2026 run is listed): the standard vocabulary and practice of ABMs.
2. **Agent-Based Models with Python: An Introduction to Mesa**: ACL is plain Python; Mesa is the field's standard Python
   ABM library, worth knowing to describe ACL in terms others use.
3. **Introduction to Complexity** (Melanie Mitchell): the field's basics; free certificate.
4. **Introduction to Open Science**: matches ACL's frozen plans and public records.
5. **Game Theory I/II**: for food sharing and cooperation.
6. **Introduction to Information Theory**: for measuring what knowledge is passed on.

## Reading from SFI's list that touches ACL directly

- P. W. Anderson, *More is Different* (Science, 1972): emergence; the case for studying wholes.
- R. Axelrod and W. D. Hamilton, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (Science, 1981): simulation of cooperation among selfish
  agents (relevant to ACL's gifts and food sharing).
- H. A. Simon, *The Architecture of Complexity* (1962): hierarchy and modular building (relevant to composites built
  from parts, CA3 and LM1).
- M. Newman, *Networks* (textbook): partner finding and band contact are network problems (MB1b, PF1).
- M. Mitchell, *Complexity: A Guided Tour* (book, readable).

## Ideas worth taking into ACL (not decisions; for the creator)

- Describe ACL with the **ODD protocol** (Overview, Design concepts, Details), the standard way ABMs are written up;
  it would help a paper and a UCR application. (ODD is from Grimm et al., not from these SFI pages.)
- Treat partner finding as a **network** question (who can reach whom), which PF1's results may call for.
