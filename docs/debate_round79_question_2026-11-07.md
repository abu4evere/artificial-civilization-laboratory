## The question (sent as one paragraph)
Council round 79 (ACL, 7 November 2026). VW1 (law A, value flows down the recipe tree) is frozen, its zero checks pass, and it is running on the cloud now; this round is about law B, which you queued next in round 78. The code facts (read today): at birth a child's caregiver is set to the mother (biology._born); each week infants and children are moved to their caregiver's tile (world.py), so carer and child are always together; in the body phase each person feeds their dependents (living children or wards whose caregiver is them) before eating: if they hold enough for themselves and the dependents, the dependents get all they lack, else a share set by the carer's care gene (dep_weight = total x (0.4 + 1.2 x care)); the caregiver passes to someone else only at the carer's death (assign_caregiver: the other parent, grown siblings, grandparents, then a kind stranger nearby). A father can feed his children only by the give action, which needs a surplus above twice his own need plus his own dependents' need; DF1 found fathers' gifts about 1/20 of what mothers feed, and women carry 0.888 of feeding-caused short weeks. Anchors (a source suggests, a study decides): Hooper, Gurven, Winking and Kaplan 2015 (Tsimane', forager-horticulturalists, full text read): fathers' net food to their children averages 1,107 calories a day against mothers' 177 (breastfeeding not counted); the analysis assumes food is pooled within the nuclear family; between families food flows to the needier household along kinship. Wood and Marlowe 2013 (Hadza, abstract only): married men with children at home bring food to camp on 42% of days, single men 28%; Hawkes, O'Connell and Blurton Jones 2014 (abstract only) dispute that men's hunting provisions their own households. Candidate forms: (B1) second feeder: after the caregiver has fed, a dependent still short is fed by its living father if he is on the same tile, by the same rule and care gene; (B2) shared family pool: both living parents on the child's tile feed the child together, each in proportion to what they hold beyond their own need; (B3) DeepSeek's broader form: any present grown kin carer with food. Questions: (a) which form is the minimal general law, and must the father be on the same tile (no food across distance)? (b) should the father's share use his own care gene, as the mother's does? (c) primary outcome and guards: is the women minus men gap in feeding-caused short weeks the right co-primary, or is that tuning toward an outcome; what should the second co-primary be (child survival to 16, or something tied to Bob such as making by women 30-49)? keep CI1's eight guards plus men's starvation? (d) order: test B now on v16 in parallel with VW1, or wait for VW1's result and test on whatever is live? (e) your estimate (0-100%) that the test is valid and informative. Please answer briefly, point by point.

## Follow-up (sent as one paragraph, the same text to all three)
Council round 79, follow-up (the same text to all three). New fact from a side look (not data; two v16 worlds, exploration seeds 26901-26902, 130 years, years 100-130 counted, read-only): of 95,714 child-weeks with the mother as carer, 89,203 have a living father (all of them the mother's partner); at the body phase he is on the child's tile in 0.053, one tile away in 0.082, and two or more tiles away in 0.865. When he is on the tile he nearly always holds food beyond his own and his dependents' needs (0.98), but the case where he is on the tile and the mother is short for the child happens in 100 of 95,714 mother-cared child-weeks (0.0010). So B in any same-tile form (B1, B2 or B3 for fathers) would act almost never: couples end the week apart. ACL already has a law for that, written and tested long ago and off in v16: MC1 'moving camp' (moving_camp = 1: partners within reach of each other end each week together on the better of their two tiles; no fixed home, no random draw). MC1 and MC1-L (256 seeds, an older nature) were judged only on extinction: couples apart 56.9% -> 16.4%, births +15%, no extra hunger, extinction no effect, but the extra children died more (0.247 vs 0.200 per birth), unexplained. The three answers to round 79: GPT chose B1 (smallest change, keeps v16 exactly where the father is absent; its own caveat: it keeps the mother-first asymmetry); DeepSeek chose B2 ('B1 writes a role priority, culture, into nature'); Gemini chose B3 ('universal kin selection', no nuclear family). GPT and DeepSeek: the women-men gap is not a co-primary (it could reward equalising hardship); Gemini: it is valid if only observed. Order: GPT and DeepSeek test in parallel on v16; Gemini says wait, because parallel tests would be confounded (my check: B against v16 is one change and not confounded; only B together with VW1 is untested, which GPT and DeepSeek cover by testing on the live or combined code before adoption). Also checked: the give action already needs the giver within 1 tile (DeepSeek was right). Questions: (1) Given co-location 0.053, should law B be tested only together with MC1, e.g. a factorial v16 / MC / B / MC+B / sham, so that MC's own effect (and its extra child deaths) is measured beside B's? Or is the real law 'partners share a place', with B second? (2) Reply to the others' arguments: keep or change your form (B1, B2, B3)? A form must equal v16 exactly when no second parent is on the tile (DeepSeek: as worded, B2's 'what they hold beyond their own need' would change the mother's own v16 rule, which feeds even below her own need by the care gene: please state B2 so it reduces to v16). (3) Primary outcome: child-weeks with unmet need and child survival to 16, with the gap descriptive? (4) Your estimate (0-100%) for your design. Please answer briefly.
