# Evolving minds: can ACL's agents develop their minds like humans? (GPT's and Gemini's opinions, not sign-off)

**The creator's question (27 October 2026):** can our agents develop their minds, the way humans did over millions of
years? The proposal discussed: make parts of how people learn heritable, like temperament, and let natural selection
decide. The same text went to GPT and Gemini; Claude was council.

**The facts given:**
- Today a child inherits 8 temperament genes: the parents' average plus a random change, sd 0.08.
- The learning machinery is fixed and identical for everyone: learning rate, discount, copying rate, the 12-recipe
  memory, and the choice temperature.

## GPT
- **A good idea, but a separate long-term direction, not the next fix.** "Natural selection does not automatically
  produce intelligence … might favor copying established habits rather than inventing anything."
- **Start with one heritable trait:** relying on own experience against copying others. Keep the rest fixed. **"I
  would not add an invented food cost for memory"** without a defensible model.
- **Generations:** use 1,000-year runs and many worlds. Don't speed up lives or births (that changes nature). Check
  that averaging the parents' genes keeps enough variation.
- **Tuning?** "Not inherently". But choosing ranges, mutation rates or costs "because they produce smarter agents
  would be tuning."
- **Test:**
  1. a read-only audit of whether today's genes change heritably across generations;
  2. a frozen study with three arms: a fixed-learning control, an evolving arm, and a control whose learning styles
     vary as much but are not inherited.
- **Chances:**
  - about 60% of seeing heritable change over 1,000 years;
  - 20% of a reproducible improvement in learning;
  - under 5% of a big step towards Bob from this alone.
- **"Evolution of learning parameters and development of a mind are different things … first establish that its
  existing minds can learn accurately, explore alternatives, and transmit what they discover. Evolution can shape those
  capacities later; it cannot substitute for capacities the architecture does not possess."**

## Gemini
- **"It is a trap right now, though sound in principle."** While copying distorts values, evolution would "simply
  optimize agents to exploit those distortions (e.g. selecting for hyper-copying foragers)". Fix leaks like HC1 first.
- **First trait:** copying weight against own-experience weight (the same as GPT).
- **Memory capacity would need a cost:** it proposed "+0.2 food per week per extra slot".
- **Generations:** don't shorten lives; run 256–512 worlds for 1,000–2,000 years in the cloud.
- **Chances:** 5% at 200 years; 25% at 1,000 years for single-trait shifts; 0% for complex minds.

## Council (Claude)
- **Both agree:**
  - it is sound in principle, but not now;
  - fix the current rules first (HC1);
  - start with one heritable trait: own experience against copying;
  - no speeding up lives;
  - many worlds over 1,000+ years;
  - small chances within our timescale.
- **Checked:** Gemini's "+0.2 food per slot" is an invented number, the kind of choice GPT calls tuning. If a memory
  trait is ever tested, its cost needs a defensible basis, or memory stays fixed. Both models put own experience
  against copying first, which needs no cost.
- **GPT's first step is a cheap read-only one:** check whether today's 8 temperament genes actually change across
  generations, before building anything new.
