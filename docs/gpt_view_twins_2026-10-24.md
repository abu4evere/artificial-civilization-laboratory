# GPT's view: twins in ACL (24 October 2026)

**Source: GPT (ChatGPT, the creator's account, main ACL chat), asked by the assistant at the creator's request ("first
ask from gpt and then we will decide"). GPT's opinion and its reading of sources, in summary; the figures and sources
are not yet checked by the assistant; not sign-off. The creator decides.**

**The question.** ACL has no twins (`give_birth` always makes one child). The creator read that twins happen about
1 in 300–400 and asked whether ACL should allow them with a small chance.

## GPT's answers

- **Rates (per 1,000 deliveries):** identical (monozygotic) about 3–4, fairly constant across populations, causes
  largely unknown (so the creator's 1 in 300–400 is roughly the identical-twin rate); fraternal (dizygotic) roughly
  2–20+, varying with maternal age (rising to the mid-to-late thirties), previous births, heritable factors and maternal
  condition; all twins about 12 globally (2010–15). Natural-fertility example: rural Gambia, 15.9 (50 in 3,136
  births, 1950–74, Sear et al.) and 14.3 in 1989–93 (8.8 to 18.3 between communities). No reliable forager figure
  (Hadza, Agta, Aché, Ju/'hoansi) was found; none should be invented.
- **Costs without modern medicine:** twins die far more often (sub-Saharan surveys, Monden & Smits 2017: 21.3% before
  age five, against 6.35% for single births; much of it in the first month, not all explained by birth weight).
  Mothers' risk is higher (Gambia ~3.3% against 0.8% per delivery, a small sample; 19th-century Estonia 0.75% against
  0.37%). Birth spacing after twins: no good basis for a special rule (Gambian twin mothers had shorter intervals, but
  likely because they were more fertile; Finland found no difference).
- **Twinning is nature** (a body event; needs no knowledge). It must **not**: rise when a people is small or dying; be
  tuned to help survival; give twins special minds (telepathy, shared memories, extra affection, knowing they are
  twins). Twins are ordinary siblings who happen to share a birth week; whether twinning helps or harms should be left
  to the world.
- **Minimal form (a candidate, not a decision):** identical 0.004 and fraternal 0.010 per delivery (about 14 per 1,000,
  close to rural Gambia); or identical only, as a documented partial first step. Identical twins: one temperament draw
  given to both, same sex. Fraternal: two ordinary draws, either sex. One pregnancy, two births in the same week,
  separate people and histories, same generation. One nursing clock for the mother; the 78-week wait applies while
  either twin lives. Feeding and care through the existing rules (no "half the love" rule); no added twin-death
  probabilities just to match human figures (accept that the current body misses prematurity and birth weight, and
  report it).
- **Check before building:** whether the "youngest child alive" test would look at only one twin (if the second-born
  dies first, the mother must not become fertile early while the other twin lives); and whether the order people act
  in favours one twin.
- **Is it worth a study?** Not a survival study (tiny effect). The value is for Bob: two people born with the same
  temperament who live different lives. With ~0.4% identical-twin deliveries, a world of 500 deliveries gives about two
  identical pairs, so the long (millennium) runs could supply them naturally later. A future read-only twin-identity
  study (GPT's "TW-ID1", concept only) would compare identical twins, fraternal twins, ordinary siblings and matched
  strangers over life, asking whether **experience** produces lasting, consequential differences beyond random drift
  (stronger causal tests would use controlled copies of one state with different histories, as ID1 did).
- **GPT's recommended order:** finish BD3 and the current read-only work; then propose a minimal twin-birth nature
  change for the creator's approval, checked for correct births, kinship, nursing and fair care; no twin-survival study.

Sources GPT named (to check): MedlinePlus Genetics (Twinning); Smits & Monden 2011 (twinning across the developing
world); Monden, Pison & Smits 2021 (twin peaks); Sear et al. 2001 (fitness of twin mothers, Gambia); a rural Gambia
study of twin mortality causes; Monden & Smits 2017 (mortality among twins and singletons); a 2024 Estonian study of
twin mothers' survival; Fraga et al. 2005 (epigenetic differences in identical twins).

## The creator's decision (24 October 2026)

"Add twins, but that should be totally random and low chance." So: a fixed, low chance per birth that depends on
nothing (not age, parity, population or anything else), as GPT's simplest form. To be drafted as a Law 126 plan (frozen,
zero checks, the "youngest child alive" check fixed for twins) after BD3, and signed off by the creator before any code.
