# GPT's view: the creator's 1,000-year yardstick, longer runs, and what Bob would look like in data (24 October 2026)

**Source: GPT (ChatGPT, the creator's account, main ACL chat), asked by the assistant at the creator's request ("tell
about these to gpt and talk about these things"). GPT's opinion, in summary; not verified sources and not sign-off.**

What was put to GPT: the creator's yardstick (a world's start against ~1,000 years later must differ, with nothing
handed to the people); Bob as the "final boss" (autonomy, a cumulative mind); the record (E5–E7 millennia declined;
nature has changed a lot since; no millennium on today's nature); timings measured on the creator's PC (~1–2 s per
world-year at ~40 people; a million years ≈ 17 days per world, needing chained save-and-continue); the assistant's
ladder (1,000 → 10,000 → 1,000,000 years).

## GPT's answers

1. **The yardstick:** right as a headline historical test, not as a single pass/fail for cumulative intelligence. The
   question: after 1,000 years, does the population have functional capabilities, enduring knowledge and historically
   contingent practices its founders did not have, or has it only repeated or lost what was there at the start?
   Report three outcomes separately, never one "civilization index": **(A) functional capability** (performance, not
   labels: yield per action given opportunity, tool performance under fixed conditions, success per attempt, breadth of
   distinct useful functions); **(B) retention** (transmitted, retained, rediscovered or lost; how long; whether
   recipients use it; probably the most diagnostic given CA1–CA3); **(C) enduring novelty and social history** (each
   with its own test: a spreading mark is not language, repeated visits are not ritual).
   **Against E7.5-style gaming, a ladder of evidence:** new property combination (candidate) → made repeatedly →
   demonstrably better or different use → adopted or reproduced by others → survives its inventor and later cohorts →
   modified into a further verified improvement (candidate cumulative cultural evolution). Compare a mature early
   window (e.g. years 20–50) with years 950–1,000, keep year 0, and show the whole trajectory.
2. **"Flat is human, decline is the problem": half right.** The Acheulean persisted ~1.5 million years (a 2026 study:
   handaxe skill improved early, little after), but similar-looking tools do not prove one unbroken lineage of
   transmission. Tasmania: Henrich's explanation is disputed (scale of loss and the demographic cause). The decisive
   line is **retention against constant reconstruction from scratch**: a people keeping one useful invention for 900
   years shows a stronger cultural mechanism than one that keeps rediscovering and losing it. Not all loss is
   pathological (people abandon what stops paying). Trajectory classes: improvement with verified transmission;
   stable maintenance; stable metrics by constant rediscovery (individual learning, not culture); change without
   improvement; erosion; extinction.
3. **Founders:** 80 primary (64 worlds), 20 as an optional sensitivity cohort. More people means more independent
   rediscovery even with no teaching, so normalise transmission and persistence to opportunity. Avoid survivor bias:
   report all worlds (unconditional, with extinction) and surviving worlds (conditional), separately. **Check the
   timing at 80 founders before promising ~1 hour** (populations may be much larger).
4. **A million years: not yet.** Impressive more than informative today. ACL has no evolving brain and a bounded set of
   discoverable kinds (E5 found every kind in 11 years): more time explores that set, it does not make it unbounded.
   Three defensible reasons for very long runs: rare transitions with waiting times beyond 10,000 years; long-term
   stability; slow heritable change (absent in ACL). Before a million-year run: a specific hypothesis about the
   timescale, a statistic able to detect it, and checkpoint equivalence and behaviour-preserving speed-ups proven
   first. **Changed ladder:** run 1,000 years; choose the next test from its mechanism (decline → study loss; stable →
   test retention; growth → test how improvements arise, and rule out slow exhaustion of a fixed set); 10,000 only to
   ask a new question; a million only if shorter runs cannot answer a concrete hypothesis. A flat result can itself
   justify a longer run.
5. **Bob in data, three levels:** (1) a history-dependent individual (experience changes later behaviour beyond
   temperament, hunger, place, age; tested with controlled state copies in an offline branch); (2) an adaptive mind (a
   past failure changes strategy; a principle reused in a new situation; new effective combinations), against simpler
   explanations; (3) an individual benefiting from cumulative history (born centuries later, learns something found
   long before, uses it, improves it, others learn the improvement). Do not pick the most impressive agent afterwards
   and fit criteria to it; no rewards for curiosity or invention; no single "Bob score"; a population-wide battery
   (experience-dependent learning, long-term memory, individuality, adaptability, flexible problem solving, cultural
   dependence, cumulative contribution). Most of levels 1–2 can be studied within one lifetime; the millennium matters
   for the history available to later individuals. Do not simply raise memory or teaching rates: first find whether
   knowledge is not met, not valued, not understandable, displaced, or never used.

## GPT's proposed M1 (not frozen)

Nature v6 unmodified; 64 worlds × 80 founders (+ optional 64 × 20); 1,000 years; windows year 0, 20–50, 950–1,000;
outcomes: population (trajectory, extinction, age structure), functional capability, retention (discovery, hand-over,
use, loss), novelty (lasting and useful vs cosmetic), individual development where records allow; the world is the
statistical unit; prior: continued erosion is plausible. Scope line to add: *"The study will distinguish historical
persistence, independent rediscovery, and cumulative improvement, rather than treating temporal change alone as
evidence of increasing intelligence."* Before freezing, confirm which measures can be reconstructed from records.

**GPT's closing challenge:** the final test is whether an individual born into a world's history can do and learn
things an equivalent individual born at its beginning could not, and whether that difference came from the world's own
history rather than from its creator.
